kb/sources/revisiting-core-ontology-requirements-engineering-2008.ingest.md:25:Jureta, Mylopoulos, and Faulkner extend the requirements problem of finding a specification that, with domain assumptions, entails requirements. Starting from stakeholder communication, they distinguish beliefs, desires, intentions, and evaluative attitudes, grounding their definitions in speech-act theory and DOLCE. The resulting ontology separates goals, measurable quality constraints, subjective or ill-defined softgoals, plans, and domain assumptions. A quality constraint can stand in for a softgoal only through a project-local justified approximation supported by sufficient correlation, and new information can defeat that approximation. Optionality and preferences, including preferences over preferences, guide selection among alternatives; defeasible consequence replaces monotonic satisfaction. The result is a conceptual account of what information requirements work should represent and what a desirable specification must account for, not a tested procedure for elicitation, justification, or revision.
