The Commonplace note supplies more concrete information, authority, coordination, and recovery conditions; the paper does not test those conditions or justify transferring goal-setting authority to an executor. Its open question about whether richer goal semantics improve agent behavior also compares with [intent controlling a local choice](../theory/intent-controls-choices-by-distinguishing-live-alternatives.md). Neither artifact establishes that a graphical goal model is the best representation for doing so. These comparisons make the paper useful for separating requirements structure from effective operational control, not as empirical support for adopting GORE in Commonplace.
