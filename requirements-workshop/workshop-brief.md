## Six distinct research threads

A. Operationalization: desired outcome vs environmental/domain knowledge vs available machine actions. Investigate whether conditional adequacy (K and S entail R) helps open-ended knowledge work and article improvement. Distinguish operationalization, automation and execution, including operationalization during execution. Goals may be initially imprecise. Do not equate usable procedure with successful outcome or import generalization into operationalization: current task-relative KB account fixes task/executor/objective and treats broadened task scope separately.
B. Bounded agency: separate control boundaries, observation boundaries and bounded reasoning. Ask how tools, retained knowledge and executable procedures change them, how instructions can presuppose inaccessible facts/control, and what RE leaves unexplained. Compare bounded-context computational model without claiming equivalence.
C. Goal interpretation/authority: method-choice delegation is not goal-redefinition authority; problem-domain scope may need separate representation. Ask when to challenge rather than silently reinterpret goals. Keep goal misinterpretation, epistemic relevance, instrumental attractiveness and conceptual blending distinct absent evidence. Existing blending case is one episode, not frequency or causal proof.
D. Tentative knowledge/adaptation: derivation is conditional on domain assumptions. Ask how assumptions are represented/tested, how failures of execution/domain model/goal formulation can be distinguished, and what monitoring/adaptive-requirements research adds to conjectural learning. Failed outcomes underdetermine the faulty component; monitoring does not identify cause or show learning.
E. Grounding/concept revision: distinguish stipulation (chosen use), grounding (connection to phenomenon/practice), empirical conjecture (potentially false claim), and conceptual revision (change after criticism), as working distinctions, not new adopted vocabulary. Ask how prior claims are read after meaning changes, how to distinguish improved understanding from changing subject, and how silent definition changes can mimic theoretical progress. Verify Zave/Jackson usage rather than accepting supplied characterization; clarify only source terms liable to confusion with KB vocabulary. Initial philosophical comparison should use already ingested relevant literature, not another survey.
F. Commitments/economics: distinguish engineering choices, epistemic adoption, and resource commitments. Ask whether commitments depend on expected-value conjectures, how investigation is worth its cost, and what RE does not explain. No automatic unification of commitment types, expected-value numerical controller, or adopted maximand.
