# Review

The H1 title, "How do we value notes?", promises a question about how Commonplace judges note value. The body has three parts, and each one serves that question:

- **Stakes** explains why the question matters. Repo and collection goals set admission baselines but do not rank, keep or reject candidates. Autonomous production makes that gap urgent.
- **Current answer** gives Commonplace's present answer as five partial valuation lenses: admission and answerability, validity versus value, reader update and comparative work gain, counterfactual use in live work, and structural and curation proxies.
- **Unsettled** lists the open parts of the same question: root questions versus sub-questions, asymmetric selection pressure, cold-start selection, and combining dimensions.

There is no claim drift. The title asserts no thesis, and the body answers the question it asks without moving to a different one. There is no scope drift either. The body's emphasis on keeping or selecting notes under autonomous production narrows "value" in a reasonable way. That narrowing is stated in the Stakes section, and it does not contradict the title's general scope. No finding remains.
