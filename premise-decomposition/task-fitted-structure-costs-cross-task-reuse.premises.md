# Premise decomposition: Current-task fit alone does not warrant costly structural entrenchment

**Note:** kb/notes/task-fitted-structure-costs-cross-task-reuse.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** For a KB expected to outlive its current questions, current-task fit warrants only narrow, replaceable adoption; costly structural entrenchment requires an enduring constraint, discriminating transfer evidence or proof for a stated scope, or coordination value that outweighs its costs.

## Premises and counterexamples

1. **Enduring constraints, discriminating transfer evidence or proof, and net coordination value exhaust the grounds that can warrant costly structural entrenchment in a KB expected to face different future questions.** — DEFEATED — Consider a KB built for a one-time, imminent safety investigation whose task-specific hierarchy must be hard-coded throughout a large corpus to make the investigation exhaustive before a fixed deadline; alternative structures could preserve the same domain boundary but cannot be deployed in time, the hierarchy has not faced a different question, and one investigator uses it without a shared-interface benefit. The temporary delivery constraint and the avoided present harm can justify accepting later migration cost even though none of the note's three warrants applies. — GLOBAL — instance
2. **Fit to the current question set alone does not establish that a structural choice will help with a later, different question set.** — HOLDS — A proof over a declared task domain, a representative coverage argument, or successful use on discriminating questions could establish wider usefulness, but each adds evidence beyond fit to the current questions.
3. **A KB expected to outlive its current questions can encounter the same transfer problem through internal drift that a framework encounters through export.** — HOLDS — Remaining in one installation preserves local history and maintainers, but it does not prevent a later question from depending on distinctions that the earlier structure merged, omitted, or routed poorly.
4. **A changed question set can make task-derived types, tags, indexes, schemas, link vocabularies, or placement rules a mismatch even when the underlying content remains available.** — HOLDS — Full-text or semantic retrieval can sometimes bypass an obsolete structure, but that supplies an alternative access mechanism; it does not make the old structure well fitted to consumers that still depend on it.
5. **Retained content makes restructuring possible but does not by itself provide the new structural access path required by a changed question.** — HOLDS — An index generated on demand was the strongest apparent counterexample, but the generator, its query interpretation, and its organizing rule create the new path; retention alone does not.
6. **When two adoptions deliver the same current usefulness, keeping one genuinely replaceable preserves adaptation options that costly entrenchment removes.** — HOLDS — Encapsulation, adapters, or narrower scope can impose present costs, but that would violate the premise's same-usefulness comparison; no case preserving equal current usefulness made the costlier-to-replace choice retain more adaptation options.
7. **A structural rule forced by independently fixed consumer, substrate, domain, or machinery commitments is warranted while those commitments remain fixed.** — HOLDS — A design that preserves all the commitments while replacing the rule would show that the rule was selected rather than forced; a design that changes a commitment falls outside the premise.
8. **Survival across genuinely discriminating questions, or a proof over a declared domain, supports entrenchment only for the scope those cases or the proof exercise.** — HOLDS — Repeated use on superficially different tasks can miss the same failure mode, but such use is not genuinely discriminating; broader adoption without discrimination increases dependency cost without supplying transfer evidence.
9. **Coordination can warrant entrenchment before transfer evidence when the expected interoperability benefit exceeds fragmentation, lock-in, and later switching cost.** — HOLDS — A shared field or routing key can fail to deliver the expected benefit, but that defeats the empirical benefit estimate rather than the conditional premise, which requires the benefit to outweigh the named costs.

## For the human

Look first at premise 1: the three-warrant rule omits temporary but decisive constraints and present-task stakes that can rationally justify accepting later migration cost.
