# Premise decomposition: Generation confidence does not by itself certify soundness

**Note:** kb/notes/llm-generation-confidence-tracks-typicality-not-soundness.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** An autoregressive model's next-token confidence does not by itself entail factual truth or inferential validity, although task-specific validation can make confidence useful for scoped decisions and high-assurance acceptance requires soundness-directed evidence from a separately validated operation.

## Premises and counterexamples

1. **An autoregressive model's generation confidence, as defined here, is the conditional probability it assigns to a possible next token under its own distribution.** — HOLDS — Sampling temperature, decoding policy, sequence-level scores, and verbal confidence reports were candidate counterexamples, but they are different quantities or transformations; none changes what the expressly defined next-token probability denotes.
2. **Factual truth and inferential validity are properties distinct from a token's conditional probability under a model distribution.** — HOLDS — A model can be perfectly accurate on a bounded task or constrained to emit valid objects, but that alignment does not identify probability with soundness; it supplies a separately established relation between two distinct properties.
3. **Without a separately established relation between next-token probability and soundness, a high probability does not entail that the continuation is true or valid.** — HOLDS — The strongest counterexample candidate was a sound-only model whose support contains no false or invalid continuations, but using that fact as a certificate depends on establishing the support invariant separately; the probability value alone does not establish it.
4. **The next-token objective alone does not determine the direction or strength of the statistical association between confidence and soundness across every evaluation population.** — HOLDS — Populations dominated by canonical facts or checked derivations can yield positive association, while populations dominated by repeated misconceptions or common fallacies can yield negative association; no objective-level constraint was found that forces one ordering independently of the learned distribution and the population being measured.
5. **The deductive no-certification claim does not depend on a general statistical anti-correlation between confidence and soundness.** — HOLDS — As an active statistical refuter, consider a declared population of familiar one-token capital completions paired with plausible wrong cities: if correct cities outrank wrong alternatives more often than the reverse, that prevalence result refutes anti-correlation for the population exactly as the note says, yet it does not make high probability entail soundness or defeat the no-certification claim.
6. **When task-specific evaluation establishes adequate calibration and discrimination, confidence can support review prioritization or abstention only within the scope and limits of that validation.** — HOLDS — A distribution shift can make a previously useful threshold fail, but that is already charged to the premise's explicit scope condition rather than counterexampled by it; no case was found in which scoped validation supports an unscoped inference.
7. **Aggregate calibration alone does not establish that confidence discriminates which individual outputs are wrong.** — HOLDS — Even when every confidence bin's observed error rate matches its stated probability, errors can remain intermixed with correct outputs inside each bin; calibration therefore need not rank individual errors above or below individual correct outputs.
8. **A verbal self-assessment is another generated judgment rather than a direct readout of the next-token probability that produced the original answer.** — HOLDS — A self-assessment can become useful by invoking retrieval, tools, re-derivation, or a trained detector, but then its evidential value comes from that additional mechanism and its validation, not from being a verbal report alone.
9. **Every high-assurance acceptance decision needs a separately validated soundness-directed operation in addition to generation confidence.** — DOUBTFUL — “High-assurance” is not given a truth condition: if it means a stringent but nonzero population-risk bound, a confidence-based selective predictor validated to that bound may justify acceptance without a compiler, proof checker, source check, or other per-output verifier; if it means claim-level certification, the listed verifier requirement is much stronger. — LOCAL — instance
10. **A detector, internal probe, or adversarial re-derivation is not an independent verifier merely by construction and must be validated before it can supply soundness evidence.** — HOLDS — Such a mechanism can in fact be reliable, but no construction label alone rules out shared errors, proxy learning, or distribution shift; a proof or empirical demonstration of reliability would be the separate validation the premise requires.

## For the human

Look first at premise 9: defining “high-assurance” as claim-level certification, rather than a sufficiently small validated population risk, would close the remaining ambiguity without disturbing the deductive headline or turning the separately refutable anti-correlation hypothesis into a premise.
