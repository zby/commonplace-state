# Premise decomposition: Unifying conjecture makes a general and its instances intelligible together

**Note:** kb/notes/conjecture-is-seeing-the-particular-as-an-instance-of-the-general.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** A unifying conjecture makes known cases and a proposed general structure mutually intelligible by recasting the cases as instances, through forms that range from shared feature to shared structure to generative model with increasing explanatory commitment.

## Premises and counterexamples

1. **There is a nontrivial subtype of conjecture whose distinctive operation is to propose a general structure and reinterpret multiple known cases as its instances.** — HOLDS — Singular existential hypotheses and predictions show that not every conjecture performs this operation, but they do not defeat the narrower subtype; lemma extraction supplies a concrete case in which proposing the general and reinterpreting prior cases are one operation.
2. **The proposed structure picks out a relevant, discriminating commonality rather than merely an extensionally fitting predicate, so the general-instance recasting actually adds intelligibility.** — DOUBTFUL — Any finite set of cases can be grouped by an ad hoc predicate such as “is proof A or was written on Tuesday”; that predicate supplies a general and instances without revealing what matters about them. The note requires usable criteria only in its later cost argument, leaving the central intelligibility claim without a stated non-arbitrariness condition. — GLOBAL
3. **A structure may be old to the field yet participate in a conjecture when applying it to these cases is not entailed by the available evidence.** — HOLDS — Merely learning an established category and deductively classifying obvious members would not be conjecture, but the note's discovery-lifecycle condition excludes that case; an uncertain application of an existing structure can still be an ampliative claim.
4. **Known cases constrain both the content and scope of the proposed general even when the structure is imported from elsewhere.** — DEFEATED — If a knowledge base conjecturally applies an already fixed principal-agent model to two local cases, those cases can constrain whether and where the model applies without constraining the model's content. Narrowing the claim from “content and scope” to the proposed mapping or local scope would preserve the central commitment. — LOCAL
5. **Shared feature, shared structure, and generative model can be ordered by increasing explanatory commitment.** — DOUBTFUL — A precise invariance theorem can explain why several cases must share a result, while a highly flexible stochastic process may be capable of generating those cases yet make few discriminating commitments. The ordering works for idealized examples only if “generative model” means a process proposed as responsible for the commonality, not merely one capable of reproducing the observations as the table says. — GLOBAL
6. **The three forms remain usable distinctions even though a single unification can occupy more than one form.** — DOUBTFUL — A latent-variable model simultaneously extracts a shared probabilistic structure and generates the observations, and the note gives no criterion for whether such a hybrid should be treated as structural, generative, or both. This blurs the taxonomy but does not defeat the general-and-instances operation. — LOCAL
7. **Mathematical lemma extraction is a genuine witness in which a new general makes earlier particulars intelligible as instances.** — HOLDS — A counterexample hunt finds bad retrospective abstractions that add no value, but not a defeater for the existential witness: when two proofs genuinely use the same intermediate result, extracting that result as a reusable lemma reveals their common proof structure.
8. **A conjecture can be ampliative without being unifying, so singular hypotheses and predictions mark a real boundary around the proposed subtype.** — HOLDS — A conjecture that one unseen contaminant caused one failed experiment may posit only a token cause and need not regroup multiple known cases; the fact that other causal hypotheses do unify cases does not erase this boundary case.
9. **Feature-based candidate retrieval is generally cheaper than recognizing shared structure, while proposing a generative model requires a further inferential step.** — DOUBTFUL — An expensive assay may be needed to detect an observable feature, whereas a familiar proof template or a mechanistic diagnostic can make structural or generative recognition nearly immediate. The note acknowledges dependence on vocabulary, tools, and access, so this challenges the default cost ordering rather than the distinction between retrieval and recognition. — LOCAL
10. **Naming a reusable structure together with discriminating instance criteria can lower the cost of later recognition enough to justify the abstraction's context cost.** — HOLDS — Names alone do not help, but the premise includes the note's safeguards: reusable criteria, exclusion of tempting false positives, and an expected-benefit comparison against added context cost; no counterexample defeats that conditional claim.

## For the human

Look first at premise 2: the note's central move needs a non-arbitrariness or discrimination condition before recasting cases under any fitting general can be said to make them mutually intelligible.
