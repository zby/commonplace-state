# Premise decomposition: Intent-framed delegation is a control regime; prompt length does not establish it

**Note:** `kb/notes/intent-framed-delegation-is-a-control-regime-not-a-short-prompt.md`
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** In consequential planner-executor handoffs, preserving upstream intent and non-negotiable boundaries depends on a governed control regime that allocates information and decision rights across the execution boundary, so prompt length or outcome wording alone does not establish adequacy.

## Premises and counterexamples
1. **In the note's scoped case, the delegated choice is a real execution-time choice of means rather than a fully pre-specified procedure.** — HOLDS — A case with no live means choice would be outsourcing or procedure-following rather than the handoff type the note is claiming about, so it does not defeat the premise within scope.
2. **Prompt text is only one possible carrier of the control relation; policies, tooling, workflow roles, and orchestrators can also bind authority, constraints, verification, and recovery.** — HOLDS — An active hunt does not find a concrete case where those surrounding controls cease to count as operative governance merely because they are not written into the prompt.
3. **A short handoff can preserve intent when the surrounding system already supplies the relevant controls, and a long handoff can fail when those controls remain unset.** — HOLDS — Concrete contrasting cases are easy to construct in the note's favor: a terse instruction inside a gated workflow inherits approval, sandbox, and escalation controls, while a detailed prompt without authority, ownership, or recovery rules still leaves the consequential failure modes open.
4. **For the military-source pattern to transfer, the target system must instantiate the same underlying information-and-authority relation rather than borrowing the label alone.** — HOLDS — No concrete counterexample shows doctrine transfer by naming alone when the target lacks bounded authority and execution-time evidence; the note also limits the source claim to relevance, not proved target effectiveness.
5. **In consequential handoffs, every failure-relevant control surface needs an operative governance mechanism, and outcome wording alone leaves those surfaces unsettled.** — DOUBTFUL — A concrete counterexample is a workflow where the worker can only propose a patch and a mandatory downstream approval gate owns authority, integration, verification, and recovery in one place; separate mechanisms for each listed surface are not needed as distinct moving parts even though governance still exists. — LOCAL — instance
6. **Upstream must hold purpose or binding constraints that the executor cannot safely reconstruct from local state.** — DOUBTFUL — A concrete counterexample is a standardized incident-response system where service objective, permissions, rollback rules, and escalation thresholds are already encoded in shared runbooks and tooling, so a terse cue can preserve intent without any nonrecoverable planner-side facts beyond the standing regime. — LOCAL — instance
7. **Because prompt length by itself does not encode who may decide what, what constraints bind, how outputs integrate, or how failures return control, length alone cannot establish structural adequacy.** — HOLDS — An active hunt finds no case where word count alone, absent the underlying governance relation, guarantees preservation of intent in a consequential handoff.

## For the human
Look first at premise 6: the note likely overstates the need for upstream-exclusive intent, and should probably allow that the binding purpose and constraints may already be prebound in the surrounding system rather than carried uniquely by the delegator.
