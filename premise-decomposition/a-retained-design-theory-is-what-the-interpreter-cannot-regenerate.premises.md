# Premise decomposition: Design rationale must preserve decision premises its interpreter cannot regenerate

**Note:** kb/notes/a-retained-design-theory-is-what-the-interpreter-cannot-regenerate.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** For the source-checkout consumer scoped to the implementation, full git history, and general knowledge, retained design rationale must supply every current decision premise that consumer needs but cannot faithfully regenerate at the required fidelity, while a recoverable explanation with no independent authority, cross-check, provenance, activation, or exact-record role has only cache status.

## Premises and counterexamples
1. **For the scoped source-checkout consumer, recoverability is relative to a declared consumer, required fidelity, and allowed source set, with separate rationale excluded from the reconstruction inputs.** — HOLDS — The active hunt found shallow checkouts, vendored readers, and consumers able to query a product owner or live service, but each changes the consumer or its allowed sources; the note explicitly scopes the first two out and requires any additional source to be declared.
2. **Faithful regeneration reproduces the same decision-relevant claim, including every warrant, epistemic status, or boundary required by the declared use, rather than any plausible story consistent with visible behavior.** — HOLDS — The hunt used two different rationales that license the same present implementation but different future modifications; that case confirms the premise, while differences that cannot affect the declared use fall below the stipulated fidelity threshold.
3. **When the allowed sources are compatible with multiple current decision premises, reasoning from those sources cannot faithfully identify which premise actually governs the design.** — HOLDS — A stronger interpreter can rank or invent explanations for identical artifacts produced under different unrecorded constraints, but the hunt found no way for such abduction to identify the actual constraint rather than a substitute.
4. **Implementation, git, and general knowledge can make intents, relevance boundaries, rejected alternatives, and warrants recoverable only when the needed content is recorded or entailed there, so recoverability must be tested premise by premise rather than assigned by category.** — HOLDS — Contracts can record scope, commits can record rejected alternatives, and tests can encode a warrant, but those cases become recoverable under the note's rule; no category supplied its project-contingent content when every allowed source omitted it.
5. **If the scoped consumer needs a current decision premise and cannot faithfully regenerate it from the allowed sources, retained rationale must supply that premise.** — HOLDS — The hunt tried on-demand counsel, a rerunnable benchmark, and a conservative rule that forbids the affected modification: counsel expands the declared source set, a reproducible benchmark makes the warrant recoverable, and the refusal removes the premise from what that consumer needs, leaving no in-scope counterexample.
6. **Nonrecoverability creates no retention obligation for a premise that is obsolete, refuted, or irrelevant to every declared consumer role.** — HOLDS — An irrecoverable historical misconception supplies a concrete test case, but once explanation, modification, audit, and exact-record uses are all absent, the note excludes it as neither current nor decision-relevant.
7. **Semantic recoverability is independent of a retained passage's authority, cross-check, provenance, activation, or exact-record role.** — HOLDS — A signed decision record, governing specification, and independently authored check can duplicate content inferable from code while still performing their distinct roles; the hunt found no reason that successful reconstruction would perform those roles in their place.
8. **When an explanation is faithfully recoverable and has no independent role, its remaining value consists in avoiding reconstruction work and is therefore cache value.** — HOLDS — The hunt considered onboarding prose, a concise shared vocabulary, and a bounded-context summary; each either only saves source access and reasoning or, if it canonically fixes meaning or triggers action, acquires one of the excluded independent roles.
9. **Whether a role-free cache should be retained depends on the reconstruction work it avoids relative to its creation, validation, retrieval, maintenance, and failure costs.** — HOLDS — Expensive repeated reconstruction can justify keeping the cache, while cheap reconstruction or high staleness risk can justify omitting it; tail risk and uncertain future use alter the compared costs rather than defeat the comparison.
10. **Greater interpreter strength can recover more explanations from surviving traces but cannot recover a particular contingent fact that no allowed source determines.** — HOLDS — Memorization, an external oracle, or a leaked copy would add a trace to general knowledge or another declared source; with those removed, the hunt found no mechanism by which reasoning strength alone selects the missing historical fact.

## For the human
Look first at premise 5: it carries the necessity claim, so any on-demand authority or live evidence source available to the consumer must be added to the declared source set before applying it.
