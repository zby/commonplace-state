# Premise decomposition: Structured-prompt gains do not establish training-distribution selection

**Note:** kb/notes/structured-prompt-gains-do-not-establish-distribution-selection.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** A gain in task performance after adding prompt structure does not by itself establish that the structure selected learned patterns from a higher-quality subset of the model's training data.

## Premises and counterexamples
1. **At least one mechanism other than higher-quality training-distribution selection can produce a task-performance gain after structure is added to a prompt.** — HOLDS — A template can require an intermediate consistency check that catches an error even if its headings have no training-subset-selection effect; the active hunt therefore found a witness for the premise rather than a defeater.
2. **Formatting constraint, additional computation, explicit task decomposition, and higher-quality training-distribution selection are causally distinguishable mechanisms even though more than one can operate in the same prompt.** — HOLDS — A prompt that merely forces a valid output schema can change compliance without improving reasoning, while a process-only prompt can require the same intermediate checks without genre headings. Co-occurrence does not collapse these interventions into one mechanism, and the active hunt found no case that makes the distinctions idle.
3. **A performance difference that all of the live mechanisms can produce contains no observation that attributes the difference specifically to training-distribution selection.** — HOLDS — Even a large or repeatable accuracy gain remains compatible with an explicit process producing the gain unless some additional intervention or measurement changes the mechanisms' predictions; the active hunt found no counterexample in which the undifferentiated outcome alone identifies which compatible cause operated.
4. **The empirical results summarized in the note report performance or behavior changes but do not include a control or direct observation that discriminates training-distribution selection from the named rival mechanisms.** — DOUBTFUL — The note reports headline task results and limitations, but those summaries do not establish that the underlying studies contain no matched ablation, mediation analysis, or activation evidence bearing on the mechanism; one such omitted analysis could falsify this bounded characterization of the current evidence. — LOCAL — instance
5. **A formal result about what a bounded learner extracts from differently arranged training data does not, without an additional transfer argument, identify the cause of an inference-time prompting effect.** — HOLDS — A theorem can hold for learning from an ordered dataset while an already-trained model responds to a prompt heading through formatting compliance or an explicit procedure; the active hunt found no bridge in the note that turns the learning-time result into an inference-time causal identification.
6. **Adopting structure for work that it directly requires or exposes does not require training-distribution selection to be the cause of any observed benefit.** — HOLDS — Evidence separation, intermediate checks, output validation, and human reviewability remain available design reasons in a model for which headings have no training-subset-selection effect; the active hunt found no defeater to that independence.

## For the human
Look first at premise 4: verify the cited studies themselves, because an omitted matched ablation or mechanistic analysis would require narrowing the note's characterization of the current evidence even though the general identification claim would remain intact.
