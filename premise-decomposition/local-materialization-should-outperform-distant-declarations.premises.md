# Premise decomposition: Local materialization should outperform distant natural-language declarations

**Note:** kb/notes/local-materialization-should-outperform-distant-declarations.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** Across a preregistered sample of LLM tasks in which a natural-language declaration is distant or its application requires a non-obvious inference, a local view generated from the canonical value should produce a higher rate of correct, non-contradictory application than declaration-only presentation while leaving the canonical value as the sole maintenance authority.

## Premises and counterexamples

1. **Natural-language content consumed by an LLM has no semantics-assigned dereference operation that guarantees a declared fact will govern every later use.** — HOLDS — Pronouns, cross-references, structured prompt fields, and deterministic decoding were candidate defeaters, but none assigns the model's downstream semantic application in the way a formal consumer assigns a binding's resolution; where a renderer resolves a symbolic field first, the guarantee comes from that renderer rather than from natural-language consumption.
2. **Across the stated population of distant or non-obvious-use tasks, declaration-only presentation leaves enough correct-application failures attributable to recovering or applying the declaration for a locality intervention to have measurable headroom.** — DOUBTFUL — Current models might ordinarily treat prominent frontmatter, headers, or repeated instruction schemas as artifact-global constraints even at long distances, while their remaining errors arise from capabilities that proximity does not repair; if that is the prevalent pattern, the declaration-only arm will be near the attainable ceiling for this intervention. — GLOBAL — prevalence
3. **Across enough of the stated tasks, putting the canonical fact or a fixed derived label beside the use site removes the particular recovery or inference burden responsible for declaration-only errors.** — DOUBTFUL — For non-obvious applications, the hard step may ordinarily be deriving a context-sensitive consequence rather than finding the fact; repeating the exact value locally would then preserve the hard inference, while a fixed label would not encode the varying consequence. — GLOBAL — prevalence
4. **Across the fact types represented in the sample, a correct and applicable local view can usually be generated from the canonical value without an additional contextual judgment becoming part of the intervention.** — DOUBTFUL — The note itself distinguishes mechanically derivable labels from contextual natural-language consequences, and non-obvious applications may commonly fall in the latter class; if so, the generated arm must either omit the useful consequence, repeat only the insufficient source value, or introduce judgment that the canonical value alone does not determine. — GLOBAL — prevalence
5. **Across the held-out evaluation set, gains from local availability will exceed losses caused by added tokens, repeated emphasis, conditional-rendering errors, or new opportunities for contradiction.** — DOUBTFUL — When one fact governs many use sites, the generated views may ordinarily consume enough context and redistribute enough attention that otherwise-correct outputs degrade or conflict as often as declaration-only failures are repaired; the proposed experiment has not yet measured this net effect. — GLOBAL — prevalence
6. **When a local view is an exact value or mechanically derived label that is regenerated or deterministically checked against one canonical symbolic value, the view does not become a second maintenance authority.** — HOLDS — A stale cached view or an independently edited copy looked like a defeater, but either fails the stated regeneration/check condition; under that condition the local text is a derived display whose truth remains controlled by the canonical value.

## For the human

Look first at premise 3: the intervention directly removes distance, but the conjecture also covers non-obvious applications whose prevalent difficulty may be contextual derivation rather than recovery of the declared fact.
