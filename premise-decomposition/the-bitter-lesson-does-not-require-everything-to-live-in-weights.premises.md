# Premise decomposition: The Bitter Lesson does not require everything to live in weights

**Note:** kb/articles/the-bitter-lesson-does-not-require-everything-to-live-in-weights.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** Sutton's Bitter Lesson requires behavior-shaping content to face scalable search-and-learning pressure, but it does not by itself require selected content to be encoded and consumed only as model weights rather than retained as natural-language, symbolic, or mixed artifacts.

## Premises and counterexamples
1. **Sutton's 2019 Bitter Lesson is best read as a claim about how behavior-shaping content is generated and selected, not as a direct claim that the winning representation must be dense model weights.** — HOLDS — The article's target is explicitly Sutton's pattern of methods that exploit computation through search and learning; an active counterexample hunt finds cases where Sutton criticizes hand-built knowledge and domain-specific structure, but not a direct requirement that durable selected content must always be stored in weights.
2. **Production method and representational form are separable axes for behavior-shaping content.** — HOLDS — Prompt optimization versus hand-written prompting, and learned versus directly set dense controller values show that the method of producing content can vary while the representational form stays fixed; no concrete case makes the two axes logically inseparable.
3. **Natural-language and symbolic artifacts can be behavior-shaping content in the same relevant sense as parameter values, because agents and systems consume them to determine future behavior.** — HOLDS — System prompts, tool definitions, schemas, programs, and skills can constrain or direct behavior; the counterexample that some such artifacts are merely documentation fails when the artifact is actually consumed by the running system.
4. **Search-selected localized artifacts already exist in bounded settings.** — HOLDS — FunSearch's retained programs and AlphaDev's discovered assembly routines are concrete cases of search or learning selecting non-weight artifacts that later shape behavior; the boundedness of those settings limits the conclusion but does not defeat the premise.
5. **Those bounded examples are enough to reject the universal weights-only inference.** — HOLDS — A universal claim that search-selected behavior-shaping content must live in weights is defeated by even bounded cases where selected programs or routines persist outside weights; the examples need not establish broad economic superiority to do that narrower work.
6. **The claim does not depend on learned localized artifacts being economical, general, or best at scale.** — HOLDS — The article repeatedly narrows its conclusion: it says artifact search still faces the scaling test and may lose on total cost, so a counterexample where artifact learning scales poorly would attack a stronger claim the article does not make.
7. **Current artifact-learning systems leave enough designer-specified structure fixed that their successes cannot validate the whole decomposition.** — HOLDS — Agent Symbolic Learning, Memento-Skills, and Co-Harness each retain directly specified structures such as edit operators, routing, judges, schedules, and artifact boundaries; no inspected example removes that residual design choice.
8. **If a localized artifact is proposed, tested, retained, revised, and retired by computational search or learning, Sutton's lesson no longer directly rejects it merely because its retained form is readable or symbolic.** — DOUBTFUL — A stronger Sutton-style reading could treat explicit artifact languages, human-readable decompositions, and hand-chosen update spaces as domain structure that becomes the long-run bottleneck even when individual artifacts inside that space are search-selected. — LOCAL — priced-exception
9. **The relevant research burden is an end-to-end comparison under comparable total budgets, not a representation-only comparison.** — HOLDS — Because costs arise from model training, inference, artifact search, evaluators, maintenance, and human decisions, a counterexample would need a setting where representation alone determines the comparison; the article's own cases show update machinery and evaluation costs changing with representation.
10. **Localized retention can remain useful where changes are sparse and artifact boundaries match the changes.** — DOUBTFUL — A concrete deployment can have sparse external changes but dense hidden dependencies, so a small localized edit still forces wide regression testing or breaks behavior through retrieval and interaction effects. — LOCAL — instance
11. **Parametric learning has a default update mechanism that heterogeneous artifact systems lack.** — HOLDS — Backpropagation supplies a general parameter-credit mechanism inside a fixed differentiable graph, while an artifact repository of prompts, tests, schemas, tools, and memory items has no comparable default way to assign blame after a failed release; validators and traces are partial substitutes rather than a general mechanism.
12. **The article's final prescription, "do not protect human-authored knowledge from search," follows from the narrow rebuttal.** — HOLDS — Once the issue is production method rather than representational form, the practical consequence is to expose explicit structure to proposal, test, retention, revision, and retirement; an active counterexample hunt found objections to the economics of artifact learning, but those objections do not defeat this weaker prescription.

## For the human
Look first at premise 8: the article should keep making clear that learned localized artifacts answer the weights-only extrapolation, not every stronger Sutton-style objection to fixed artifact languages and designer-chosen update spaces.
