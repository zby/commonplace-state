# Premise decomposition: Admitting nonbinding natural-language candidates lets criticism precede formalization

**Note:** kb/notes/acceptance-gate-language-bounds-what-a-reflective-loop-can-reach.md
**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** For a reflective loop considering externally interpreted theories, admitting a natural-language proposal without operative authority permits criticism of its commitments before a faithful formal surrogate exists, while the stage's cost depends on governance and coupling rather than representation alone and its semantic reach depends on available translation paths.

## Premises and counterexamples

1. **The capability requires search or another upstream path to supply the gate with a natural-language theory whose commitments exist before a faithful formal surrogate; admission does not generate such a candidate by itself.** — HOLDS — The hunt found loops whose search emits only formal terms, but those cases remove the stated input rather than defeat the conditional capability; where search does produce a preformal theory, direct admission changes when that theory can enter evaluation.
2. **A language-capable critic can challenge at least some commitments of an externally interpreted theory without first constructing a symbolic artifact with formal semantics.** — HOLDS — Quantitative tests may require a simulator or operational model, but that does not prevent earlier criticism of a theory's causal premises, scope, undefined predicates, or proposed observations; the hunt found no requirement that every such criticism first be encoded symbolically.
3. **Direct natural-language interpretation identifies the candidate's intended commitments well enough for substantive criticism to target the candidate rather than an unintended reading.** — DOUBTFUL — A proposal that says scheduler failures arise when demand exceeds “fixed capacity” may mean physical capacity, configured allocation, or capacity available after interference; a critic can flag the ambiguity, but cannot yet know which substantive premise to attack or which test discriminates the intended theory. Natural-language admission therefore avoids a formal-surrogate prerequisite without necessarily avoiding an interpretation-fidelity problem. — LOCAL — instance
4. **A gate restricted to formal objects cannot directly evaluate a preformal prose theory; the loop must first present an admissible formal surrogate if that theory is to reach the gate.** — HOLDS — Putting a natural-language compiler upstream, or inside a wrapper around the gate, still performs translation before the formal gate evaluates its output; allowing the gate itself to interpret the prose changes the admissible language and is not a counterexample.
5. **Treating a formal surrogate as the same candidate requires preserving the consequences relevant to adoption, and checking the surrogate does not by itself warrant its correspondence to an informally interpreted source.** — HOLDS — A proof-carrying translation from a source with formal semantics can establish equivalence, but then the relevant source commitments have already been formalized; for a genuinely preformal theory there is no formal statement of intended meaning against which the surrogate's ordinary proof can establish fidelity.
6. **A reflective loop can separate evaluative admission and retention from operative authority, so a theory can be criticized as a proposal without becoming a procedure, certification, or contract.** — HOLDS — Some governance regimes automatically couple admission to action, but an isolated proposal record or review branch supplies a concrete separation; those coupled regimes limit applicability rather than defeat the possibility claimed for nonbinding candidates.
7. **Natural-language form and nonbinding status do not by themselves make rejection cheap; authority, downstream coupling, sunk cost, and rollback cost can dominate prototype standing.** — HOLDS — An informal theory can motivate costly dependent work without becoming a contract, while an isolated typed model can be replaced cheaply; the counterexample hunt therefore found form alone neither necessary nor sufficient for low replacement cost.
8. **Proof and criticism warrant different properties for externally interpreted theories: proof establishes entailment within a formalization, while criticism can attack interpretation and world fit but supplies no mechanical guarantee.** — HOLDS — Adding verified measurements or correspondence assumptions only moves world-facing claims into additional premises, and an informal critic can still misjudge them; neither move collapses the two warrant targets in the stated empirical scope.
9. **Cheaper formal construction, proof generation, and proof checking do not by themselves resolve an uncertain source-to-surrogate mapping or supply evidence of model-to-world fit.** — HOLDS — Automation can eliminate mechanical formalization costs, but the hunt found no case in which lowering only those costs determines an unsettled concept's intended boundary or creates missing deployment observations.
10. **A reliable upstream translation can let a formal-only gate reach the same semantic improvement even though its evaluation order remains translation before evaluation.** — HOLDS — A prose scheduling theory translated faithfully into a temporal-logic model can be evaluated and adopted through a formal gate, so direct admissibility controls presentation order rather than semantic reach when such a translation path is available.

## For the human

Look first at premise 3: prose admission removes the need for a formal surrogate, but ambiguous prose can shift the same fidelity problem into the critic's interpretation unless clarification counts as the first criticism or the candidate is sufficiently determinate.
