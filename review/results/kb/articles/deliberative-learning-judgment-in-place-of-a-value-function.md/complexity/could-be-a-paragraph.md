# Review

## Summary

A one-paragraph rewrite would lose several load-bearing parts that no single insight contains:

- **The worked example.** It traces one routing edit through admission by judgment, trace-guided narrowing of its scope, and revision of the review check itself. Later sections rely on it for the reversibility argument and the open-verdict argument.
- **The boundary cases of the definition.** A validator is the limiting case. Opaque weights fail the first condition. Direct input-driven updates, trace logging and count-triggers fail the second.
- **The three-precedent argument.** The Gödel machine, PRODIGY and EURISKO each carry a different inference: what proof costs, single-loop targeted correction, and double-loop criterion revision. Together they locate the bottleneck in what filled the value-function role.
- **The Bitter Lesson argument.** It separates production method from representational form, engages a named rival hypothesis and an empirical counter-study, and states a bootstrap condition.
- **The success criterion and empirical test.** These include the counterfactual definition of "learned", the open verdict for recent edits, the two-part operative-dependence and improvement check, four failure modes, and a falsification condition.

These parts are distinct claims, boundary cases and failure conditions. The multi-section structure is justified.
