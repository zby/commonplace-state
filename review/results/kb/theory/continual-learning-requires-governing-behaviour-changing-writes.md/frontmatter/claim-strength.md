# Review

## Summary

The title "Continual learning requires governing behaviour-changing writes, not just storing content" makes a contestable claim. An informed reader could argue the opposite in two ways. One view treats continual learning as a parametric problem of plasticity and forgetting, so governance of writes is incidental. Another view, common in agent-memory systems, treats accumulating and retrieving retained content as sufficient for learning. The title rejects both by making governance (selection, validation and authorization) the requirement.

The main verb "requires" carries the hard point; it does not presuppose its subject and then predicate an obvious consequence. The "not just storing content" contrast names the position being denied, which sharpens rather than weakens the claim.
