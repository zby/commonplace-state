# Review

## Summary

No sentence has a plausible unintended reading.

Candidates checked and cleared:

- "Truth does not change with time, and for a universal claim it is never established." "It" can only refer to truth.
- "So use of a claim inside the theory can be a test, and often it is not." Negation and pronoun scope are clear.
- "Neither may be substituted for the other." The section heading and the two preceding paragraphs establish the two kinds of evidence as the antecedents.
- "Which repair to make is a decision, taken by comparing the test records of the claims involved and what each repair does to the theory's fit to its problem." Read literally, this compares two unlike things: test records and repair effects. The natural reading is "comparing (the test records) and (what each repair does)", meaning both are weighed. No reader would plausibly take it otherwise.
- "Tests of the claim do not settle them." "Them" can refer to the objectives, commitments and grants of authority, or to whether the claim belongs. Both readings make the same point here, so the ambiguity does not change the meaning.
- "The three questions are separate, not statistically independent." The contrast is explicit, and the next sentence settles it.
