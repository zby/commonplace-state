# Review

The title asserts that a better-factory claim compares operative states, not the change that produced them or a candidate. It also asserts that the comparison runs under an assessment relation fixed in advance. An informed reader could reasonably argue the opposite on both counts. Improvement is commonly read off a construction event or a staging evaluation of an uninstalled candidate. It is also commonly judged under criteria chosen after the result is seen. The note itself concedes that a retrospective criterion can ground a legitimate improvement claim, which confirms that the antecedence requirement is a contestable position and not a truism. The claim is strong enough.
