# Review

The central claim uses three abstract terms: "claim", "warrant" and "working theory".

"Warrant" is the strongest candidate for unearned generality, since the concrete alternative would be "truth". The note argues for the abstraction. The Scope section says claims in the KB may be empirical, formal, source-derived or procedural, so literal factual truth is not the only independent standard. The mechanism does not depend on the kind of warrant either. Any standard that tests a claim over its own stated scope ignores the other claims in the theory, and fit is relational. Downgrading to "truth" would leave the formal-validity and source-support cases unaddressed. The opening paragraph names those cases as separate warrant routes, and the abstract claim forbids treating any of them as settling fit. It therefore forbids more than the concrete version, not merely covers more.

"Claim" and "working theory" are the natural level of the argument. Its examples operate on claims as units and on a theory as the structure they compose into, and no narrower term, such as "note", appears in the reasoning.

The abstraction is concentrated where the argument needs it, in the warrant-versus-fit distinction, not spread across the prose.
