# Review

The note is a conceptual argument. It reports no observations and cites no evidence. Its material claims are of three kinds:

- Possibility claims, worded with "may" or "can": a warranted claim may fit poorly; a false claim may integrate smoothly; a contradiction may identify a defect elsewhere.
- One consequence derived from those possibilities: neither evaluation determines the other, and coherence is not independent evidence of warrant.
- One normative rule: the prohibition on substitution.

Each claim's status can be read from the text. The possibility claims are hedged as possibilities, and that matches what an argument by counterexample can establish. The central claim is a negative one, "does not determine". It follows from those possibilities, so its declarative wording is not overstated. The note's own construction, the two evaluations, is introduced as "at least two different evaluations", which is light proposing language. The Scope section deliberately leaves fit without a complete definition. No quantitative or empirical-sounding claim appears.

No support is spread across claims, because the note presents no external evidence that could spread. No well-supported finding is hedged without reason.
