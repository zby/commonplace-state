# Assessment

Proposed purpose: Help readers designing automated review of explanatory articles distinguish what a reach-and-economy assessment can report now from the experiments needed to establish its compression interpretation and practical benefit.

The argument can stand. No rewrite or restructuring of the argument is needed.

## Recommended change, ranked by value

1. Make the assessment and its two validation questions easier to distinguish on first reading. In the opening, “The three assessments have different roles” introduces a count before clearly naming its members: reach and economy are grouped together, predictive coding is an experimental method, and task gain is an outcome measure. The next paragraph counts two claims. Name the textual assessment, the compression experiment, and the task experiment consistently, keeping the two hypotheses explicit. At the start of “Testing the two claims,” briefly reconnect these experiments to the report already described under “What the reviewer would do.” Preserve the detailed qualifications in the experimental subsections; the issue is the reader's map, not the argument's substance. **Cost:** a few local transitions and an opening paragraph. **Pass:** `readability-and-flow`, scoped to the opening through “The proposal concerns explanatory articles” and the introductory paragraph of “Testing the two claims.”

No other pass has a clear enough benefit to recommend. In particular, the technical coding discussion is necessary to distinguish net savings from savings obtained by giving the article away. Its acknowledged protocol gaps should survive. The article defines its central terms, and its title is literal.

## Sentence a hostile reviewer would attack first

“Reach and economy provide practical evidence that the reader gains a more compact, reusable account.”

The reviewer could ask how either property is assessed reliably, what counts as an independent assumption, and why these judgments should track net code savings after representation costs. The later section acknowledges the missing general protocol and proposes testing the relationship. That makes this the proposal's central exposed hypothesis, rather than an established measurement result.

## Candidate overclaim decisions

- **Divergence — defend:** “Reach and economy provide practical evidence that the reader gains a more compact, reusable account.” Preserve the proposed evidential role of reach and economy. The article explicitly calls them plausible indicators, distinguishes the proposal from Schmidhuber's result, and proposes experiments that could undermine it. Narrowing this to mere reconstruction would discard the contribution. Consistent naming in the opening should keep that hypothesis visible without presenting it as validated.

No additional candidate overclaim warrants a narrowing recommendation. The task experiment is already separated from causal attribution, the compression experiment already counts representation costs, and the closing limits what a favorable assessment certifies. Do not add `narrow-overclaims` solely because a defended hypothesis is recorded.

## Stale qualifications

None found. The explanatory-article scope remains relevant; coding experiments remain conditional on a feasible domain; the task section preserves the distinction between assessment usefulness and a confirmed compression interpretation. The five-part reconstruction table and the “two claims” references match the body. The opening's “three assessments” is a presentation ambiguity, not a stale qualification requiring a claim change.

The drift-versus-duration example makes the difference between deriving a consequence and checking it concrete, while the experimental sections carefully separate compression, usefulness, and causal attribution.
