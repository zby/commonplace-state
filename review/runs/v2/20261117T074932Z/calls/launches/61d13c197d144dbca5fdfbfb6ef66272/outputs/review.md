# Friction check: Removing approval waits can leave required human evaluation in place

**Central claim (one sentence):** Making adoption run without an approval wait does not, by itself, remove required human evaluation, because human evaluation can still be required for monitoring or later revisions, and whether it is required depends on whether the named process, under its requirements, stalls or leaves a task undone without it.

## Filter
**Filter:** SURVIVES

The claim holds three things together: adoption runs unattended, human evaluation is still required somewhere, and "required" is judged relative to a named process. These can coexist; the weekly-review example is one consistent instance of all three. The nearest strain is between "stated requirements" (line 12) and "actual execution" (Scope). The note divides them so that actual waits fall under the blocking condition and written requirements under the task condition, so the claim does not split. It does leave the source of "required" divided, which is the top joint below.

## Thinnest joints

The weakest three joints all sit where the note uses its own stipulations instead of showing a mechanism. These are the basis of "required" (stated vs. habitual vs. functional), the line between an optional study and a revision's required assessment, and the choice of process boundary. Each is consistent, but each relies on stipulating the relevant fact in the example instead of giving a criterion a reader could apply to a new case. The basis of "required" ranks first because the other two inherit it. The optional-study boundary ranks above the process-boundary joint because the note states the optional-study rule as a general principle, while it openly hands the process boundary to the reader to choose. The two HOLDS joints are direct inferences from the note's own definitions and are included only to complete the ranking.

Outside the joints: the title and description match the body, but the filename (`evaluation-that-controls-adoption-helps-determine-the-change`) states a different claim from the title.

## For the human
Look first at line 12: decide whether "required" is anchored in written requirements, observed waits or the function a task serves, because the optional-study and process-boundary joints both depend on that choice.
