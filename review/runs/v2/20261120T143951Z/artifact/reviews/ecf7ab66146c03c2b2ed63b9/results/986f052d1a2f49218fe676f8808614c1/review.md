# Review

The title makes a contestable claim about what keeps a trial branch provisional in practice. An informed reader could argue that withholding approval until a later evaluation is enough. The note gives a reason to doubt that position. Later work can depend on the trial branch before any approval is given, making it costly to abandon. The title attributes a practical effect to backtracking, and the body confines that effect to cases where contrary evidence can lead the process back to a usable state and redirect the search.
