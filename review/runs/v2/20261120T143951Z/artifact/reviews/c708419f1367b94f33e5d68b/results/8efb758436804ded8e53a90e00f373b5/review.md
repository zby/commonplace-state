# Review

The title asserts that routing retention through reflection can change what a later lesson targets. Its main verb makes that architectural claim directly, and the subtitle spells out what “second-order” means in practice.

An informed reader could argue that targeted model editing already counts as revising a prior commitment because it changes the behavior that commitment governed. The note makes the criterion for second-order retention explicit. The system's later lesson must target the represented commitment itself, and rescoping requires a representation of its applicability boundary. The difference matters even when both pathways produce the same behavior.
