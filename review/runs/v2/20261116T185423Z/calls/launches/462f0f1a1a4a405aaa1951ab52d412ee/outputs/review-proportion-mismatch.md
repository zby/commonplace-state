# Review

The core claim is that live system use can supply an initial selection environment when no complete fixed oracle decides theory fit. The note develops it in two places: the opening portfolio of six consequence types, and "Why live use can select," which gives concrete mechanisms and the counterfactual-effect criterion. The secondary sections are each about as long as the main argument or shorter. "Initial selection is vulnerable to self-confirmation" bounds the claim and passes its detail to a linked note. "Why 'initial' matters" qualifies the claim. Scope and Open Questions are brief. No secondary section outgrows the load-bearing argument, and none of them needs to become its own note.
