# Review

## Summary

The candidate makes the contribution the commission asks for, rests on adequate evidence, and stays within its brief. One scope bullet narrows the claim more than the argument needs. This is a nonblocking change.

## Commission fit

The reader's task is deciding whether retained material supports an intended citation. The candidate opens with the commissioned example: a paraphrase put inside quotation marks. It states the limit as one of evidence ("The limit is one of evidence."), not as a claim that writers cannot make unsupported assertions. It then gives the two honest moves, narrowing the citation and returning to the source, which is where the commission says to stop.

Every must-keep item is present:

- A paraphrase preserves the idea but not the words.
- Quotation marks cannot recover the words.
- Claiming more precision or broader support requires going back for the missing evidence.
- A wording check verifies a match only under its comparison rule and against the text it actually compared. It does not certify the original author, the context or truth.
- Missing evidence is not a pass.

The paragraph about checks comes after the two moves. That ordering is acceptable, because the must-keep items require this content and the paragraph is short. The quote checker gets one illustrative sentence, which the commission prefers. That sentence does not teach the validator or survey checking techniques, and the candidate leaves out the storage design and its copyright history. The reserved decisions are respected: the title and path are unchanged, and the note is not split.

## Evidence

The authorized sources support each sourced claim:

- **ADR 073** supports the split between the two kinds of input. A bounded verbatim passage may suffice to judge a use. Other claims depend on "broad, distributed, or contextual material" that a few quotes cannot represent, and those need the full snapshot. The candidate's two moves restate this split for a general reader.
- **ADR 046** supports the checker illustration: the check covers `verbatim`-marked quotations only, compares them by substring after normalization against the linked source, and leaves paraphrase "unchecked by construction".

The rest of the body is reasoning, as the Scope section declares. The qualifier examples are covered by the info finding above.

## Contracts

- **Type contract:** the description discriminates, the main claim is near the top, and the limits sit under `## Scope`.
- **Collection contract:** the claim still stands after the Commonplace example is removed, so it holds as theory. Commonplace appears only as a scoped illustration.
- **Scope finding:** the out-of-scope exclusion of sources whose original is gone conflicts with the reach the argument supports (see the warn finding).

## Affected citations

The writer's report says no citation loses support, and that is accurate for all five inbound passages:

- **ADR 046** relies on the claim that a paraphrase claims no exact words and so is unchecked. The candidate keeps it ("has nothing to test in a paraphrase, which never claimed exact words").
- **ROME ingest** relies on the claim that a citation cannot be broader than its capture. The candidate keeps it ("A citation is supported only as far as the material it rests on reaches").
- **Claims-and-grounding tag head** relies on the claim that stronger notation cannot restore lost fidelity. The candidate keeps it ("Stronger notation is not a third move").
- **Atomic-step note** relies on the claim that a passage not captured word for word needs a return to the source before it can be quoted. The candidate keeps it in the "Return to the source" move.
- **Peters and Chin-Yee ingest** relies on the point about dropped scope qualifiers, which the candidate supports. The report correctly notes that the ingest's gloss calls a paraphrase "unverifiable", which is looser than what the candidate says. That is not a lost use.
