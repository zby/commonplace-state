# Commission: When a redesign can happen again

## Intended contribution
Help a maintainer tell whether a particular kind of change took effect and whether it can happen again from the state it produced. Let a reader apply the test to their own workflow after reading the examples.

## Must keep
Separate a real change used later from a proposed change, and a repeatable change in one part from general editability, inspectability, beneficial improvement, autonomy, or compounding. A later episode witnesses a transition; a specified mechanism can support a prospective conclusion within its assumptions. Preserve the six causal questions where sound. People can carry out some steps; expanding the observer's boundary by itself does not establish a retained route. Keep the Agno case bounded to target-side edits, not evidence about the surrounding platform. Preserve useful incoming uses; do not present a dependence on inspection as unchanged if it conflicts with the correction.

## Must exclude
No unsupported claim that ADR 028 informed ADR 057. No new external empirical claims. Distinguish invented illustrations from observed cases. Avoid substituting a list of system names or six abstract labels for a worked example.

## Reserved decisions
Do not edit other notes or rename the target. Operator approves the exact candidate before publication.

## Current direction
The operator approved the plan-stop rewrite and asked for more examples because the current note is extremely abstract. Lead with a concrete sequence and use contrasting examples where the verdict actually changes. Work the hypothetical overshoot circuit: an internal circuit halves a read-protected gain from 8 to 4 to 2; each later cycle uses the replacement. The observer can identify this relation from the design; the updater need not model or expose it. It establishes repeatability of gain adjustment only, not circuit redesign. Show in a familiar workflow how one change can be installed and used yet prevent the same kind of change next time (e.g. a deployed review rule disabling future rule changes). Work at least one real case within its evidence: the tag validator caught what the search missed; a later use of the new check is not a second redesign of the check. Bring distinctions in beside the steps they explain. Use literal, short wording; give the reader evidence to judge rather than merely announcing the verdict. Inspect the incoming citation passages for compatibility with the revised test. No dependent edits.
