# Review

## Summary

The article has six headed sections after an unheaded thesis introduction, and it carries more distinct non-obvious claims than that.

Distinct claims and necessary support units:

1. Deliberative learning (rejectable edits to a readable, addressable store) is a distinct learning paradigm, and an on-balance judgment can stand where a value function stands in weight training.
2. A worked example: a single edit traced through admission, trace-guided narrowing and revision of the review check. This is an inference-supporting unit that later sections refer back to ("as in the routing-line example above", "like our routing line").
3. The two-condition definition and its boundary cases (validators as the limiting case; click-ranking jobs, tabular RL tables and count-triggers fail the second condition).
4. The payoffs of a readable store and their costs, including legibility without faithfulness.
5. The historical bottleneck was what filled the value-function role: proof (Gödel machine), fixed formula (PRODIGY, single-loop with targeted correction) and changing heuristics (EURISKO, double-loop).
6. On-balance judgment defined as agreement across defensible value functions, with open verdicts handled by reversibility.
7. The bet: a fixed-weight language model can fill the judge slot for double-loop learning.
8. The Bitter Lesson selects production methods, not representational forms; the Sutton/Javed rival hypothesis and harness-evolution study are engaged; the bootstrap condition applies.
9. A "learned" verdict is a counterfactual on-balance test, normally open for a recent edit, with a two-part empirical check.
10. Current evidence status and four failure modes, with a falsification condition.

Each headed section maps to one or more of these: the example section to 2, the comparison section to 3–4, the precedents section to 5–7, the Bitter Lesson section to 8, the evidence section to 9–10, and "Where to go next" is navigation. No section only repeats framing from another. The info finding notes one short summarizing bridge, which is acceptable.
