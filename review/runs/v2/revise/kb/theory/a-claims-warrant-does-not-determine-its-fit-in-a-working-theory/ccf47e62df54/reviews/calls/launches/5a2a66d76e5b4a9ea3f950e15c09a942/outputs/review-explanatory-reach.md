# Review

The central claim is that a claim's warrant does not determine its fit in a working theory, and that apparent fit does not establish warrant. The mechanism is the different object each evaluation tests. Warrant tests the claim against an independent standard over its stated scope. Fit tests a relation between the claim and the rest of the theory. Because fit depends on the other claims, the same warranted claim can fit well in one theory and poorly in another. The note names the specific routes: duplication, a mismatch in abstraction level, scope not aligned with the decisions to guide, and irrelevance to the theory's purpose. In the reverse direction, a claim can appear to fit because neighbouring claims already assume it, so coherence is circular rather than independent support.

Variation test. Suppose fit were defined as a property of the claim alone, rather than a relation. Then redundancy and abstraction mismatch could no longer separate fit from warrant, and the central claim would weaken. The conclusion therefore depends on the relational premise. The mechanism is hard to vary.

Falsifiability. The claim would be refuted by showing that every warranted claim necessarily improves any theory it joins, or that smooth integration always tracks an independent warrant test. The duplicate-claim case already rules out the first.

Ad-hoc accommodation. The Scope entries do not protect the claim from counterexamples. The statement that warrant and fit need not be statistically independent narrows what is claimed to "determination", and the opening paragraph already uses that wording ("does not settle"). Leaving fit without a complete scalar definition is a stated limitation, not an escape hatch: the central argument does not depend on any particular fit metric.
