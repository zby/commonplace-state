# Review

## Summary

The note's core insight is that warrant and fit are separate evaluations, and it is compact. A one-paragraph rewrite could keep that thesis, but it would lose or compress several load-bearing elements:

- the concrete ways a warranted claim can fit poorly, such as redundancy, wrong abstraction level or mismatched scope;
- the mechanism behind spurious fit: neighbouring claims or the implementation already assume the claim, so coherence is not independent evidence;
- the inference that a contradiction with a warranted claim may require revising another part of the theory, which makes fit relational;
- the distinction between exchanging evidence, which is allowed, and substitution, which is not;
- the scope conditions, including broad warrant, an incomplete definition of fit, non-empirical commitments, and the absence of a statistical-independence claim.

Each of these is a boundary case, failure condition or inference rather than background. The section structure is already light, with only two headed sections after the lead. It is proportionate to the content.
