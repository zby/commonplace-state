# Review

The main claim is that independent warrant and fit in a working theory are separate evaluations, and neither determines the other. Most of the note supports that claim directly:

- The "warranted but poor fit" paragraph and the "apparent fit without warrant" paragraph give both directions of the non-determination.
- The "inform but not substitute" section is a boundary condition. It stops readers from taking the claim as a ban on evidence moving between the two evaluations.
- The Scope bullets are boundary conditions: the broad sense of warrant, the deliberately open definition of fit, the role of commitments and authority grants, and the point that the claim is not statistical independence. Each one narrows how the claim can be misread, and none adds a separate thesis.

The only real branch is the contradiction paragraph. It partly works as a consequence that strengthens the main claim, but it also starts a theory of repair under contradiction. It is short and does not hide the central point, so the finding is INFO rather than WARN.
