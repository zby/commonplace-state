# Review

## Summary

The note has three sections: an untitled lead, "The evaluations can inform but not substitute for each other", and "Scope". It makes at least four distinct non-obvious claims:

1. Independent warrant does not imply fit. A warranted claim can be irrelevant, redundant, at the wrong abstraction level or wrongly scoped for the working theory.
2. Apparent fit does not imply warrant. Coherence around a claim may come from neighbours or an implementation that already assume it, so integration is not independent warrant.
3. A contradiction between a warranted claim and the theory may call for repairing another part of the theory, so fit is relational.
4. The two evaluations may exchange evidence, but neither may substitute for the other.

The second section carries claim 4 and the inference that separates informing from substituting. The Scope section carries boundary conditions: the broad sense of warrant, the deliberately incomplete definition of fit, non-empirical commitments, and the point that the claim is not one of statistical independence. These conditions qualify the main claim and are not restated framing. With four claims and three sections, the note does not have more sections than claims.
