# Review

## Summary

No detail in the note has outgrown its argumentative role. The note is short, and each supporting detail is about one sentence or a compact list.

## Assessment

- The list of fit failures in the second paragraph ("irrelevant", "duplicate", "wrong abstraction level", mismatched scope, maintenance-only detail) fits in one sentence. Each item names a distinct way a warranted claim can fit poorly. That gives the abstract claim concrete content without a worked example, so it is proportionate.
- The paragraph on apparent fit gives a three-sentence mechanism: a claim already assumed by neighbouring claims, local usefulness that encodes a misconception, and coherence that is not independent evidence. This mechanism is the support route for the second direction of the thesis, so it should not be cut further.
- The paragraph on contradiction lists four alternative repairs. This is an extension of the claim more than illustrative detail. Its length matches its role, so detail overhang does not apply.
- In "The evaluations can inform but not substitute for each other", each paragraph has one job: the first shows how evidence can pass between the two evaluations, and the second states the prohibition. Each needs its sentences to stop the distinction being read as a claim that the two evaluations are isolated.
- The Scope bullets are one or two sentences each. They draw boundaries (broad warrant, no scalar fit, normative inputs, no statistical-independence claim) and do not elaborate. The bullet on objectives and commitments is the most peripheral, but it is already one sentence.

Nothing should be compressed in place.
