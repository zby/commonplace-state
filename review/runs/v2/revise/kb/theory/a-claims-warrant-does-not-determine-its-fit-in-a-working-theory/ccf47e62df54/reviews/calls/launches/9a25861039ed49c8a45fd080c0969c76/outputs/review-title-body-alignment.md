# Review

## Summary

The body supports the title's claim. It argues that a warranted claim may fit poorly because it can be irrelevant, redundant, at the wrong abstraction level or wrongly scoped. It argues that contradiction with a warranted claim may call for revising the theory rather than rejecting the claim.

The body establishes more than the title promises, however. A full paragraph argues the converse: apparent fit does not imply warrant, because a false or weakly supported claim may integrate smoothly when neighbouring claims already assume it. The section "The evaluations can inform but not substitute for each other" frames the prohibition symmetrically. The Scope section states the note's claim as "neither evaluation determines the other." A reader linking to the title would expect only the warrant-to-fit direction and would miss the fit-to-warrant half. That half carries the note's warning about treating system coherence as evidence of truth.

A title that matches the body would state the bidirectional claim. For example: "A claim's warrant and its fit in a working theory do not determine each other." Alternatively, the body could be cut back to the one direction the title names.
