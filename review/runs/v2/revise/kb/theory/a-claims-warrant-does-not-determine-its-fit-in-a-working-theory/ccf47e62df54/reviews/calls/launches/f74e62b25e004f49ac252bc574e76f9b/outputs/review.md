# Critique: A claim's warrant does not determine its fit in a working theory

**Note:** kb/theory/a-claims-warrant-does-not-determine-its-fit-in-a-working-theory.md
**Central commitment:** Whether a claim is warranted and whether it fits a working theory are two evaluations with different questions and failure conditions; each may supply evidence to the other, but neither may substitute for the other.
**Critique mode:** claim
**Attack outcome:** partially lands

## Strongest case against it

The opponent is a confirmation holist joined with a pragmatist/Popperian view of corroboration. Quine, Duhem and coherentist epistemologists such as BonJour hold this view, and the KB's own Popperian stance gives it partial support.

1. **No independent warrant for empirical claims.** On Quine-Duhem holism, a single empirical claim never meets evidence alone. It is tested together with auxiliary assumptions, so its warrant is a function of how it coheres with the rest of what is believed. Coherence is the note's own description of fit, so "warrant" collapses into a wider kind of fit. The note concedes much of this when it says contradiction may reveal "a defect in another claim, a scope boundary, a bad decomposition, or a larger theory that needs revision". That is the Duhem point. If blame can always be reassigned within the theory, the claim has no warrant that is assessed apart from the theory.

2. **Use is corroboration.** Under Popper, a claim earns standing by surviving severe tests, and a working system exposes its claims to many demands. The note grants that "A claim that repeatedly fails to predict, guide recovery, transfer, or survive later demands may deserve renewed factual or formal scrutiny". Conservation of expected evidence then requires that surviving those same demands raises warrant. So the prohibition that "This claim helped the system" cannot become evidence of truth is either false or empty: use that could have refuted the claim and did not is evidence for it.

Together these points say the distinction is not between two evaluations. At most it is a gradient within one coherence-and-corroboration evaluation, and the "prohibition on substitution" forbids an inference that is often valid.

## How the note engages it

**Partially engaged.**

- On point 2, the note has the materials for an answer. It does not say that use is never evidence of warrant. It says that coherence is "evidence about integration without being independent evidence that the claim is warranted". It also names the mechanism that makes use non-independent: the implementation, vocabulary or neighbouring claims "already assume" the claim. That is the correct Popperian reply, because a test shaped by its hypothesis is not severe. The word "silently" in the prohibition also narrows it to unacknowledged transfer. But the note never states the rule that separates allowed exchange from forbidden substitution. As written, it can be read as an asymmetry: failure in use counts against warrant, but success does not count for it. The opponent's symmetry argument hits that reading directly.
- On point 1, the note does not engage. It speaks of "independent warrant" in the description and in the second paragraph, but never says what warrant is independent of. Holism is compatible with the note only when "working theory" means a purpose-relative, operative subset whose fit is judged by relevance, non-redundancy, abstraction level and usefulness, and when warrant is judged against the wider evidence base. The scope section ("Fit is intentionally not given a complete scalar definition") and the linked note on system use imply this reading, but neither states it. A holist reading the relational-fit paragraph will find support rather than an answer.

The title claim, that warrant does not determine fit, survives both points. The holist cannot claim that a well-supported fact is automatically worth keeping in a given purpose-specific theory. The redundancy, wrong-abstraction and maintenance-cost examples in the second paragraph settle that direction. What the attack weakens is the converse, that fit does not deliver warrant, and the word "independent" on which that converse depends.

## Constructive findings

- State what "independent" means: warrant assessed against evidence and standards that the working theory under evaluation did not shape. This is not a claim that the assessment is free of all background belief. One sentence would turn the holist objection from a refutation into a refinement the note already accepts.
- Replace the implied asymmetry with an explicit rule for exchange. Success in use counts toward warrant to the degree that the demand could have exposed the claim and was not shaped by assuming it. Counting it beyond that degree is substitution. This keeps the Popperian corroboration point and keeps the self-confirmation warning in the third paragraph.
- Connect the contradiction paragraph to the distinction. Say that reassigning blame within the theory is a fit operation, and that it does not by itself change the warrant of the claim that triggered the reassignment. At present that paragraph reads as a concession to holism, not as an application of the distinction.

## Secondary objections (optional)

- The scope bullet on objectives, commitments and grants of authority does not say whether these items have warrant in the broad sense the note defines, or whether they bypass warrant and act only on fit.
- The note links only to the system-use note. That note in turn relies on a sibling, `system-use-provides-evidence-of-theory-fit-not-independent-warrant.md`, which covers the evidential-exchange point. The present note does not link to it, so a reader of this note cannot find the more detailed treatment of the symmetry objection.
