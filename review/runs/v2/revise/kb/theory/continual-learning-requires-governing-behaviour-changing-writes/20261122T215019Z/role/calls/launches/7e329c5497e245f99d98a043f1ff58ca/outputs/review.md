# Role in the KB: Continual learning requires governing behaviour-changing writes, not just storing content

## Role

The note is the KB's anchor for what counts as continual learning in a deployed system: persistence is insufficient, and a write must be selected, validated, authorized and coordinated by the system's own evidence-responsive loop. Its dependents are almost all source ingests that cite it as the claim an external system evidences, illustrates or departs from, most often for "persistence is not learning", "non-weight writes count", or "writes need regression validation". No visible dependent argues from it as a premise in a further theoretical argument; 18 of 48 linking files were cut by the cap and are unseen.
Relied on: persistence/storage is insufficient; governance list (choose, validate, authorize, coordinate); validation against regression; non-weight representational forms count and a weight-only updater is limited; behavioural authority on a consumption path and the knowledge-artifact versus system-definition-artifact distinction; the system-selected, evidence-responsive discriminator; the broad durability criterion for learning; form differences in installation cost and verifiability; codification as a form move. Unused by visible dependents: the cheap-online-installation constraint (lessons taking effect before retraining); "it need not touch all three every time"; "mature release engineering already governs hand-authored readable changes"; the sentence that storage, contextual activation and parametric adaptation remain subproblems (possibly used by the cut llm-agents-not-faithful passage).
