# Review

## Summary

The note has an untitled opening and three headed sections. It makes considerably more distinct non-obvious claims than that:

1. Because the exact-spec/proxy-theory regime cannot be identified before scale tests it, practical systems are hybrids, and the split is not codified versus learned.
2. Code extraction's generality cost depends on the regime: none within declared scope for arithmetic-regime decompositions, possible loss at larger scope for vision-feature decompositions.
3. Relaxing is the complement of extraction, and it reverses extraction rather than symbolic membership.
4. The trajectory runs in both directions and recurs at different stack levels (edge detection, FlashAttention, tokenizers).
5. Every code-extraction codification is a bet that the extracted regularity is load-bearing, which is the author-side view of the unsupported-scope diagnosis.
6. Spec mining narrows the bet from composition to load-bearingness, because it starts from a working system.
7. Relaxing signals detect a losing bet.
8. Epiplexity and the induction-bias results bound when bets are safe; the second suggests arithmetic-regime bets persist under scaling.
9. Three working heuristics: codify for leverage rather than permanence, prefer what-specs over how-specs, and treat composition failure as a relaxing signal.

Each section adds new substance. "The trade-off depends on which regime you're in" sets up the regime distinction and relaxing. "Every code-extraction codification is a bet" develops the bet framing, spec mining and the formal bounds. "Working heuristics" turns these into decision rules. No section only restates framing from another, so sections do not outnumber claims.
