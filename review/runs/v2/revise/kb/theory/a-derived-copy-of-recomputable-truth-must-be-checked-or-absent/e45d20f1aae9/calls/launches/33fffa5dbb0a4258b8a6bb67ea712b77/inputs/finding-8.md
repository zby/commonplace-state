# Review

The note's central framework is a dichotomy: a derived copy of recomputable truth is either **checked** (validator re-derives and fails on mismatch) or **absent** (deleted or replaced by a live read). It also enumerates four preconditions for checking and four instance surfaces.

## Boundary cases tested against the dichotomy

- **Simplest instance — a byte-identical duplicated file.** Maps cleanly to "checked" (one comparison) or "absent".
- **Hand-maintained copy explicitly marked untrusted (partial, dated, or "may be stale").** The opening claims exactly two valid states, but the very next sentence locates the forbidden middle only where the copy is "maintained by hand and trusted by consumers". The asymmetry argument turns on trust suppressing recomputation; an explicitly untrusted copy does not suppress it. The linked compiled-views note also admits "marked stale" as an acceptable response. This case falls outside the two states without being forbidden by the note's own mechanism — the WARN.
- **Copy regenerated by a build step.** The note classifies ADR 025's build-time listings as deletion. The copy still exists for consumers; it is generated, not checked and not read live. Coverage is possible under a generous reading of "absent", hence INFO.
- **Value produced by execution (precondition 3).** The note explicitly excludes this from the rule's domain, so it is handled.
- **Judgment-derived summaries.** The note routes these out of the rule through precondition 1 and the managed-staleness regime, so the boundary is stated.

The four-item preconditions list and the four-instance list are proportionate to their argumentative role; the fix is to clarify the dichotomy's scope (trusted copies) or name the untrusted/regenerated cases, not to add more categories.
