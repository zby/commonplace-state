# Friction check: A fixed review rule can use lessons from new incidents only if it can identify when they apply

**Central claim (one sentence):** A standing instruction such as "check past incidents" can stay fixed while incidents accumulate, but a review benefits from a new incident only when the reviewer recognizes the earlier failure's operative feature in a case that shares no surface identifiers with it.

## Filter
**Filter:** SURVIVES

The claim wants three things at once: the rule stays fixed, new lessons get used, and identifying when they apply is necessary. These can coexist when identification is done by the reviewer applying the rule. They cannot all hold if the rule itself must do the identifying, because a fixed rule cannot name the features of incidents that have not happened yet. The title uses the second reading ("it can identify"). The body uses the first. The claim survives only on the body's reading. The title-level mismatch is reported as the second joint, not as a dissolution.

## Thinnest joints

The weakest joint is the step from "the instruction only says where to look" to "a reviewer following it could still recognize the lesson." The note's conclusion that the rule need not change depends on that recognition. The note stipulates the recognition instead of explaining where it comes from. The title joint is next. It assigns identification to the rule, while the body denies that the rule identifies anything. That mismatch makes the 'only if' either false of the example or close to definitional. The closing generalization ranks third because it rests on one stipulated success and does not consider failed recognition. The last two joints hold. They follow from the instruction's wording and from the example's own construction.

## For the human

Look first at "A reviewer following the original instruction could still recognize it." Say what lets the reviewer pick out the operative feature, or state that recognition is assumed. Then make the title's "it" agree with that answer.
