# Review

## Core claim

Stated in one sentence from the note's own commitments: a fixed instruction to consult past incidents can absorb lessons from new incidents without being rewritten, provided the reviewer can recognize which feature of an earlier failure bears on the current page; the incident teaches a distinction, not a rule change.

## Prominence

The title states the claim as a conditional. The first paragraph restates it plainly in three short sentences: the rule can stay fixed, it says where to look, and it does not say which failure applies. Nothing precedes the claim. There is no terminology block, analogy stack or related-note apparatus.

## Subordination of later material

The second paragraph is one concrete example (shared-origin citations offered as independent support). It shows why lookup by surface feature fails and recognition succeeds. The third paragraph turns the example into a test and closes by restating the claim: "The incident shows what the review must distinguish, not that the review rule must change." Every paragraph serves the core claim, and the note is short enough that nothing competes with it.

## Possible revision target (not a finding)

The closing sentence is the sharpest form of the claim. An author could lift its contrast, "a distinction to recognize, not a rule to change", into the opening paragraph so that the first and last statements match. This would only make the claim more memorable. The current shape already makes the claim easy to find.
