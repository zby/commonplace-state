# Review

## Summary

The central assertion is the title. Its pronoun "it" can refer to the rule or to the review applying the rule, and the two readings have opposite consequences: the rule must change as lessons accumulate, or the rule can stay fixed. The body's example and closing sentence favour the reviewer reading. They do not settle what the title asserts, because the title grammatically assigns identification to the rule. Other assertions pass. The example's condition for when the lesson applies (shared origin plus reliance on independence) is stated precisely.
