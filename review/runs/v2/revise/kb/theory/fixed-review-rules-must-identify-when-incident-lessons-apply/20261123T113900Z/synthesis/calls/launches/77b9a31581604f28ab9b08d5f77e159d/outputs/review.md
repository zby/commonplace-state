## Opportunities

### 1. Align who identifies applicability: rule or reviewer

- Location: the title, "A fixed review rule can use lessons from new incidents only if it can identify when they apply", read against paragraph 2, "A reviewer following the original instruction could still recognize it", and paragraph 3, "The incident shows what the review must distinguish, not that the review rule must change." The description, "leaves the reviewer to determine which feature of an earlier failure matters", supports the body's version.
- Reader problem: the title reads as a requirement on the rule: the rule must identify when a lesson applies. The body says the rule only tells the reviewer where to look and does not need to change. The reviewer does the identifying. A reader who carries the title into the body could take the closing sentence as contradicting it, and so miss the note's distinctive point: a new incident updates what the reviewer must distinguish, not the rule's text.
- Direction: make the title, the description and the closing sentence name the same agent. One possible resolution, if the body states the intended claim: "A fixed review rule can absorb new incident lessons when the reviewer can identify which feature of a past failure applies." The core-claim review suggested moving the closing contrast, "a distinction to recognize, not a rule to change", into the opening. That would then reinforce the same reading from both ends.
- Combines: cross-cutting. It extends the core-claim review's non-finding revision target, which proposed matching the opening and closing statements. None of the four checks raised a finding.
- Preserve or decide: the author must choose between two materially different theses before changing the wording. One is that the rule must encode applicability conditions to carry new lessons. The other is that the rule can stay fixed because applicability is left to the reviewer's recognition. The body supports the second, and the title and file name, "fixed-review-rules-must-identify", lean toward the first. Keep the conditional "only if": the lesson transfers only when recognition succeeds. Also keep the example's contrast between source-name lookup and recognition of a shared origin.
