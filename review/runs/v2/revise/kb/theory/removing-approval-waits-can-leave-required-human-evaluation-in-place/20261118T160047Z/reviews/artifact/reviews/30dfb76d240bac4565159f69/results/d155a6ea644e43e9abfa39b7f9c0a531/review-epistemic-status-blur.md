# Review

## Summary

The note is mainly a conceptual distinction followed by a recommendation derived from it. Its material claims are:

- Definitional distinctions: an actual wait versus a specified task versus optional assessment. They are presented as the note's distinction ("This distinguishes..."), which is appropriate for a qualitative decomposition the note builds itself.
- An existence claim ("can leave"), supported by constructed cases. Those cases are labelled "hypothetical", and the weekly-review example states its own epistemic limit: "This example establishes a gap against the specification, not that skipping review causes harm."
- A normative consequence ("A claim of full automation must say which tasks it preserves..."). It follows directly from the distinction and reads as a derived recommendation, not as an empirical finding.
- One inherited claim, that routine approvals do not establish dispensability. It is attributed by link.

The note makes no quantitative or empirical-sounding claim without evidence. It keeps usefulness and necessity apart from requirement status ("Whether a specified task is useful or necessary is a separate question"), so support for the distinction is not presented as support for claims about harm or value. The "can" modals in the Scope section fit the support given. No well-supported finding is hedged without reason.
