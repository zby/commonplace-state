# Review

## Summary

The note's main coined term, "resolution-switching", is defined in the opening
paragraph and recurs throughout. It is the subject of the note rather than
borrowed KB jargon with a plain-language equivalent. Most later uses also
restate what it means locally: "movement among the views a task needs" in the
criterion section, "helps a reader assemble those views" in the discovery
section, and "whether useful views at different scopes exist and whether the
reader can move among them" in the boundaries section. A reader does not need
to scroll back to follow these sentences.

The other KB-flavoured terms are used briefly and grounded where they appear.
"Addressability grain" is introduced and glossed in the same sentence. "Grain"
is used only once more, immediately afterwards. The "cost gradient" from
progressive disclosure is defined in the paragraph that uses it again.
"Resolution-switching fluidity" is defined in the sentence that introduces it,
and later uses of "fluidity" restate what it is relative to. No term loses its
grounding through repeated unexplained reuse.
