# Review

## Summary

The note's strongest claim, using only its own commitments, is this: a quote check's pass certifies only that the span occurs, under a stated normalization rule, in the evidence the check actually used. Fidelity to the original, attribution, context and truth stay unverified unless separate checks establish them. The title compresses that claim well.

The body does not lead with it. The opening paragraph explains what an exact-quotation marker asserts and the one relation a quote check tests. Next come the four-question taxonomy, then normalization and occurrence. Only in "Which evidence a pass is relative to" does the note say plainly that a pass is relative to the compared evidence and that the binding stops at the capture. The description frames the note as a general survey of quote checking. That matches the body's order better than the title does, so the title's claim competes with sections that have the same weight.

The material itself is defensible and mostly serves the claim: normalization and occurrence are further ways a pass can say less than it seems to. Its order and framing hide that role. The simplest fix is to state in the opening that a pass is relative to the evidence used and that the capture-to-original link is unchecked. The existing sections can then read as elaborations of that limit.

A possible revision target, separate from this verdict: the note could unify all four sections under one claim, that a pass is relative to its rule, its occurrence constraint and its evidence. The current text implies this framing but does not state it.
