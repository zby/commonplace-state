# Review

## Summary

The core claim is that a citation's fidelity is bounded by the capture layer holding the passage, and that no downstream notation can raise it; only re-capture can. Two sections carry that claim: "Capture is layered because it is forced to be" establishes the layers and why they are unavoidable, and "Why notation cannot repair it" gives the mechanism. The second rests on the derived-copy rule: a paraphrase has no mechanical derivation rule, so a `verbatim` mark creates an unchecked trusted copy. Each section has a substantive paragraph, and the first also has a three-item enumeration. Together they are the largest part of the body.

The secondary material is proportionate. The fidelity-versus-authority distinction in the opening is one paragraph and prevents a likely confusion with a neighbouring note. The corollary on locators and the Scope section are one and two short paragraphs. The open questions are two bullets. No secondary section outgrows the load-bearing argument, and none is a candidate for splitting into its own note.
