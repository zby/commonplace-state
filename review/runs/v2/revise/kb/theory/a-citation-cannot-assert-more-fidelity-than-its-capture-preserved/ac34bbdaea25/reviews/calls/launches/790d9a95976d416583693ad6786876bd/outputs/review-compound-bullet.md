# Review

## Summary

No bullet packs two separable claims; the longer bullets each develop one idea with supporting detail.

The bullets over about 30 words are the three capture-layer bullets. Each one defines a layer and then states what that layer permits a citation to do. The Paraphrase layer bullet, for example, says a claim "may cite it, but must not use quotation marks" and that verification requires the authoritative artifact. Both rules follow from the layer's definition, so they elaborate a single point: what the paraphrase layer is and how it may be cited. Splitting them would separate each layer from its citation consequence, which is what the list exists to pair. The Second-hand bullet has the same shape: a definition, an example, and the rule to name the intermediary.

The Open Questions bullets each pose one question with a short motivating clause. The Relevant Notes bullets are a link plus, at most, a short gloss.
