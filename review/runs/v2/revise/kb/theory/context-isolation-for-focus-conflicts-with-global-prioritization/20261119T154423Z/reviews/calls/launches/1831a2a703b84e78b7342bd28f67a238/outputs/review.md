# Premise decomposition: Context isolation for focus conflicts with global prioritization

**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** Under bounded effective context, a competing workload whose comparison-sufficient live summary cannot coexist with deep task context without degrading focus, and open-ended trade-offs lacking a locally calibrated common scale, isolating an agent's context for task focus necessarily conflicts with relational global prioritization because it removes the live comparison set that global prioritization requires.

## Premises and counterexamples

The argument consumes two groups of load-bearing premises: four that establish the core conflict under its stated universal conditions, and six that delineate its local-judgment boundary and its proposed narrow-deep / wide-shallow architectural split.

In the core conflict group, three premises hold under their stated conditions: per-window feasibility pushes toward excluding competing-work summaries when co-loading degrades focus; a stateless, isolated worker has no live comparison set outside its window; and static priority rules cannot track a changing workload (and the separate mechanism splitting indiscrimination from failure direction also holds). The single **global** failure sits on the fourth core premise—the claim in Condition 3 and Section 1 that compact injected cutoffs work only when a locally calibrated numeric scale exists, leaving open-ended trade-offs with no alternative to a global view comparing concrete tasks side by side. A concrete marginal exemplar (or exemplar-anchored ordinal rubric plus a live cutoff) delivers concrete side-by-side comparison in O(1) tokens without any numeric scale, falsifying the universal claim under its stated conditions unless Condition 3 is rescoped to regimes where marginal anchors themselves break down (concurrent multi-worker congestion, multi-dimensional Pareto frontiers, cross-task synergies, or near-margin comparisons).

The remaining five non-holding findings are **local** to the note's task-internal boundary and Section 2 split: one defeats the claim that manifestation timing is task-internal, and four dent the narrow-deep / wide-shallow interface (observer-relativity of scale-bearing facts, unstated tangent-recognition triggers, pre-filtering circularity and deep-feasibility dependence in wide-shallow triage, and coupling of judgment-bearing stop rules back to operator queue congestion). Those local dents do not refute the core conflict; they show that the architectural split leaves more of the conflict unresolved at the interface than Section 2's mechanism claims.

## For the human

Start with the global defeater in Finding 1 (line 26 / Condition 3): concrete marginal-exemplar injection lets an isolated worker do concrete side-by-side calibration in O(1) tokens without a numeric common scale, so the universal claim's third condition needs rescoping to cases where compact marginal anchors fail (such as multi-worker congestion, multi-dimensional trade-offs, or near-margin comparisons).
