# Premise decomposition: Context isolation for focus conflicts with global prioritization

**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** Under bounded effective context, a competing workload whose comparison-sufficient live summary degrades focus if co-loaded, and open-ended trade-offs lacking a locally calibrated common scale, isolating an agent's context for deep task focus universally prevents reliable relational global prioritization and forces a split between narrow-deep execution and wide-shallow triage across separate bounded calls.

## Premises and counterexamples

The commitment decomposes into three load-bearing layers:

1. **The conflict core (context pressure, epistemic blindness, and impossibility of local substitutes).** To establish a genuine universal conflict rather than a mere engineering omission, the argument consumes five premises: bounded-window feasibility pushes hard tasks toward context isolation; stateless isolated workers carry no live comparison set outside their window; zero live signal of competing work prevents matching decisions that flip with competing work (unstated information-theoretic core); static priority instructions cannot supply live relative scale across a changing workload; and compact injected live cost signals fail without a locally calibrated numeric scale. The first four hold (alongside the note's separate causal split between indiscrimination and default-driven failure direction), while the fifth fails globally via concrete marginal-anchor injection—a ~50-token concrete exemplar of the marginal displaced task provides concrete side-by-side comparison without any numeric scale. An additional unstated applicability premise—that a comparison-sufficient summary is actually large enough to degrade focus in ordinary multi-agent coding workloads rather than being absent due to session siloing or concurrent staleness—is doubtful on prevalence grounds.
2. **The architectural consequence (mandatory split into separate bounded calls).** Moving from the conflict to the claim that execution and triage cannot share one bounded call consumes the premise that any focus degradation from co-loading makes a shared call unworkable. Because soft degradation can stay within task feasibility while avoiding larger compression errors across a split interface, that binary premise fails locally.
3. **The split's interface mechanics (producer-side local reporting, consumer-side shallow triage, and unbundled stopping).** Making the narrow-deep / wide-shallow split operate consumes three premises: that isolated workers can identify "scale-bearing local facts" purely from task-internal scope; that wide-shallow triage can rank competing items from compressed summaries without deep context; and that diminishing-returns stopping cleanly belongs to triage while workers handle local hard-blocker stops. The first fails locally on observer-relative cross-boundary salience, and the other two are dented locally by non-decomposable cross-item interactions, tractability estimation, and mid-run tangent loops that never trigger hard blockers.

## For the human

Look first at Finding 1 (`GLOBAL`, `DEFEATED`, `instance`): concrete marginal-anchor injection defeats Condition 3's claim that compact injected cutoffs require a locally calibrated numeric scale, so the universal commitment must be rescoped around what defeats marginal anchors too (mid-run queue drift, hidden cross-boundary stakes, salience asymmetry, or multi-resource trade-offs).
