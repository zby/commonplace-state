# Assessment: A claim's test record does not determine its fit in a working theory

**Purpose (as given):** Judge whether the rewritten note states good theory, and improve its text where the reviews find real defects.

## Verdict on the theory

The central thesis is good theory and survives every review: truth, test record and fit are separate standings, a good test record does not make a claim fit, and fit counts toward the test record only where the claim could have failed. The existential witnesses (a well-tested claim that fits poorly; a false claim that fits smoothly) and the logical point about contradiction repair all HOLD under premise decomposition and critique. The defects are local: one definition contradicts the note's own operative criterion, one boundary case (formal claims) breaks the truth/test-record section, the Popper §82 grounding is adapted without saying so, and a few sentences overstate. No depth 1 report proposes a split, merge, different claim or rejection: composition friction says SURVIVES, premise decomposition routes every failure LOCAL, and the critique's attack "partially lands" only on the independence definition and on background relativity. The synthesis offers moving the contradiction section out as one of two options but does not choose; that is a question for the operator below, not a stop. No rewrite is needed, so the plan proceeds with passes.

## What reads well

The three-question frame followed by one section per separation is clear, and its examples (irrelevance, duplication, wrong level, maintenance cost; vocabulary that bakes in a misconception) make each separation checkable.

## Sentence a hostile reviewer would attack first

"A test is independent when its outcome could have gone against the claim and does not presuppose the working theory." Under Duhem–Quine holism every test presupposes retained auxiliaries, so read literally the test-record category is empty; and the note's own later rule ("What decides is whether the use could have gone against the claim, not where the evidence was produced") contradicts it. Five reviews converge here (internal-consistency, underspecified-assertions, framing-mismatch, composition friction, critique, premise decomposition).

## Ranked changes

1. **Align the independence definition with the could-have-failed criterion.** Narrow "does not presuppose the working theory" to what the reviews converge on: the test does not presuppose the claim under test, or an auxiliary whose acceptance would make the outcome automatic. Then make the "What decides" sentence consistent with it. The frontmatter description and the role report both treat could-have-failed as the operative criterion, and the role report lists the presupposition clause as used by no dependent, so this changes no claim a dependent relies on. Cost: two sentences. Pass: `narrow-overclaims` (fixes findings 5, 6, 7).
2. **Scope the truth/test-record separation for formal claims.** The note admits derivation as a test, then says every surviving claim is still a conjecture and truth of a universal claim is never established; a proved theorem is a counterexample. Say what a derivation settles (truth relative to its premises, fallible only in the proof's correctness) or scope the conjecture claim to empirical and source-derived claims. The role report shows no dependent uses the truth question or the kinds of test. Cost: one clause in the opening bullet and one in "A test record is not truth". Pass: `fix-strategy-taxonomy` on finding 3.
3. **Mark the §82 adaptation.** Popper's condition is a sincere attempt to refute (an intent condition he says cannot be formalized); the note's is could-have-failed (an outcome condition). State that the note adapts Popper's condition rather than restating it, and cite the layer the ingest finds it in (§82 starred note *1, Appendix *ix). Cost: one sentence. Pass: `fix-strategy-taxonomy` on finding 4.
4. **Narrow three overstatements in "The two kinds of evidence inform each other" and its slogan.** (a) "has earned a new independent test": separate failing to yield a useful prediction (a fit problem that warrants a new test) from yielding a wrong one (a possible refutation, subject to the contradiction section), and drop "earned" (finding 2). (b) "may require restructuring … force a change of fit": surviving a test forces restructuring only when the claim contradicts retained claims, and the contradiction section leaves the repair to a decision; narrow to "may call for" or name the contradiction route. (c) "'This claim is well tested' is not a reason to retain it in this theory" denies what the Scope concedes; narrow to "not by itself a reason". Cost: three sentence edits. Pass: `narrow-overclaims`, same run as item 1.
5. **Remove the terminology-history paragraph and the redundant restatements.** The "warrant" paragraph is revision history (flagged by all four compression checks). The role report shows the evaluation tag head and three source ingests still say "warrant", so keep a bridge clause in the test-record bullet rather than deleting outright. Cut the trailing "It is not a guarantee that later tests will go the same way." State the could-have-failed condition once in "Fit is not an independent test", keeping the contrast "not where the evidence was produced"; keep the section 4 slogan's condition as a decision rule. Cost: one paragraph removed, one clause added, one paragraph folded. Pass: `readability-and-flow` (fixes finding 1).

## Overclaim candidates: support or divergence

Divergence (defend; passes may not narrow):

- The title: "A claim's test record does not determine its fit in a working theory".
- "Three things can be asked about a claim in it, and no answer to one settles another."
- "A well-tested claim may fit poorly."
- "A false, overgeneral or weakly tested claim may fit smoothly, because the current implementation, the vocabulary or the neighbouring claims already assume it."
- "Neither may be substituted for the other."
- "Which repair to make is a decision, taken by comparing the test records of the claims involved and what each repair does to the theory's fit to its problem."

Support (may be narrowed): the independence definition; "What decides is whether the use could have gone against the claim, not where the evidence was produced"; "Truth does not change with time, and for a universal claim it is never established"; "A claim that has survived every test so far is still a conjecture"; "has earned a new independent test"; "a claim that survives a severe test may require restructuring the working theory"; "'This claim is well tested' is not a reason to retain it in this theory".

## Claim questions and stale qualifications for the operator (not passes)

- **"Warrant" in dependents.** `kb/tags/evaluation-README.md` (its description names "warrant versus theory fit") and the ATMS, formal-learning-theory and AGM ingests still use "warrant" for what this note now calls test record. Updating them is dependent work the passes cannot do; the readability pass keeps only a bridge clause meanwhile. `kb/tags/theory-builder-README.md` labels two questions where the note names three.
- **Failed tests have no place in the test record.** The record holds only survivals, so an accepted falsification, which does bear on truth, is outside all three standings (premise decomposition, friction HOLDS residual). Add failed tests to the record, or say that only survival leaves truth open. Claim change; operator decides.
- **Background relativity of the test record.** The critique and premise decomposition argue that a test record is relative to the background held fixed, so revising fit can re-read past tests; the note should say this is coupling, not settlement. Adds content; operator decides.
- **"Truth does not change with time"** fails for claims about a live, self-modified system unless claims are read as time-indexed propositions. Operator decides whether to state that reading.
- **Agreement conjunct in the fit definition** conflicts with the contradiction case where a contradicting claim would improve the theory after restructuring; say whether the conjuncts are indicators or fit is measured against the current theory.
- **Role of the contradiction section** (synthesis item 2): link it explicitly as a consequence (repair is a fit decision the test record does not settle) or move it to its own note. Not decided here; the passes leave it in place.
- **Untaken recommendations from linking ingests** (role report): formal-learning-theory (convergence does not warrant the current output) and AGM (formal admissibility separate from warrant, with citation). Additions of claims; operator decides.
- **Fit as a property of the claim–theory pair** (critique secondary): the opening calls all three "things asked about a claim"; one clause would fix it. Operator decides.
