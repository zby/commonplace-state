# Premise decomposition: A claim's test record does not determine its fit in a working theory

**Register:** claim
**Central commitment (one sentence):** A claim in a working theory has three separate standings: truth, test record and fit. No answer about one settles another, and agreement with the working theory counts toward the test record only where the claim could have failed.

## Premises and counterexamples

The commitment has three parts, and each part consumes different premises. The separation part relies on definitions of each standing. Each definition must be stated so that the other two answers do not follow from it: the timelessness of truth, the composition and independence condition of the test record, and the conjunctive definition of fit. The two non-entailment parts rely on existential premises: a well-tested claim can fit poorly, and a false claim can fit smoothly. The could-have-failed clause relies on the §82 grounding and on how the note restates it. The contradiction section relies on the logical point that a contradiction does not select a repair.

The existential and logical premises hold. The doubts sit in the definitions. A derivation is admitted as a kind of test, yet the note says truth is never established. Falsification has no place in the record because the record holds only survivals. The independence condition is relative to the working theory, which ties the test record to fit. The fit definition has a conjunct that a claim worth restructuring for would fail. Two of the note's sentences deny what its Scope concedes, or turn Popper's intent condition into an outcome condition.

Each failure can be repaired by narrowing a premise. None reaches the non-entailment thesis, which survives on its existential witnesses. So every routing is LOCAL.

## For the human

Look first at the derivation case (`a derivation for a formal one`). Admitting proofs as tests while saying truth is never established is the one checkable defeat of the separation thesis. The fix is to scope the separation to non-formal claims, or to say what a derivation does settle.
