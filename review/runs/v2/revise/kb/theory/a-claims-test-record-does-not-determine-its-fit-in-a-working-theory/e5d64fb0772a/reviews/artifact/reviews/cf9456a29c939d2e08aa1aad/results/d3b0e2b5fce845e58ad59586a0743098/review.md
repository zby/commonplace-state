# Critique: A claim's test record does not determine its fit in a working theory

**Note:** kb/theory/a-claims-test-record-does-not-determine-its-fit-in-a-working-theory.md
**Central commitment:** A claim's truth, its test record (the independent tests it has survived so far) and its fit in a working theory are three separate standings, and no answer to one settles another. Agreement with the theory counts as a test only where the claim could have failed.
**Critique mode:** claim
**Attack outcome:** partially lands

## Strongest case against it

The opponent is a confirmational holist in the Duhem–Quine line, with Lakatos and a coherentist such as Thagard in support. The holist does not deny that truth differs from the test record; the note's Popperian point about dated corroboration is safe. The attack targets the separation between test record and fit, and through it the note's definition of an independent test.

1. **No test is free of the working theory.** Duhem showed that a hypothesis meets an observation only together with auxiliaries: instrument theory, initial conditions and ceteris paribus clauses. Quine generalised this: statements face experience as a body. In a working theory the auxiliaries are mostly other retained claims. The note requires an independent test not to "presuppose the working theory". On this view no test meets that requirement, so the test-record category is empty.
2. **Popper concedes it.** Popper measures severity relative to background knowledge. Basic statements are accepted by a decision that depends on the theories already in use (§§27–30, which the linked ingest summarises). So corroboration is relative to whichever part of the working theory serves as background. A claim's test record then partly depends on its fit with that background.
3. **So the standings are entangled, not separate.** Suppose fit-driven revision removes or rescopes an auxiliary. The holist says past tests are then re-read: a prior survival may stop counting as a survival at all. Fit has changed the test record without a new test. Lakatos adds that whether an anomaly counts as a refutation or as a puzzle for the protective belt is decided by the programme's heuristic. That is a fit judgement, made before test outcomes are scored.

If the attack holds fully, the note's three-way separation reduces to two: truth versus the coherence of the whole web. "Test record" then names a view of fit, not a separate standing.

## How the note engages it

**Partially engaged.**

- The note's operative criterion survives the attack. "What decides is whether the use could have gone against the claim, not where the evidence was produced" places independence in the possibility of failure, not in freedom from presuppositions. A holist accepts that a test using auxiliaries can still be risky for the target claim. Popper's severity, relative to background knowledge, is exactly that.
- The section "A contradiction does not say which claim to give up" is the Duhem problem in practice. It accepts that logic does not locate the fault, and it gives the choice to a decision that weighs test records and fit. That answers holism's point about where refutations land.
- The Scope section says the three questions are "separate, not statistically independent". That blocks the reading that the note claims no causal or evidential coupling.
- Not answered: the definition still says an independent test "does not presuppose the working theory". This contradicts the later criterion and is what the holist attacks. Also not answered: a test record is relative to the background used in the test, so revising fit can change the test record retrospectively. The note's "settles" claim survives, because dependence is not settlement. But the note presents the standings as more cleanly separable than its own epistemology allows.

The attack therefore lands on the definition of independence and on the missing relativity to background. It does not land on the central claim that a good test record does not make a claim fit, or that fit does not amount to a test.

## Constructive findings

- Restate independence so it excludes presupposing the claim under test, or any auxiliary that makes the outcome automatic, not the whole working theory. This removes the conflict with "use of a claim inside the theory can be a test".
- Add that a test record is relative to the background held fixed during each test, as well as dated. Say that revising fit can re-read past tests. State why this is coupling, not settlement, so "no answer to one settles another" stays defensible.
- Optionally link the contradiction section to the Duhem problem by name. It already gives the holist's answer, and naming it shows the note anticipated the objection.
- Cite the sincere-attempt requirement at the layer where the linked ingest finds it: §82 starred footnote *1 and Appendix *ix.

## Secondary objections (optional)

- **Coherentist objection.** BonJour and Thagard treat explanatory coherence as justification, so on their view fit itself raises credibility. The note's restriction, that fit counts only where the claim could have failed, roughly matches the Bovens–Hartmann and Olsson result: coherence raises credibility only when the agreeing sources are partly independent. The objection mostly fails. Citing that condition would strengthen the note against coherentists.
- **Is fit a standing of the claim?** Fit is defined relative to a theory and its problem ("handles its problem better with the claim than without it"). So it is a property of the pair of claim and theory, not of the claim alone. The opening calls all three "things can be asked about a claim", which slightly blurs this. One clause would fix it.
