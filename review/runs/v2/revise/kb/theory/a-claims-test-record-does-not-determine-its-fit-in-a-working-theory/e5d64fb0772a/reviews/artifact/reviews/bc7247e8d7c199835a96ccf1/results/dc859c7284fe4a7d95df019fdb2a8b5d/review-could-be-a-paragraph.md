# Review

## Summary

A one-paragraph rewrite would lose load-bearing content, so the multi-section structure is justified.

## Paragraph test

A one-paragraph version would say roughly: truth, test record and fit are separate standings of a claim, and none settles another. That keeps the title claim but loses:

- **The condition under which fit counts as a test.** Agreement adds to the test record only when the claim could have failed it (§82). This turns "fit is not a test" into a decision rule, not a blanket denial.
- **The failure cases for test record to fit.** These are irrelevance, duplication, wrong level of abstraction, mismatched scope, and detail that changes no inference. They are the concrete ways a well-tested claim fits poorly.
- **The two-way interaction.** Repeated fit failure earns a new independent test without refuting the claim. A severe test survived can force restructuring even when the result is less convenient. This is a separate inference from the separation claim.
- **The contradiction case.** A contradiction does not identify which claim to drop. The repair is chosen by weighing test records and fit.
- **Boundary conditions.** Fit has no single scale, authority and objectives lie outside testing, and the questions are separate but correlated.

Each item is a non-obvious claim, a failure condition or a boundary case. Compressing them into one paragraph would drop these.
