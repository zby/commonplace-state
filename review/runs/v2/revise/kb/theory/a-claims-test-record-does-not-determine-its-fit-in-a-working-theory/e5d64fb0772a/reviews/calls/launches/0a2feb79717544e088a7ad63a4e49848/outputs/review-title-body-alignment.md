# Review

The body supports the title's claim. The section "A good test record does not make a claim fit" argues it directly, through irrelevance, redundancy, wrong scope or level, and maintenance cost. The title is still narrower than what the body establishes.

The body establishes a broader claim: truth, test record and fit are three separate standings of a claim in a working theory, and none settles another. The body argues four of these separations explicitly:

- a test record is not truth (dated corroboration versus timeless truth);
- a test record does not make a claim fit (the title's claim);
- fit is not an independent test, except where the agreement could have gone against the claim;
- a contradiction between claims does not by itself decide which claim to give up.

The third direction gets the same weight as the title's. The closing section says "Neither may be substituted for the other", the description puts the "could have failed" condition up front, and both outbound "extends" links build on the limit of fit as a test. A reader who knows only the title would expect a one-directional note and miss the converse that later notes depend on. The fix is to retitle around the separation of standings, or at least name both directions between test record and fit.
