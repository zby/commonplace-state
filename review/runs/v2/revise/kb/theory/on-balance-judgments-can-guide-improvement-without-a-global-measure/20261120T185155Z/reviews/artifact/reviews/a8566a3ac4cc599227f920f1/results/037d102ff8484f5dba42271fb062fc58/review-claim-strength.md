# Review

## Summary

The title "On-balance judgments can guide improvement without a global measure" is contestable. An informed reader could argue the opposite: measurement-first improvement practice holds that you cannot improve what you do not score, and decision-theoretic positions treat a complete ordering or utility function as a precondition for a rational choice. The note answers that position: the evidence supports only a partial order, and analysis cannot supply the weights. The title is not a truism.

The title has no presupposed-claim shape. Its main predicate, "can guide improvement without a global measure", asserts the contested point directly instead of assuming it in the subject.

The "can" leaves the title at the level of possibility, while the body commits to more specific rules. Sharpening is optional, and the gate passes.
