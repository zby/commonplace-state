# Review

## Summary

The central claim is existential and modal: removing approval waits *can* leave required human evaluation in place. Its support is a derivation from the note's definitions, illustrated by hypothetical cases. Because a wait is defined by execution blocking and a requirement by the specification, removing one need not remove the other. A single coherent case, the weekly-review example, is enough to exercise an existential claim.

The normative consequence, that a claim of full automation must name preserved tasks and retired requirements, follows from the derivation for any process to which the definitions apply. The note explicitly declines wider claims. It does not claim that skipping the review causes harm, that the specified task is useful or necessary, or who deserves credit for an improvement. The scope it invites reliance on matches the domain its derivation exercises.
