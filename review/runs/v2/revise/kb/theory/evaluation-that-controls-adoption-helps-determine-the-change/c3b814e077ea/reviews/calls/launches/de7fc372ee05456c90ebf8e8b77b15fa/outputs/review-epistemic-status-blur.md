The note's material claims are conceptual: a stipulated criterion for when human evaluation is a dependency ("The distinction is relative to the process being automated and its stated requirements"), consequences derived from that criterion, and two worked cases. The cases are explicitly labelled hypothetical ("Consider two hypothetical cases"), so they read as illustrations rather than observations. The title's "can" and the opening's "need not" and "not necessarily" give the central claim existence force, which the derived weekly-review example supports.

The criterion is the note's own qualitative construction, presented as the relativization it adopts rather than as an empirical finding; the gate does not flag light qualitative decompositions merely for being the note's own. The note makes no quantitative or empirical-sounding claims. The routine-approvals link supports the narrow claim it is attached to (approval history does not show dispensability) and is not spread to the note's broader criterion. No well-supported finding is hedged without reason.
