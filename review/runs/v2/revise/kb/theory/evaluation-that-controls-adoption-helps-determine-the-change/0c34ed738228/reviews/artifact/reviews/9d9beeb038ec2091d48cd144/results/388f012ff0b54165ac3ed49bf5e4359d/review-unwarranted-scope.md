# Review

## Summary

**Claimed scope.** The claim covers any revision process in which a judgment, human or programmatic, controls whether a proposed change enters use. Such a judgment contributes to which system results. Assessment after adoption does not make that same contribution.

**Support.** The support is a conceptual derivation from what controlling adoption means, plus two cases explicitly labelled as hypothetical. The note does not offer observations as evidence and does not invite reliance on empirical frequency.

**Exercised domain.** A derivation covers what its premises hold over. The premise is that adoption is gated by a required judgment, and that premise defines the claimed class. The transfer from human to program evaluators is argued: the difference lies in what the judgment controls, not in who makes it.

**Limits stated.** The Scope sets out what the distinction does not establish: absence of other influence, learner membership, and quality. An Open Questions entry marks the reporting of evaluators outside the learner boundary as unresolved.

The claimed scope does not exceed the support the note presents.
