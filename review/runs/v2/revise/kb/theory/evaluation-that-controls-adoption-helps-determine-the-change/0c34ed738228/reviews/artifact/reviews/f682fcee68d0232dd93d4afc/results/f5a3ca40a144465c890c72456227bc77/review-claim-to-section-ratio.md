# Review

## Summary

The note has four headed sections: "The same activity can perform different roles", "One episode can contain both roles", "Scope" and "Open Questions". The untitled opening states the central claim. The note has three distinct claims that are not obvious:

1. Whether an evaluator helps determine the resulting system depends on whether the judgment controls adoption, not on who makes it (opening).
2. An assessment after adoption can still have a causal role, but in a different transition, such as a later revision or rollback (end of the first section).
3. Once you name the change and decision being examined, whether approval was required is a fact about the process. Redescribing the episode cannot turn required approval into assessment after adoption (second section).

Each section adds something of its own. The first section carries the paired hypothetical cases that make the distinction concrete, and it adds claim 2. The second section carries claim 3 and grounds "adoption" in operative change. Scope carries boundary conditions the argument needs: no other influence is ruled out, learner-boundary membership is a separate question, and approval does not establish quality. Open Questions holds one unresolved question. No section only restates framing, and there are no more sections than claims plus the support units they need.
