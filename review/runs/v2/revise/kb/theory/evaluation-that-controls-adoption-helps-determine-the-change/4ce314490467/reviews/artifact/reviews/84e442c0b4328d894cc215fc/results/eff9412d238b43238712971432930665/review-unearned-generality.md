# Review

The central claim is stated in concrete vocabulary: approval waits, human evaluation, adoption, monitoring and revision decisions. The examples use the same terms, describing an agent, a maintainer, a new written procedure and a weekly sample review. The claim does not move to a more abstract register than the argument uses.

The most general term is "process" and its "specification". The mechanism earns it. The distinction depends on the difference between execution gating and task assignment, which is a property of any specified process, not of agent instruction adoption in particular. The note applies the same distinction to adoption and, separately, to subsequent revision processes. It also extends the wait concept to recommendations and habitual waits on the same criterion. Abstraction appears where the argument needs it and is not spread across the text.
