# Review

The note's material claims are conceptual distinctions and consequences derived from them: a wait and a specified task are different changes; a full-automation claim must name preserved tasks and retired requirements; retiring a review is a change in requirements, not automation. These are presented as derivations from the stated definitions of blocking dependency and unfulfilled requirement, and the register fits that status.

The examples are marked as hypothetical ("Consider two hypothetical cases"). The note also limits what an example supports: "This example establishes a gap against the specification, not that skipping review causes harm." The note says an unfulfilled requirement is "not by itself evidence that the system would fail or degrade without the task," which stops the definitional distinction from being read as an empirical claim about harm. The note makes no quantitative or empirical-sounding claim without support.

The cited routine-approval note is used for the claim it makes, not as support for a neighbouring claim. No well-supported finding is hedged without reason.
