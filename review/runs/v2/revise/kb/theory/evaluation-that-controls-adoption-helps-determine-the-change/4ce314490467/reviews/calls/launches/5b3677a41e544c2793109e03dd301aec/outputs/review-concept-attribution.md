# Review

The note makes two attributions to linked notes; both resolve and both hold.

The first identifies the linked note as "the comparison of a system before and after an adopted change" and says that a judgment that the system improved does not show whether human evaluation was required to operate it. The target's title and opening paragraph describe a comparison of two operative states, a predecessor and an adopted successor, under an antecedent relation. Its Scope section states the same separation this note relies on: "Whether an evaluator also controlled adoption is a separate causal question". The attribution matches the target's core concept.

The second cites the linked note for the claim that routine approvals do not establish that human judgment is dispensable. That is the target's title claim nearly verbatim, and its opening paragraph argues it: repeated approval can conceal an unexercised exceptional-case check. The surrounding sentence ("Removing that stop requires deciding whether its check can be automated or is unnecessary") is consistent with the target's second paragraph on what removing an approval stop requires.
