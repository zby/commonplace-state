# Review

The note's load-bearing passages separate the label for an activity from the role it performs in a process. "Calling an activity evaluation does not tell us whether execution waits for it or the specification requires it" explicitly separates the term from the property that matters. The two worked cases apply the distinction to concrete objects and keep the activity (examining results and forming a verdict) separate from its process role (a dependency of adoption or an optional study).

The one imported concept, the before-and-after comparison of an adopted change, is presented as complementary, and the note says what it adds: an improvement judgment does not settle whether human evaluation was required. The note does not claim to inherit or redefine the linked concept. The sentence about human-authored acceptance rules separates authorship of the rules from a new human judgment for each change, so attribution is not blurred.

No passage permits materially different assignments of term, concept, prior account, explication and object of application.
