# Review

The note's strongest claim, stated using only its own commitments, is: a better-factory claim is determinate only when four things are declared in advance. These are the compared operative states (the predecessor and the installed successor), an antecedent scope-indexed assessment relation, the route by which the successor was produced, and an evaluator location that is separate from the learner boundary.

The claim is easy to find. The opening paragraph spends one sentence on the retention and learning background. It then says why improvement is a separate claim and states the claim itself. The second paragraph restates the claim as four declarations. Every later section serves one of those declarations or a consequence of one: relata and routes, the antecedent relation, preserved paths as a consequence of scope indexing, evaluator location, and non-entailment between retention and improvement. The Scope section bounds the claim and does not compete with it. No analogies or terminology blocks stand in front of the claim.

The finding concerns memorability. The claim is stated at three different levels of completeness. The title names two elements, the opening paragraph three and the second paragraph four. The headings do not follow the four-item list exactly. The simplest fix is to let the four-declaration sentence carry the opening and keep the retention background as one subordinate clause. A second option is to make the section order follow the four declarations visibly.

The `TODO!` line before the opening paragraph is editorial residue rather than apparatus. It does not hide the claim, but it is the first thing a reader sees.
