# Review

The core claim, from the title and description, has three parts: the relata are operative states, the assessment relation is antecedent, and evaluator location is declared separately from the learner boundary. The first two parts get the two longest sections. The relata section defines three terms and develops two successor routes. The relation section explains antecedence, scope indexing and the extent of the compared states.

The preserved-path section is secondary, because it derives a consequence of scope indexing. It runs to three paragraphs, which is about the length of the evaluator-location section. Its third paragraph separates preservation from compounding, which a reader could otherwise conflate with the claim, so the length has a purpose. The evaluator-location section is shorter, but it builds on the admission/measurement distinction already developed in the relata section. That distinction is the mechanism that separates evaluator location from the learner boundary, so this part of the claim is not underdeveloped overall. The Scope section is long, but its bullets bound the claim rather than develop secondary material. No secondary section crowds out the load-bearing argument.
