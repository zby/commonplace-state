# Review

The note's main claim, taken from its title, description and the "Four declarations" paragraph, has three parts. A better-factory claim compares a predecessor with an operative successor. It uses an antecedent, scope-indexed assessment relation. Its evaluator location is declared separately from the learner boundary. Most side material in the note supports this claim.

The deployed-system paragraph places all operative machinery inside the compared states. That premise belongs to the relata. The "Neither claim entails the other" section separates improvement from learning attribution. That boundary is needed because the opening derives the better-factory claim from retention's silence about quality. The Scope bullets bound antecedence and the two-route reading. These stay.

The preserved-path section is the main branch. It is a sound consequence of scope indexing, but it is downstream of the declarations, not part of them. With the compounding contrast, it adds a second topic with its own evidence needs. It also forces a Scope concession about relations that permit retirement of prior capability. The last Scope bullet uses this clause to justify stating the structure for factories. If the section is rehomed, that bullet needs a different justification or can say that the structure is general.

The oracle paragraph is a smaller case. Its final clause supports the evaluator-location declaration. Its first two clauses open a branch that the note explicitly does not resolve.

The TODO line about "software house" is an editorial marker, not a branch. This criterion does not assess it.
