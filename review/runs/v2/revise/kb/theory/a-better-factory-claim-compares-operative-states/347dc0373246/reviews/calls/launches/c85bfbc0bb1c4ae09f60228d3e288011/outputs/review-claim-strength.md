# Review

## Summary

The title asserts that a better-factory claim takes operative states (not changes or candidates) as its relata and is judged under an assessment relation fixed before the development it judges. An informed reader could reasonably argue the opposite on both counts: that improvement is properly judged on the change or the staged candidate that produced it, or that a retrospective criterion chosen after seeing the result is an adequate basis for calling a factory better. The note itself engages the second objection in its Scope section, which shows the claim is contestable rather than a truism. The claim passes the strength test.
