# Review

One paragraph cannot hold this note without losing load-bearing content. Even without background and connections, the note makes several separate non-obvious claims, each with its own support path:

- A candidate is not a relatum. Measurement over snapshots and candidate admission differ in whether the evaluation lies on the causal path.
- Antecedence is what makes the comparison capable of failing. Scope indexing keeps non-regression from being either unmeetable or vacuous.
- Preserving the factory-development path follows from scope-indexed non-regression. It is weaker than compounding and is checked at a different time.
- Evaluator location separates from the learner boundary according to whether a judgment gated installation.
- Passing the comparison and factory learning do not entail each other. The experience link has to be identified by a manipulated contrast.

The Scope section adds boundary cases: an antecedent comparison can still measure the wrong thing; a declared relation may permit retirement of prior capability, which removes the preserved-path consequence; and the two routes are readings of an episode, not a partition of physical processes. A one-paragraph version would have to drop either these boundary cases or the inference steps linking scope indexing to path preservation and gating to evaluator location. The multi-section structure is justified.
