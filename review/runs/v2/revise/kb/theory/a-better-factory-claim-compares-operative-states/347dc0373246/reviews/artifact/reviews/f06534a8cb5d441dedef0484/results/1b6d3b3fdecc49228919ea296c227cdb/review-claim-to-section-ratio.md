# Review

## Claim count against sections

The note has an untitled lead, five argument sections, a Scope section and Open Questions. The distinct non-obvious claims are:

1. The relata of a better-factory claim are the predecessor and the operative successor, not a candidate or a construction event. A successor arises by measurement over snapshots or by candidate admission.
2. The assessment relation must be antecedent and scope-indexed: non-regression on the predecessor's prior scope plus a named target that exceeds a threshold. Antecedence makes the comparison capable of failing.
3. Scope-indexed non-regression already requires preserving a factory-development path when that path is in the predecessor's scope. That requirement is weaker than compounding and is checked at a different time.
4. Evaluator location is a separate declaration from the learner boundary. Which side a judgment falls on depends on whether it gated the successor.
5. Passing the comparison and factory learning do not entail each other. The experience link needs a manipulated contrast, and internal retention controls do not supply the improvement claim.

Each argument section carries one of these claims and does not restate an earlier section. Section 3 could look like support for section 2, but it derives a separate consequence and draws a separate contrast with compounding. Scope carries boundary conditions: antecedence does not make the chosen dimensions correct, alternative relations are possible, and the claim's transfer beyond factories is limited. Open Questions names two unresolved problems. No section only repeats framing.
