# Simplification synthesis: A better-factory claim compares operative states under an antecedent assessment relation

The core claim is easy to find. The main simplification gains come from one organizing choice and two consolidations. The note announces four declarations but does not follow them through its sections. One caveat is repeated across the body. One corollary section affects the note's stated scope more than its placement suggests.

## Opportunities

### 1. Let the four declarations organize the note

- Location: opening paragraph ("The claim holds between two operative states of the factory, under an assessment relation fixed before the development it judges, and its evaluator sits at a declared location that is not settled by the learner boundary.") through the "Four declarations" paragraph, and the section headings that follow.
- Combines: core-claim-obscured (three forms of the claim; headings not mapped to the four items) and marginal-value-redundancy finding 1 (two lists in a row).
- Preserve or decide: The note does not settle whether "how a state becomes the successor" is a separate declaration or part of the relata. The Scope section treats the route as a reading of an episode, and the evaluator-location section depends on it. The author must make that choice before the headings can mirror the list. The title names only two elements. The author may keep it as a compressed headline or broaden it, but should not leave the reader with three competing lists. The section "Neither claim entails the other" is not one of the four declarations, so its role (the boundary between improvement and learning attribution) should stay visibly separate from that list. The `TODO!` line leaves an unresolved choice between keeping "software factory" and retargeting to "software house". It also comes before the opening. Resolving that choice affects the title and every heading, so it should be settled before any restructuring.

### 2. Give the "not a quality verdict" limit one home

- Location: opening paragraph ("Throughout, *better* means better under the declared relation…"), antecedence paragraph ("That is all antecedence buys…"), oracle paragraph in "Evaluator location and learner boundary are separate declarations", and Scope bullets 1–2.
- Combines: detail-overhang (caveat stated four times), marginal-value-redundancy findings 3 and 4, and branch-bloat finding 2 (oracle paragraph's first two clauses).
- Preserve or decide: Keep these points distinct: (a) no oracle is supplied; (b) dimensions can be correctly declared in advance and still measure what does not matter; (c) antecedence guards auditability and does not define improvement (Scope bullet 3, which no gate flagged); and (d) human acceptance judgments return the human-inclusive boundary to the headline result. Point (d) is the one load-bearing part of the oracle paragraph and should stay in the evaluator section. The author decides where the full limit lives. Scope is the natural place, but the antecedence paragraph is an alternative if the author wants the limit next to the mechanism.

### 3. Decide the role of the preserved-path section

- Location: "Preserving a repeatable factory-development path falls out of scope indexing" through Scope bullets 4 and 6.
- Combines: branch-bloat finding 1 and marginal-value-redundancy finding 2. Detail-overhang judged the section proportionate, and marginal-value-redundancy judged the preservation/compounding split to carry a needed distinction. The checks therefore agree that the section is sound and disagree about whether it belongs.
- Preserve or decide: The last Scope bullet says the structure is stated for factories *because* the preserved-scope clause depends on factory development being in scope. If the section is rehomed, the author must give that bullet a different justification or say that the structure is general, which the bullet already partly concedes for three of its parts. If the section stays, its role should be stated where it begins, along with the retirement-of-capability concession that qualifies it. One possible resolution is to open the section with "This is the part of the claim that is specific to factories". The defensive "not an extra virtue" sentence can be cut either way.
