# Review

## Summary

The central claim is that a better-factory claim compares operative states under an antecedent assessment relation. Each part carries a mechanism that constrains it.

- **Operative states as relata.** A discarded candidate has no behavioral-authority path, so comparing against it says nothing about the factory that later work depends on. If the premise is varied to "any constructed state counts", construction events become improvement claims. The note explicitly rules that out.
- **Antecedence.** If dimensions may be chosen after the fact, any successor that differs at all can be shown to differ favourably on some dimension. Antecedence is what lets the comparison fail. Dropping it breaks the conclusion, so the mechanism is load-bearing.
- **Scope indexing.** Without the index, non-regression is either unmeetable or vacuous. The preserved-path consequence is derived from this mechanism rather than added beside it.
- **Evaluator location.** An admission decision lies on the causal path and a post-hoc measurement does not. That is a specific causal reason the two declarations come apart.

**Falsifiability.** The claim would fail if a comparison could be made determinate without one of these declarations, or if post-hoc dimension choice did not systematically favour the successor.

**Escape hatches.** The Scope qualifications narrow the claim to auditability and a declared relation shape. They do not shelter it from counterexamples; they state what the mechanism does not deliver.
