# Review

## Summary

The core claim, from the title and opening, is this: a better-factory claim compares two operative states under an assessment relation declared before the development it judges. The two sections that carry this claim get the most development.

- "The relata are operative states" defines predecessor, candidate and operative successor. It also explains why a discarded candidate never enters the comparison.
- "The assessment relation is antecedent and scope-indexed" is the longest section. It covers the two components, why prior declaration matters and what it does not show, the non-regression scope, the whole-environment span and the preserved factory-development path.

"Improvement, retention and learning are different claims" is secondary. It separates this comparison from the retention and learning claims. Its three hypothetical cases and the paragraph on causal contribution are shorter than the assessment-relation section. They also serve the core claim, because they show what a passed comparison does and does not establish. The Scope section qualifies the main claim and does not grow into a competing topic. Nothing should be split out.
