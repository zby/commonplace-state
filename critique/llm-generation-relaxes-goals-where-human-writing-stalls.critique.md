# Critique: LLM generation relaxes a goal it can't satisfy and hides the constraint a human writer stalls on

**Note:** /home/zby/llm/commonplace/kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md
**Central commitment:** When no artifact satisfies a goal's full conjunction of constraints, LLM generation silently returns a typicality-biased *relaxation* — a real witness for a weaker problem dropping the hard conjunct — where a human writer would have *stalled* at that constraint, and this displaces the soundness check onto the reader at far higher cost.

**Critique mode:** claim

## Strongest case against it

**The stance:** an ML researcher (call it the *no-objective decoder* objection) holds that the entire argmax → conjunction → witness → relaxation apparatus is a metaphor imported from constraint satisfaction / optimization that does not describe what a language model does, and that once you strip the metaphor the note's distinctive claims either collapse into the banal ("LLMs sometimes produce fluent-but-wrong text") or become unfalsifiable.

The reasoning, made as strong as it gets:

1. **There is no argmax, and no objective.** Autoregressive decoding samples token-by-token from `p(next token | prefix)`. With temperature > 0 (the default for any real writing use) it is explicitly *not* taking an argmax; with greedy decoding it takes a per-token argmax over the *vocabulary*, which is emphatically not an argmax over *artifacts that satisfy a goal*. The note's pivotal sentence — "it is always running one operation: return the **argmax** — the most plausible artifact available" — names an operation the system does not perform. There is no global "most plausible artifact" being selected; there is a sequence of local conditional samples. The witness/relaxation story needs a global optimizer over the artifact-level constraint set, and that optimizer does not exist in the architecture.

2. **The "conjunction of constraints" lives in the critic's head, not the model.** Nothing in the model represents "C-speed ∧ Lisp-dynamism ∧ rest" as a satisfiable/unsatisfiable conjunction. The model has no representation of the goal as a logical conjunct set, no notion of which conjunct is "hard," and no "satisfiability" predicate to silently flip from hard to soft. So "the dropped conjunct is demoted from hard to soft in silence" attributes a decision to a system that never had the conjuncts as objects. The decomposition into conjuncts is supplied entirely by the reader doing the post-hoc analysis; the note then treats that supplied structure as if the model operated on it.

3. **The human/machine asymmetry is overdrawn and may be backwards.** The note's load-bearing contrast — human pen *stalls hardest* exactly where the model is *smoothest* — romanticizes the human and caricatures the model. Humans confabulate fluently across gaps in their reasoning constantly (the whole "writing is thinking" tradition exists *because* most writers don't stall; they bullshit and have to be trained to stop). And modern models do not uniformly "flow" over hard constraints: RLHF'd models hedge, refuse, say "this is actually contradictory," emit "I'm not sure," and chain-of-thought models visibly backtrack. So the claimed anti-correlation ("felt difficulty and machine fluency are anti-correlated with truth at the one constraint that matters") is asserted as structural when it is at best a contingent, model- and prompt-dependent tendency — and the strongest form of the objection is that the human "stall as beacon" is itself rare and unreliable, so the contrast is between an idealized human and a strawman machine.

4. **Therefore the residue is unfalsifiable or banal.** Once 1–3 land, "relaxation" reduces to: *LLMs sometimes produce fluent text that is wrong in a way that's hard to localize.* True, known, and not requiring any of the witness machinery. The specific, non-banal claims — that the dropped conjunct is *the typical-vs-novel one specifically*, that the output is *engineered to be unlocalizable*, that the check cost is *O(1) for humans vs. exhaustive-audit for relaxations* — are the parts the metaphor is doing illegitimate work to support, and none of them are operationalized to the point where they could fail. "Engineered to be unlocalizable" especially smuggles intentionality into a sampler.

In one form: *the note explains a real surface phenomenon (fluent-but-unsound prose) with an optimization-theoretic story the system does not instantiate, and the story's distinctive predictions ride on that non-existent structure.*

## How the note engages it

**Partially engaged — and the engagement is the note's weakest seam.**

- **On falsifiability (objection 4): engaged, and this is the note's best defense.** The "This is a claim that can be wrong" paragraph (lines 42–43) gives two directional predictions — under-witnessed throughput rises, and difficulty's diagnosticity of soundness falls toward chance — and names the conditions that would refute the mechanism. This blunts the "unfalsifiable" half of objection 4. It does *not* rescue the "banal" half: both predictions are also consistent with the deflated reading ("LLMs produce hard-to-localize wrong text"), so confirming them would not vindicate the witness machinery specifically over the banal story.

- **On the metaphor / no-argmax core (objections 1–2): unengaged.** This is the decisive gap. The note never acknowledges that "argmax over the most plausible artifact" is not the decoding operation, never addresses temperature/sampling, and never defends treating the conjunction-of-constraints as something the model operates on rather than something the analyst imposes. The hedge "argmax — the most plausible artifact available" (line 25) is stated as fact, not as a deliberately chosen idealization with named costs. The author would need to concede the framing is a *reader-side model* of an emergent regularity and argue it still earns its keep — but the note instead writes as if the model literally returns a relaxed witness.

- **On the human/machine asymmetry being overdrawn (objection 3): partially engaged.** The Scope section (lines 46–51) restricts the claim to "where the prose is doing the thinking" and the hallucination section concedes hedging-adjacent behavior exists elsewhere. But it never confronts the strong form: that humans routinely confabulate over gaps (so the "stall as beacon" is rare), and that RLHF/CoT models do sometimes flag contradictions (so "the model flows" is not structural). The note treats the stall as reliably involuntary and the flow as reliably smooth; the objection's sharpest version denies both.

## Constructive findings

- **Demote argmax from mechanism to model, explicitly.** Add one sentence conceding that decoding is token-by-token sampling, not artifact-level argmax, and reframe "relaxation" as an *emergent regularity* of plausibility-driven sampling — high-typicality continuations dominate where the constraint set is unsatisfiable — rather than a literal optimizer returning a witness. This costs the note nothing it actually needs and removes the single cleanest line of attack. The witness/conjunction language can survive as the *reader's* analytical decomposition (which is in fact how the note uses it everywhere except line 25).
- **Distinguish the note's claim from the banal one with a discriminating prediction.** The current two predictions are also explained by "LLMs produce fluent wrong text." Add one that specifically tests the *typicality-biased* and *anti-localizable* sub-claims — e.g., that across matched errors, the conjunct that gets dropped is disproportionately the low-frequency/novel one (testable by labeling), or that localization cost for LLM faults exceeds that for human faults *controlling for error severity*. Without this, objections 1–2 reduce the note to the banal reading even if the headline predictions hold.
- **Engage the confabulating human and the hedging model.** State the asymmetry as a *tendency under composition-as-discovery*, bounded by: human stalls are unreliable (writers confabulate), and model hedging exists but is typicality-driven too (it hedges where hedging is typical, not where soundness fails). This makes objection 3 a scoping refinement rather than a refutation, and is consistent with the note's own "confidence tracks typicality" backbone.
- **Drop or soften "engineered to be unlocalizable."** Intentional language ("engineered," "optimized to look maximally satisfied so its surface points away") is the most attackable phrasing because it imputes a goal to a sampler. "Tends to be unlocalizable, because high-plausibility surface is selected for at every conjunct" says the same thing without the smuggled agency.

## Secondary objections (optional)

- **The O(1) vs. exhaustive-audit cost claim is asserted, not derived.** A human-flagged fault is only O(1) if the human flag is trustworthy and correctly localized; in practice readers re-audit human prose too. The asymmetry is real in direction but the O(1) figure overclaims precision.
- **The hallucination-sibling section (lines 53–55) is labeled hypothesis but leans on the same contested coherence/correspondence split** — if the parent ("confidence tracks typicality") is the real mechanism, "relaxation" and "hallucination" may not be two faces of one parent so much as two relabelings of it, which would make this note less distinct from its own cited sibling than it claims.
- **"Semi-decidable, asymmetric" search framing (line 12) is another imported formalism** that, like argmax, may be more evocative than literal; same demote-to-model remedy applies, lower stakes.
