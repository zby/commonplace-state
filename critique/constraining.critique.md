# Critique: Constraining

**Note:** kb/notes/definitions/constraining.md
**Central commitment:** Constraining is a single coherent kind — "narrowing the space of valid interpretations a text or symbolic artifact admits" — that spans from a definition note at one end to executable code at the other, and is orthogonal to distillation.
**Critique mode:** definition

## Strongest case against it

The strongest opponent is not someone who denies that interpretation-narrowing is real. It is someone who holds that **"narrowing the space of valid interpretations" silently fuses two unlike operations into one word, and the gradient framing is what hides the seam.** Call this the *two-joints* objection. It has three teeth.

**1. The light end and the heavy end narrow different things by different mechanisms, and the note's own table proves it.** Look at the spectrum rows. "Define a term" and "Add structured sections" narrow the *reader's hypothesis space*: a human or LLM still does the interpreting, and the artifact merely makes some readings less plausible or "dispreferred." "Extract a deterministic function" and "Create a formal schema/grammar/route table" do not narrow a reader's hypothesis space at all — they *remove the reader* and substitute a formal consumer whose output is fixed by mechanism. These are not two intensities of one operation; they are two operations: **persuasion-of-an-interpreter** versus **replacement-of-the-interpreter**. The note even concedes the seam when it calls codification "a phase transition: the nature of the artifact changes fundamentally" (in codification.md) — a phase transition is, by definition, *not* a smooth gradient of the same substance. You cannot simultaneously say "it is a single gradient" and "the far end is a phase transition where the nature changes fundamentally." One of those is wrong. The opponent says the gradient is the marketing and the phase transition is the truth.

**2. The "fewer valid readings" test is unfalsifiable across the very range it is supposed to unify, because "valid" means something different at each end.** At the light end, validity is *normative/pragmatic*: a reading is "invalid or dispreferred" because a convention says so, but nothing stops an agent from reading it the old way — the note admits constraints "without any runtime enforcing it." At the heavy end, validity is *mechanical*: an invalid input is rejected by a parser regardless of anyone's preference. So the central test ("are there readings that were previously plausible but are now invalid or dispreferred?") quietly swaps its meaning of "invalid" as you slide the cursor. A definition that does not change a single downstream behavior still passes the test ("dispreferred" is enough), which means the boundary admits things that make *no difference in use*. This is the classic idle-distinction failure: if a "constraint" need not change what any consumer does, the category is measuring intention, not effect.

**3. The orthogonality-with-distillation claim leaks at the heavy end, so the 2x2 is not clean.** The note's flagship "constrain without distilling" example is "store an LLM output." But storing an output and *thereby committing to one interpretation of a prompt* is precisely a targeted transformation of recorded material (the prompt + the model's response) into a use-shaped artifact for a downstream consumer — which is the definition of distillation given in the sibling note. The note tries to wall this off by saying distillation requires transforming *recorded source material*, but a prompt-and-completion pair *is* recorded source material the moment it is stored. So the canonical cell that is supposed to demonstrate orthogonality ("Constrained / Not distilled = stored output") is contestable, and if it falls, the two axes are correlated at the corner that matters most, weakening the claim that they are independent dimensions.

The opponent's synthesis: there are two real joints here — (a) **focusing an interpreter** and (b) **replacing the interpreter with a mechanism** — and the second one already has its own name in this very KB (codification). "Constraining" is then either (i) redundant with codification at the only end where the effect is robust, or (ii) at the light end, an idle relabeling of "writing more precisely / defining terms / using conventions," which needed no new theoretical term. Either way the unified category does not carve a joint; it spans across one.

## How the note engages it

**Partially engaged — and the engagement is asserted, not argued.**

- The note *anticipates* tooth 1 structurally: it says the light and heavy ends "use different media and different levels of force, but they count as constraining for the same reason: they reduce semantic latitude" (para 2). This is a bare restatement of the disputed claim, not a defense of it. It names the difference (media, force) and then declares it immaterial without saying why the difference between focusing an interpreter and removing one is *less* fundamental than the shared label. The codification note's own "phase transition... nature changes fundamentally" language directly undercuts this and is never reconciled.
- Tooth 2 (idle distinction) is **unengaged**. The "Misuse Cases" and "Exclusions" sections all police the *outer* boundary (don't call retrieval / ease / accumulation constraining) and never address the *inner* worry that the light-end "dispreferred" criterion is too weak to require any behavioral difference. The note explicitly keeps "dispreferred" in the test (Scope, line 16) and even lists "constraining is not always enforcement" (line 50) as a *feature*, which is exactly the move the opponent attacks.
- Tooth 3 (orthogonality leak) is **partially engaged but circularly**. Both this note and distillation.md repeat the "store an LLM output = constrain without distilling" example as the load-bearing demonstration, and both *define* distillation as requiring "recorded source material" in a way that conveniently excludes a stored prompt/output. The note never confronts the obvious rejoinder that a stored output is itself recorded material being shaped for a consumer. The distinction is stipulated, not earned.

## Constructive findings

- **Decide whether the central commitment is "one gradient" or "two joints with a phase transition between them," and stop asserting both.** If the phase transition at codification is real (the codification note insists it is), the honest framing is: constraining is a *family resemblance* term, not a single quantity on a single axis, and the table is a family album, not a thermometer. Saying so out loud would let the note contend with tooth 1 instead of papering over it.
- **Add a behavioral-difference clause to the test to defeat the idle-distinction charge.** Require that a candidate constraint rule out at least one reading that *some consumer would otherwise have acted on* — i.e., narrowing that no consumer's behavior could ever reflect is not constraining, it is decoration. This converts "dispreferred" from an escape hatch into a falsifiable condition and directly answers tooth 2.
- **Either defend or retire "store an LLM output" as the orthogonality witness.** To defend it, the note must argue why a stored prompt/completion pair is *not* recorded source material reshaped for a consumer; that argument does not currently exist and the burden is real. A cleaner witness for "constrain without distill" might be a *hand-authored* schema or route table written from scratch (no prior recorded source), which sidesteps the leak.
- **Name the genuine distinction from codification at the light end.** If the only robust, behavior-changing instances of constraining are the codified ones, the note owes a concrete example of a *purely natural-language* constraint that demonstrably and durably changes a consumer's behavior without any formal consumer — and an account of why that is not just "good writing / a definition." Lines 41-42 gesture at this ("many constraints never need to codify") but give no instance where the non-codified constraint provably bites.

## Secondary objections (optional)

- **"Relaxing" is asserted as the clean inverse but is not symmetric.** Relaxing "a constrained component with a more general-purpose one" describes swapping codified mechanism back to natural language — a heavy-end operation. There is no described inverse for the light end (you do not "relax" a definition by un-defining it). The pair-naming claim inherits the same light/heavy asymmetry as the main category.
- **"Prior Analogues" lists four formal-end analogues (gradual typing, formal spec, ontology engineering) and only one that touches the light end (Carnap's explication).** This lopsidedness is itself evidence for the two-joints objection: the literature the note reaches for clusters at the codification end, suggesting that is where the real concept lives.
- **The distinction "constrained vs distilled is at the artifact level, not the decision level"** (carried in distillation.md) is invoked to rescue orthogonality but is never stated in this note, so a reader who only has constraining.md cannot reconstruct the defense against tooth 3.
