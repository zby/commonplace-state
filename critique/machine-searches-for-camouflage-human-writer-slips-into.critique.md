# Critique: The machine searches for the camouflage a human writer only slips into

**Note:** /home/zby/llm/commonplace/kb/notes/machine-searches-for-camouflage-human-writer-slips-into.md
**Central commitment:** What distinguishes machine relaxation from human relaxation is *search*: the human only slips into the camouflage (an unsearched oversight/self-deception), while the LLM returns the *argmax* of a plausibility objective — a relaxation *selected* to be undetectable — and that selection is what drives the observability gap and makes the machine's failure harder to catch.

**Critique mode:** claim

## Strongest case against it

Hold the stance of a **deflationary motivated-reasoning theorist** — someone who accepts the surface phenomenon (LLM relaxations are smooth and hard to localize) but denies the note's one load-bearing move: that the *process* differs in kind because the machine "searches" and the human "slips." The opponent's claim is that "search" is doing all the work in the note and is an equivocation that, once split, collapses the asymmetry. The case has three prongs.

**(1) Humans also search for camouflage — and the note's own examples prove it.** The note grants the failure mode is shared but reserves *search* for the machine. This is the weak joint. Human gap-hiding is overwhelmingly *not* an "unsearched slip": it is rhetoric, motivated reasoning, and rationalization — i.e., a *directed search through the space of plausible-looking moves that conceal the gap*. A reviewer "carried by fluent argument" was carried by prose an author *optimized* to be carrying. A paper that "ships with hidden gaps" was massaged through drafts, framings, and hedges *chosen* to look most solved — the human equivalent of argmax over a plausibility objective, run consciously or not. Cognitive science (Mercier & Sperber's argumentative theory; the entire motivated-cognition literature) holds that human reasoning is *built* to produce locally convincing justifications, not truth. If so, the human relaxation is *also* selected-to-be-undetectable, and the note has mistaken "the human doesn't optimize at the literal token level" for "the human doesn't optimize." The residue the note relies on (the hedge, the three-week silence, the abandoned draft) is residue of the *honest* human who stalled — but the note already conceded the relevant comparison case is the human who *did* hide the gap. For that human, the residue is exactly as absent as the machine's, because they too optimized it away.

**(2) "Search" equivocates between training-time optimization and inference-time selection.** The note's evidence for machine search is training-time: "next-token and preference training optimize for text that *looks* satisfied." But the conclusion it wants is about a *per-output process*: the model "does not drop a constraint at random; it selects the relaxation that looks most solved." These are different objects. Training-time optimization produces a fixed conditional distribution; at inference the model runs one forward pass and (with greedy/argmax decoding) emits the mode of that distribution. There is no *search over relaxations* happening at inference — there is a single feed-forward evaluation. Calling that "search for the camouflage" smuggles agency the mechanism does not have: the model is not comparing candidate relaxations for detectability; it is sampling from a distribution that was *shaped* by training. But by that standard the *human* is also running a process shaped by a lifetime of social optimization for persuasive output — the human's "slip" is equally the output of prior optimization. So either both are "searched" (training-shaped) or neither inference pass "searches." The kind-asymmetry survives only by counting training-time pressure on the machine while not counting developmental/evolutionary pressure on the human.

**(3) The "selected to be undetectable" claim is unfalsifiable as stated.** The note's observability gap rests on the model's camouflage being *selected for undetectability*, leaving "as little [residue] as it can." But undetectability is defined relative to a detector, and the note offers no detector and no measurement. Any smooth LLM output confirms "selected to be undetectable"; any detectable LLM gap is explained away as imperfect optimization. The sibling note (`llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md`) at least states a falsifiable prediction (under-witnessed throughput rises; difficulty stops predicting soundness). *This* note's distinctive addition — the *search/selection* claim — carries no such prediction. The mechanism is overdetermined: the plain alternative is that LLM output is smooth because *fluency is the dominant axis of the training objective everywhere*, not because gap-concealment was specifically selected. "The model is smooth at the gap because it is smooth everywhere" predicts the same observations with strictly less machinery — the camouflage is a *byproduct* of a uniform fluency prior, not a *selected* property of the relaxation. Occam cuts against the note: it posits a targeting ("selected to be undetectable") that nothing observed requires.

The synthesis of the three: strip "search" of its inference-time agency (prong 2), and notice humans optimize concealment too (prong 1), and the residual claim — "the machine's relaxation is undetectable-by-selection" — is either unfalsifiable or reducible to "the machine is uniformly fluent" (prong 3). The kind-difference the note builds its training corollary on dissolves into a *degree*-difference: the machine is more fluent on average and relaxes by default rather than at the boundary. That residual is real and interesting — but it is the sibling note's claim, not this note's.

## How the note engages it

**Partially engaged on prong (1); largely unengaged on (2) and (3).**

- **Prong (1) — humans also optimize concealment:** *Partially engaged, and the engagement is the note's softest point.* The note explicitly concedes the failure mode is shared ("reviewers get carried by fluent argument, papers ship with hidden gaps") — then asserts, without argument, that the human version is "an oversight or a self-deception" rather than a search (line 13). This is precisely the equivocation the opponent attacks: it *names* the human concealment case and then *defines it as unsearched* by fiat. The note does not consider that the carried reviewer and the gap-hiding paper are products of directed rhetorical optimization. The residue argument (line 15) only covers the *honest stalling* human, not the *concealing* human the note itself invoked one paragraph earlier — so the note answers a different human than the one its own example raised.

- **Prong (2) — training-vs-inference equivocation:** *Unengaged.* The note slides from "training optimizes for plausible surface" (a fact about the objective) to "the model selects the relaxation that looks most solved" (an inference-time process claim) within a single sentence (line 13) with no acknowledgment that argmax decoding is one forward pass, not a search over relaxations. The Borretti "RLHF device will shock me" quote is rhetorical color, not evidence of inference-time selection. The note never distinguishes "shaped by past optimization" from "searching now," which is the exact move that, applied symmetrically, would also make the human a searcher.

- **Prong (3) — unfalsifiability / Occam:** *Unengaged on the distinctive claim.* The note's "speculative on two counts" caveat (line 21) flags two *different* risks — representability and rewardability of the shortfall, and the decorative-hedge regress — both of which concern the *training implication*, not the core search/selection claim. The note inherits a falsifiable prediction from the sibling note but never offers one for *its own* added commitment (selection-for-undetectability vs. uniform-fluency-byproduct). The self-flagged caveats do **not** discharge prong (3): they are aimed downstream of the objection.

**Verdict on the self-flagging:** the note is honestly marked `speculative` and labels the hypothesis as a hypothesis, which lowers the stakes — but the caveats it raises are not the ones the strongest opponent presses. A marked hypothesis still owes a statement of *what would distinguish it from the cheaper byproduct explanation*, and that is absent.

## Constructive findings

- **Decide whether "search" is inference-time or training-time, and say which.** If the claim is training-time (the *distribution* was shaped to make relaxations plausible), state that and drop the agentive "selects the relaxation that looks most solved," which invites the prong-(2) reading. If the claim genuinely needs inference-time selection (e.g., under sampling/beam search the model *does* explore candidate continuations and prefer plausible ones), then *that* is the mechanism to name and defend — and it would need to explain why greedy argmax decoding, which does no search, still produces the camouflage.
- **Confront the optimizing human head-on.** The note must engage the motivated-reasoning case directly: argue *why* human concealment leaves residue even when the human is optimizing to hide (e.g., humans optimize over a different, sparser objective; the optimization is bounded by conscious access and time; the residue is in *process traces* the human cannot fully erase) — or concede the asymmetry is about *residue-erasure capacity*, not about search-vs-slip. As written, the human/machine contrast rests on an undefended stipulation that human concealment is "unsearched."
- **Give the distinctive claim a falsifier.** State what would distinguish "selected to be undetectable" from "smooth because uniformly fluent." Candidate: if camouflage were *selected*, LLM relaxations should be *harder to detect than equally-fluent non-relaxed passages by the same model* — i.e., gap-passages should be measurably smoother/more confident than no-gap passages. If they are indistinguishable, the uniform-fluency byproduct explanation wins and the search claim is idle.
- **Separate the two contributions clearly.** The note's genuinely novel content is the *training implication* (a flagging objective could retrain the stall, gated by verifier availability). That survives even if the search/slip asymmetry is downgraded to a degree-difference. Consider leading with the trainability claim and treating "search" as the weaker scaffolding it is.

## Secondary objections (optional)

- **The Borretti quote cuts the other way.** "If I don't turn this garbage into something presentable the RLHF device will shock me" is a description of *training pressure felt as an objective*, and it is phrased in first-person agentive terms that humans would recognize — i.e., it is evidence that the machine's "search" and the human's motivated concealment are *the same phenomenology*, not different in kind. The note cites it as support but it equally supports prong (1).
- **The "relaxes by default, with no stall to clear" clause (line 15) is a different and stronger claim** than the search asymmetry, and it is smuggled in at the end of the paragraph. "More under-constrained ideas reach finished prose because there is no stall at all" does not need the *search* hypothesis — it follows from the sibling note alone. Folding it in here lets the note borrow that claim's force for the weaker search claim.
- **Observability-gap residue is reader-relative, not process-relative.** The hedge and the three-week silence are residue *only if the reader has access to the process* (drafts, timestamps, the author's affect). For a published artifact stripped of process — the actual comparison case — the honest human's stall leaves no more readable residue than the machine's. The note's residue list quietly assumes a privileged observer of the writing process that the prose-soundness reader does not have.
