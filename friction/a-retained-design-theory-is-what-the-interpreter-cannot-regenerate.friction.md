# Friction check: Design rationale must preserve decision premises its interpreter cannot regenerate

**Note:** `kb/notes/a-retained-design-theory-is-what-the-interpreter-cannot-regenerate.md`
**Central claim (one sentence):** For a source-checkout consumer, retain current, decision-relevant premises that implementation, git, and general knowledge cannot reproduce at the required fidelity, while treating explanation those sources can reproduce as a cache only when it has no independent role.

## Filter

**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim asks recoverability to depend on a declared consumer, fidelity threshold, and source set; it retains premises that fail that test, exempts independently authoritative or operational records from the cache test, and subjects only recoverable, role-free explanation to cache economics. These commitments occupy successive branches of one retention rule rather than requiring one passage to be both indispensable and dispensable.

## Signal — thinnest joints

1. **“Natural-language explanations whose fidelity requires judgment instead need an explicit lineage and review path.”** — UNSUPPORTED — The stricter rule for mechanically derived copies establishes that deterministic checking can govern those copies; it does not establish why judgment-dependent prose specifically requires both explicit lineage and a review path, or why those controls are sufficient for its fidelity.
2. **“Once the fidelity test passes and independent roles are excluded, judge recoverable explanation as a cache.”** — THIN — Recoverability establishes that the semantic content can be reconstructed, and excluding named independent roles removes several reasons to preserve a duplicate, but the note does not establish that reconstruction savings are the only remaining function such a passage can serve or that its list of role exceptions is exhaustive.
3. **“A knowledge base whose retained knowledge participates in its own redesign splits these functions between an interpreter and retained text, since theory-mediated self-improvement needs both interpretation and retention from one substrate.”** — THIN — The linked two-function premise supports the need for both interpretation and retention in one substrate, but it does not by itself allocate Naur's mapping, justification, and modification judgments between interpreter and text; that allocation needs a concrete account of which function retained premises perform during redesign.

## For the human

Look first at the unsupported jump from judgment-dependent prose to mandatory lineage and review, then decide whether the cache rule needs a positive account of all remaining passage roles rather than only exclusions.
