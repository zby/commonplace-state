# Friction check: The reachability conjecture: train the house, not the LLM

**Note:** kb/articles/the-reachability-conjecture-train-the-house-not-the-llm.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** At least one automated software house can reach open-ended coherent software change within a declared scope, horizon, and resource envelope by computationally training retained software and natural-language notes while keeping 2026-09-02 LLMs fixed and removing humans from internal production and theory-holding roles.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim asks these properties to hold at once: fixed current LLMs, open-ended coherent change, scope-bounded practical reachability, computationally trained legible internal state, and no human in an internal production or theory-holding role. They can coexist as one concrete conjecture because the article makes the claim existential and bounded rather than universal. The main possible contradiction, "open-ended" versus "declared scope," does not dissolve the claim because the article defines open-endedness as relevant novelty inside the declared demand stream, not unbounded competence over all software projects.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **"Current LLMs can produce both forms. Software can make a proposed change operative and feed later consequences into another update. The process is a schematic loop"** — UNSUPPORTED — The reason establishes that code and notes can be generated, retained, and reused. It does not establish that the loop can perform semantic admission and credit assignment well enough for retained changes to become adequate project-specific theory rather than accumulated local repairs.
2. **"What has changed is that a machine can now apply informal project-specific state ... A computational house could therefore pass Naur's functional test"** — THIN — Applying an informal paragraph in one decision supports possible use of project-specific state, but it does not by itself support sustained theory-holding across delayed consequences and novel demands. The missing step is how repeated local interpretation becomes the team-like program-theory function Naur used as the test.
3. **"This is compatible with the Bitter Lesson's preference for general methods that scale with computation because the lesson selects how behavior-shaping structure is produced, not the form in which it is retained"** — THIN — The reason answers a disqualifying objection to legible state, but only at the category level. It does not support the stronger practical inference that computational search over software and notes will scale enough to replace repeated human authorship of the decisive theory.
4. **"If that scope requires a machinery change ... the automation obligation requires the house to make it. Self-application is then a consequence of automation, not a separate requirement"** — THIN — The obligation can include machinery change, but requiring self-application does not show that the same training path remains controlled when context assembly, validators, or selection policy are themselves being revised. The article still needs a more concrete story for revising the machinery that governs revision.
5. **"A user remains external when supplying requirements, feedback, domain knowledge, or an acceptance judgment. A person who repeatedly diagnoses the theory failure or chooses the successor theory is filling the excluded internal role"** — HOLDS — The boundary works as a role distinction: supplying demands and acceptance is different from repeatedly choosing the system's internal successor theory. Its remaining weakness is not the inference itself, but the operational gray zone where domain knowledge plus acceptance may smuggle in theory repair unless the witness records who made which diagnosis.

## For the human
Look first at the update-loop inference: the article most needs the missing concretization between "retained computational changes are possible" and "retained changes become adequate project-specific theory."
