# Friction check: A provisional causal selective-evaluation model

**Note:** kb/work/causal-impact-selective-evaluation/selective-evaluation-model.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** A selective-evaluation method needs separate objects for applicability, impact prediction, observation, evidence selection, and commitment because proving a check inapplicable is different from omitting it under uncertainty, and only the latter changes what acceptance can honestly claim.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
The claim asks two things to hold at once: the model must be concrete enough to separate the decision objects, and modest enough not to pretend that this separation already yields a settled objective, numerical policy, or acceptance rule. Those commitments coexist. The note's caution clauses keep the model from overclaiming.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **"One hypothesis worth testing is a hybrid: use enforced structure to over-approximate the possibly affected obligations, then assign probabilities to reachable or uncertain cross-effects."** — UNSUPPORTED — The earlier selector taxonomy shows why reachability, causal impact, and similarity are different, but it does not show why this hybrid is the next hypothesis the argument earns rather than one live design option among several.
2. **"A fixed-input output diagnosis can refine downstream obligations once an input has been assembled. It cannot replace whole-harness impact analysis when an interface or router edit changes the assembled input or context upstream."** — THIN — The note names the upstream/downstream distinction, but the cited diagnosis is imported rather than worked through here, so the routing boundary is asserted more than demonstrated.
3. **"At least three honest responses remain: leave the obligation unknown and refuse the corresponding warrant, execute the check, or define a new commitment rule over observed results plus a declared residual-risk condition."** — THIN — The note shows that omission under uncertainty breaks the original warrant, but it does not establish that these three responses are exhaustive rather than illustrative.
4. **"The locality hypothesis is most plausible when a sparse change lands in a pre-existing decomposition that matches the evaluated functions, with explicit dependencies and a small downstream impact closure."** — THIN — This reads like plausible mechanism talk, but the note does not derive why these are the decisive locality conditions or why other sources of spread would be secondary.
5. **"The conservative baseline is therefore candidate-relative applicability followed by full evaluation of the applicable set."** — HOLDS — Given the note's opening distinction between mechanical inapplicability and uncertain omission, the stated reason is enough to support this conservative baseline, though it does not yet establish any efficiency advantage over fuller baselines.

## For the human
Look first at the hybrid-selector proposal: it is the point where the note most clearly moves from a defended distinction to a preferred next design without enough intervening argument.
