# Friction check: Theory building inside the system

**Note:** kb/articles/theory-building-inside-the-system.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Inside a declared human-plus-machine boundary, the right way to describe theory building is as a dated allocation of its functions across computational, human, and joint actors, and Commonplace's traced episodes currently support a partial allocation in which criticism and proposal have moved computationally while other functions remain human or untraced.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim can hold as one thing. It asks for a partial, dated actor profile rather than a total answer to whether the system "has" the theory, and those commitments can coexist. The strain is evidential: several rows are interpretive or explicitly untraced. That weakens support, but it does not force the claim to split into incompatible versions.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“What they do not supply is a check that the formal assumptions match the world. In the table above, that check is a row of its own, on the human side.”** — UNSUPPORTED — The note establishes only that proofs are conditional on assumptions. It does not trace any episode that isolates assumption-to-world checking as a human-only function, and it does not rule out computational support for part of that check.
2. **“The question with a measurable answer is which functions of theory building the computational part now supplies.”** — THIN — The article mostly offers a disciplined reading, not a demonstrated measurement. It later says only two computational rows and one human row are directly supported by traced episodes, while several others are best readings or explicitly untraced.
3. **“Decision content is continuous, so ‘the machine does 40% of the theory building’ has no principled meaning. ‘The machine applies and criticizes, while a human admits’ does.”** — THIN — Continuity defeats naive percentages, but not measurement in principle. The note does not yet show why no weighting or unitization could make a quantitative comparison principled; it shows only why a function-by-function description is more intelligible here.
4. **“One sentence from the maintainer restored the intended claim each time. The episode therefore supports three assignments: criticism and proposal on the computational side, and coherent modification on the human side.”** — THIN — The two repair cycles show a real boundary, but they do not isolate coherent modification as the only missing capacity. The failure could also sit in objective preservation, context selection, or another untraced dependence.
5. **“Measure improvement per human judgment, not per human hour.”** — THIN — The note gives a reason to distrust raw time as the sole metric, but not enough to privilege judgment count over severity, latency, or scope of the remaining interventions.

## For the human
Look first at the “measurable answer” move: either narrow the article to a partial interpretive profile, or add a clearer account of what turns these actor assignments into measurements rather than best-effort readings.
