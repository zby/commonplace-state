# Friction check: The bitter lesson selects production methods, not representational forms

**Note:** kb/notes/the-bitter-lesson-selects-production-methods-not-representational.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** The bitter lesson favors search-and-learning over hand-crafting as a production method rather than favoring distributed weights as a representational form, so learned localized artifacts remain a coherent scaling conjecture whose decisive unresolved problem is cross-artifact credit assignment.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim's required properties can coexist: localized prompts, code, skills, or records can be proposed, evaluated, selected, and retained computationally while remaining natural-language or symbolic artifacts. The note also keeps the conceptual claim distinct from its conditional empirical conjecture: it does not require every representational form to scale equally, nor does it infer persistent localized semantic content merely from persistent external governance functions.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“The hard core is credit assignment without a chain rule”** — UNSUPPORTED — The bounded examples establish that no general large-corpus method is demonstrated, but they do not establish that credit assignment is the decisive bottleneck rather than proposal search, evaluation cost, nonstationarity, consolidation, or combinatorial interactions; naming adjacent problems does not supply the missing discrimination.
2. **“Three discrete substitutes are visible in the methods surveyed here: explicit dependency edges bound the affected validation work, retained episodes carry attribution signals, and accumulated evaluation checks price candidate changes.”** — THIN — Episodes can contain attribution evidence, but dependency edges chiefly narrow an impact set and checks chiefly accept or reject candidates; neither operation by itself assigns responsibility for the deployment outcome that prompted a change, so calling all three credit-assignment substitutes outruns the stated mechanisms.
3. **“Hand-crafting-forever ... fails the lesson exactly as charged. What scale removes is generalization whose scope was asserted rather than tested, and hand authorship is a common way to embed such scope. The compliant position is to test search and learning over localized forms too.”** — THIN — Hand authorship can produce an empirically tested artifact, while computational search can overfit an asserted evaluation scope; the association between hand authorship and unearned scope supports testing localized artifacts, but does not by itself make perpetual hand production fail in every domain or establish search-and-learning as the required alternative there.
4. **“Before backpropagation scaled, hand-crafted features could likewise appear to mark a fixed boundary of learning because no general method reached them. That analogy motivates a search for the missing machinery.”** — THIN — The historical analogy shows that an apparent boundary can be contingent on missing machinery, but it supplies no evidence that large-corpus localized learning has an analogous method waiting to be found rather than a structural scaling limit; it motivates exploration only in the weak possibility sense the note later acknowledges.
5. **“The lesson's target is hand-crafted content, not human authority.”** — THIN — Objectives, commitments, and adoption vetoes are usefully distinguished from learned knowledge content, but the preceding list establishes that the note chooses to reserve these operations; it does not show from Sutton's production-method argument that human authority is categorically outside the lesson's target or cannot itself be partly subjected to search, evaluation, and learned delegation.

## For the human
Look first at the unsupported elevation of credit assignment from one missing component to the decisive hard core, because the scaling conjecture and its proposed research program are organized around that diagnosis.
