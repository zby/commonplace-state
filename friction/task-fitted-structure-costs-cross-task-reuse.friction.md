# Friction check: Current-task fit alone does not warrant costly structural entrenchment

**Note:** kb/notes/task-fitted-structure-costs-cross-task-reuse.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** For a KB expected to face changing questions, current-task fit warrants reversible adoption but not costly entrenchment unless an enduring constraint, discriminating transfer evidence or proof, or coordination value supplies an additional warrant.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim asks current usefulness and future adaptability to coexist, and its proposed object is coherent: task-derived structure can be used now while its replacement costs remain bounded, then become entrenched only when a separate warrant supports that commitment.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“Failing to find a boundary-preserving rival makes entrenchment defensible”** — UNSUPPORTED — The preceding conditional says that a rule is inherited if replacing it actually requires changing a commitment. Failure to find a rival does not establish that necessity without a stated search boundary or adequacy test, so absence of a found alternative cannot yet carry the warrant.
2. **“If a KB is expected to outlive its current questions, current-task fit warrants adopting task-derived structure at the narrowest useful scope. Until another warrant appears, keeping that structure replaceable preserves the adaptation options that entrenchment would spend.”** — THIN — Replaceability does preserve adaptation options, but that benefit alone does not establish the proposed default. The note does not compare it with the recurring costs of local variation, indirection, delayed standardization, or migration into a later shared form; its coordination exception shows that these competing costs can reverse the choice.
3. **“Only seven of 139 automatically writing systems implement all seven tracked lifecycle operations. That result supports an adoption-versus-retirement asymmetry in the reviewed corpus.”** — THIN — The count contrasts automatic writing with complete lifecycle coverage, but absence of at least one of seven operations does not by itself isolate retirement, compare adoption and removal rates, or concern structural elements specifically. It supports incomplete lifecycle curation more directly than the narrower asymmetry claimed here.
4. **“It earns wider scope only by remaining useful across different questions that could have exposed a bad fit, and only for the scope those cases exercise or a proof establishes.”** — THIN — Discriminating cases can expose some bad fits, but the note supplies no threshold for how many or which differences make the evidence sufficient to warrant costly commitment. Limiting the inference to exercised scope prevents unrestricted extrapolation, yet the promotion step remains under-specified, as the note's own open question acknowledges.
5. **“Drift and export pose the same transfer test: both bring structure fitted to one question set into contact with another. One does so across time, the other across installations. Hardcoding a profile therefore bets not only on other people's KBs, but also on the KB's own future.”** — HOLDS — The shared change in question set is enough to establish the stated transfer exposure, and costly hardcoding makes future internal mismatch a real bet even though drift and export can differ in magnitude and migration options.

## For the human
Look first at the move from preserving adaptation options to prescribing narrow, replaceable adoption: the missing concretization is the decision rule that compares option value with the recurring costs of staying replaceable.
