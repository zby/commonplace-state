# Friction check: A domain-priced exception does not refute an idealization

**Note:** kb/notes/a-domain-priced-exception-does-not-refute-an-idealization.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** A defeated crisp claim can remain refutable as an idealization when independently attested domain practice marks its exceptions and those exceptions remain bounded and subordinate, whereas ordinary, unbounded, or dominant exceptions still refute it.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“Every one of those counterexamples is priced by the domain that supplies it. … On the criterion above, the defeated premises were arguably ideal-type claims”** — UNSUPPORTED — The paragraph pools different signatures from Python, Ruby, runtime implementation, Erlang upgrades, and prototype-based languages without showing that each cited counterexample is independently marked, bounded, and subordinate in its own domain; the pooled list therefore does not establish the conclusion for “every one” of them.
2. **“A pipeline whose premise verdicts are only DEFEATED and HOLDS has no verdict for *holds as an idealization, exceptions priced*. It therefore cannot accept such a repair and cannot reject one either”** — UNSUPPORTED — The absence of a dedicated verdict establishes that the distinction cannot be recorded in that verdict field, but not that a reviewer cannot test, accept, or reject an idealized reformulation through revised premise text or another part of the workflow.
3. **“What the two cases jointly establish is the mechanism and the missing verdict, not a rate”** — UNSUPPORTED — One case says only that an ideal-type reading was arguably available and unasked, while the other says an idealization might properly have failed; together they illustrate an omitted question but do not establish that the verdict vocabulary caused claims to ratchet toward weakness.
4. **“The general form is that the domain's own practice already treats the exception as exceptional, in ways attested before anyone raised it against the claim”** — THIN — Prior independent marking blocks an ad hoc label, but a marked interface, warning name, cost, or ritual alone does not establish that the exception is bounded, subordinate, or even relevant to the idealization's first-order regime.
5. **“A competing paradigm exists whose defining move is not making the commitment at all — evidence that the commitment is real in the original rather than an accident of description”** — THIN — A rival can show that a design choice is contestable, but its existence does not by itself show that the original domain treats departures from that choice as exceptional or priced.

## For the human
Look first at whether “domain-priced” is only one necessary guard alongside boundedness and subordination, then make each worked counterexample earn those guards separately.
