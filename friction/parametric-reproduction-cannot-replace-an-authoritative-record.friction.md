# Friction check: Parametric reproduction alone cannot replace an authoritative record

**Note:** kb/notes/parametric-reproduction-cannot-replace-an-authoritative-record.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Reproducing an authoritative record's content does not by itself replace that record, because substitution requires a separately governed artifact or service that carries the authority guarantees rather than merely restating them.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
Content reproduction and authoritative substitution are compatible but distinct goals: a parametric artifact can supply both at once if governance gives it stable identity, integrity, attribution, and the other guarantees appropriate to the record, while an ungoverned recollection supplies only the first.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“The same distinction explains why system-definition artifacts—artifacts the system consumes with binding force—survive even when a model can reproduce the facts behind their constraints.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Binding force establishes that some governed authority must survive, not that the explicit system-definition artifact must survive; the note's own governed-checkpoint case permits the authority to move into a parametric artifact or service.
2. **“Successful substitution therefore requires: … Contestability”** — UNSUPPORTED — The premise that reproduction does not transfer institutional status establishes the need for governance, but it does not establish that every authoritative record must support appeal or amendment; the note needs either a narrower class of authority or an argument that even final and immutable authoritative records are necessarily contestable.
3. **“The durable role of a retention layer is therefore to preserve authority, not exclusive possession of information.”** — THIN — The listed authority guarantees support a durable role for retaining authoritative records, but they do not show that authority is the retention layer's durable role generally; authority-free material may still need explicit retention for reliable activation, inspection, local specificity, or lossless detail.
4. **“Stable, repeated, authority-free content is a candidate for weights—or for deletion once models reproduce it reliably.”** — THIN — Reliable reproduction supports considering parametric storage, but it does not alone establish safe deletion: the note leaves activation, coverage measurement, failure recovery, and the future availability of the reproducing model outside the criterion.
5. **“Reproduction alone does not accomplish substitution: copying content does not transfer the source's institutional status.”** — HOLDS — Reproduction is concretized as the model's ability to state content, whereas substitution includes a governance act that designates an operative object; success at the former therefore does not entail the latter.

## For the human
Look first at the claim that binding system-definition artifacts themselves survive: the note has established the persistence of governed authority, but its own checkpoint example shows that this does not establish persistence of the original artifact or representation.
