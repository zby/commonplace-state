# Friction check: Opposed recompute factors do not decide documentation segmentation

**Note:** kb/notes/opposed-recompute-factors-do-not-decide-documentation-segmentation.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Opposed directions in per-reconstruction saving and recurrence do not order audience cache value and therefore cannot decide documentation segmentation, which among feasible alternatives requires comparing a specialized arrangement's marginal audience-fit benefit and all of its incremental system costs against the best shared arrangement.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim commits to one compatible decision model: factor magnitudes determine cache value, while audience fit and the system-wide costs introduced by a split determine whether segmentation improves on a shared baseline. Its feasibility filters, common-currency condition, and separate treatment of mutually exclusive architectures prevent those commitments from requiring one scalar comparison where none is available.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“Documentation that records something the source cannot regenerate … offers no recomputation to price: segment such content according to the historical, evidential, or governing role it serves.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Excluding non-reconstructable content from the cache calculation and distinguishing recoverability, provenance, and authority do not establish that those roles should determine artifact boundaries; one artifact could carry several explicitly typed roles.
2. **“Low traffic reduces incidental detection opportunities only when uses sometimes compare the layer with source.”** — THIN — The existence of some source-comparing uses does not establish how detection opportunity scales with traffic, because per-use comparison rates may differ and source-change triggers, generated checks, or task failures can expose drift through other paths.
3. **“net cache value ≈ (net saving per equivalent reconstruction) × (reconstructions avoided) − (maintenance)”** — THIN — The definitions support subtracting maintenance from avoided reconstruction cost, but the product additionally assumes a stable or correctly weighted average saving and additive reconstruction events; the note states common horizon and currency requirements without stating those aggregation assumptions.
4. **“The first asks whether the cache has positive net value.”** — THIN — Positive value decides maintenance only relative to a specified no-cache baseline and absent a better use of the same constrained resources; the approximate value model alone does not establish that positive value is the full maintenance criterion among competing feasible caches.
5. **“When a split adds a layer to an otherwise unchanged shared arrangement, it pays only when this benefit exceeds every system-wide cost caused by the split …”** — THIN — The marginal framing identifies the right baseline, but it does not state how audience-specific benefits are aggregated at arrangement level or whether “exceeds every” means exceeding the sum of all incremental costs; without that total delta, the condition can be read as a set of pairwise comparisons that still permits a negative net result.

## For the human
Concretize the split comparison first by defining one arrangement-level specialization benefit and one aggregated incremental-cost total; then decide whether the role-based segmentation advice needs its own argument.
