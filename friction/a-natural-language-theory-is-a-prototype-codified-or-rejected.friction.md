# Friction check: Representational form does not determine whether a theory is a prototype

**Note:** kb/notes/a-natural-language-theory-is-a-prototype-codified-or-rejected.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** A theory's prototype standing depends on how operationally bound and costly to revise its current commitments are, not on whether those commitments are represented in natural language or symbols.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
The claim commits to one arrangement: representational form governs how consequences are assigned, operational binding governs the cost and grain of revision, and epistemic acceptance remains a separate relation. A disposable formal model and an operative prose authority witness these properties at once without requiring codification to end prototype standing or prose to preserve it.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“Among theories with comparable downstream coupling, pressure to codify tends to rise with invocation frequency and the cost of a wrong reading, and to fall with volatility.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Naming a falsifying comparison does not supply evidence or a mechanism strong enough to establish these three prevalence gradients; in particular, it does not show that volatility lowers codification pressure rather than increasing demand for an executable, rapidly replaceable specification.
2. **“a codified artifact should become operative authority only after the theory has been accepted for the scope that the artifact commits”** — THIN — The later requirement–objective distinction shows that executable success cannot itself constitute acceptance, but it does not establish the temporal prohibition: bounded operative trials might be part of producing the evidence on which acceptance rests, so the claimed ordering needs an additional risk, reversibility, or authority premise.
3. **“Revision remains local only while consumers are loosely coupled to the current version.”** — THIN — The prose-authority and scratch-model examples show that form alone does not determine propagation, but they do not establish loose coupling as the exclusive condition for locality. Stable interfaces can shield tightly bound consumers, while internal premise dependencies or reconstruction costs can make a loosely consumed theory expensive to revise.
4. **“Codification often accompanies stronger commitment because formally assigned consequences make an artifact easier for validators, executables, and other components to consume.”** — THIN — Easier consumption supplies a plausible path from codification to added consumers, but it does not establish that those consumers are actually added often enough to support the prevalence claim; the note's own next sentence correctly makes added binding a separate step.
5. **“If machine assistance reduces artifact-construction, proof-generation, or checking costs, more candidates can be expressed symbolically before anyone adopts them. Formal models then become experiments within the prototype phase.”** — THIN — Lowering a genuine bottleneck expands the feasible candidate set, as the later qualification says, but it does not by itself establish pre-adoption uptake or low operational coupling; those require demand for symbolic experiments and restraint from making the cheaper artifacts operative.

## For the human
Look first at the unsupported frequency–misreading–volatility prevalence claim; either give its causal comparison a concrete witness or separate it from the form-versus-binding thesis that does not depend on it.
