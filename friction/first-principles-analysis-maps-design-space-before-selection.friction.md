# Friction check: First-principles analysis maps a design space before selecting within it

**Note:** kb/notes/first-principles-analysis-maps-design-space-before-selection.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Before a consequential design decomposition becomes load-bearing, first-principles analysis should derive a revisable map of consequence-changing choices from inherited boundary constraints, expose rival positions and decompositions, and let requirements and evidence select only after that exploration.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
The claim asks constraint-bounded generation, analytically separate but potentially interacting axes, empirical revision, rival decompositions, and delayed closure to hold at once. These properties do not demand that derivation precede prototyping or that every analytic cell be feasible, because the note makes prototypes part of map revision and requires a witness or feasibility argument before treating a cell as an option.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“The same axes predict the default evidence each form admits: read natural-language content, test symbolic content, and probe distributed-parametric content.”** — UNSUPPORTED — The two stated axes distinguish semantic assignment from reinterpretation and localized from distributed content, but neither distinction establishes why reading, testing, and probing are the adequate default review methods. That conclusion needs a bridge from each form's semantics and access path to what observations can refute claims about it.
2. **“Implementation, tuning, debugging, and workflow repair also search within the distinctions and operations admitted by the system's architecture.”** — THIN — The fixed-search-space mechanism is clear for an activity whose operators are held fixed, but these activities can include architectural revision. The extension needs to specify when they are downstream searches constrained by the decomposition and when they are capable of changing it.
3. **“[Explanatory-reach] supplies a negative test. A derivation should rule out a plausible rival and identify the premise that does so, or predict how the conclusion changes when a premise changes.”** — THIN — Rival exclusion and premise sensitivity test discrimination and counterfactual dependence, but the note does not establish that either is necessary for, or sufficient evidence of, an explanation continuing to work beyond the cases that produced it.
4. **“Agreement across rival maps is corroboration, not proof, because the derivations may share a hidden premise.”** — THIN — Agreement adds evidential weight only when the derivations exert partly independent pressure. Calling their formulations materially different does not establish that independence, and the note leaves the needed criterion as an open question.
5. **“First-principles exploration counters this failure by moving search outward across decompositions before moving it inward within one.”** — THIN — Searching across decompositions creates opportunities to expose excluded regions, but it counters premature closure only if the rival derivations differ on load-bearing premises and the comparison can reveal consequential differences. The ordered procedure requires those conditions without yet supplying a stopping or coverage test for them.

## For the human
Look first at the representational-form example's jump from its two axes to reading, testing, and probing; it is the least-supported demonstration that the derived dimensions predict a design consequence.
