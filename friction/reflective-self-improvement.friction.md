# Friction check: Reflective self-improvement

**Note:** kb/articles/reflective-self-improvement.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Recurrent compounding requires a retained benefit to help produce later improvements, while reflection can make the machinery and decomposition of improvement revisable under a separately fixed adjudication boundary, making Commonplace a plausible but still unmeasured testbed for this form of control.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
Retained causal contribution, recurrent feedback, a revisable decomposition, and a fixed per-episode adjudication boundary can coexist because the boundary constrains how a candidate is judged during one episode without permanently removing the boundary or decomposition from separately authorized later revision.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“By routing notes individually, the system can load only the relevant theory. This reduces competition for context as the system grows, rather than embedding the theory in Python strings or executable lineages.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Selective loading can reduce the amount of theory in context, but the article gives no comparison showing that natural-language notes reduce competition better than selectively loaded code-backed artifacts; representational form alone does not establish the claimed advantage.
2. **“Commonplace's claim is not merely that humans can edit it. The claim is that retained procedures and artifacts make human–agent redesign part of an explicit operating path, and that installed changes remain available for later use and revision.”** — THIN — The later audit and validator example support editability and some reuse, but they do not establish that the retained procedures form one operative redesign path across the heterogeneous changes on which the testbed claim depends.
3. **“A replacement installed through one revision must remain inspectable and revisable in later episodes. This lets discovered omissions change later audits.”** — THIN — Continued inspectability and revisability make such change possible, but do not show that an omission will be represented, routed into a later audit, or granted authority to alter that audit.
4. **“This affordance also needs a stable adjudication boundary.”** — THIN — The following argument shows why a valid improvement comparison needs fixed terms, but it does not establish that addressability itself needs that boundary; the necessity belongs to adjudicating improvement, not to making commitments revisable.

## For the human
Look first at the claimed context advantage of routed natural-language theory: it needs a concrete comparison that separates selective routing from representational form.
