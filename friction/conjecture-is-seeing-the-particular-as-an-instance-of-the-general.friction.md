# Friction check: Unifying conjecture makes a general and its instances intelligible together

**Note:** kb/notes/conjecture-is-seeing-the-particular-as-an-instance-of-the-general.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** A unifying conjecture proposes a general structure and recasts known particulars as its instances, with shared-feature, shared-structure, and generative forms making progressively stronger explanatory commitments.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The proposal of a general and the reinterpretation of known cases can be one operation; the three forms vary that operation's commitment without requiring them to be strictly nested.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“Naming amortizes discovery cost only when the structure is reusable and its criteria exclude tempting false positives.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Reusability and discrimination matter, but excluding tempting false positives is not necessary for amortization if later savings outweigh the cost of checking them; the sentence also omits how often the structure will be reused and what naming and maintenance cost.
2. **“The cases constrain the general's content and scope; the general identifies what matters about the cases and how they relate. Each side therefore makes the other more intelligible.”** — THIN — The general supplies an interpretation of the cases, but constraint by cases does not by itself show that those cases make the general intelligible rather than merely underdetermine or test it; the reciprocal conclusion needs a more specific account of what “intelligible” means in each direction.
3. **“Unifying conjectures can relate cases through three forms of increasing explanatory commitment.”** — THIN — The table distinguishes organization, structural commonality, and process explanation, but it does not establish a single ordering across non-nested forms; a detailed shared structure can commit to more than a weak generative sketch, so the comparison needs a fixed basis such as causal assumptions, consequences, or ways of being falsified.
4. **“Similarity search over numerical representations can cheaply surface candidates that share features. Recognizing a shared argument or proof structure usually requires semantic comparison. A generative model requires a further step.”** — THIN — The escalating workflow is plausible, but it depends on what the numerical representation and retrieval tool encode: similarity search can surface structural candidates, while an already represented generative relation can be retrieved without fresh process proposal. The note states that dependency immediately before this sequence but does not carry it into these comparative cost claims.

## For the human
Look first at the amortization condition: decide whether false-positive exclusion is genuinely necessary, or replace it with an explicit expected-benefit comparison that permits some verification cost.
