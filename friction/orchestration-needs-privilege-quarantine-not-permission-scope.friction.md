# Friction check: Agent orchestration needs a privilege quarantine, not just a permission scope

**Note:** /home/zby/llm/commonplace/kb/notes/orchestration-needs-privilege-quarantine-not-permission-scope.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** When one role in an orchestration is exposed to untrusted, attacker-reachable content, the defense that closes the risk of that content steering a high-privilege action is a role-level privilege quarantine (bar the content-exposed role from privileged actions entirely; let a separate actor role act only on the reader's already-vetted structured output) — not finer per-call tool-permission scoping.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim wants two things at once: (1) a structural argument that per-call scoping is *categorically* unable to close the injection risk ("architectural, not a matter of tuning"), and (2) an honest admission, in the Scope Limit section, that quarantine itself "does not eliminate risk from the pipeline — it relocates where the risk concentrates," leaving a residual path (a manipulated reader emitting well-formed-but-poisoned output) essentially unaddressed. These look like they could pull apart into "quarantine solves it" vs. "quarantine doesn't solve it" — but the note holds them together by narrowing the claim: quarantine closes specifically the *direct* path (untrusted content steering a call that itself has privileged tools), and explicitly declines to claim it closes the *indirect* path (a trusted actor deceived by a compromised reader's structured output), naming that as needing "its own oracle." That's a real, single, scoped claim rather than a dissolve — but the scoping is thin enough, and the word "closes" strong enough, that it's flagged again below as the weakest joint.

## Signal — thinnest joints

1. **"Quarantine closes that gap upstream of any single call, by denying the untrusted-content role the privileged capability altogether" / "The defense that actually closes this is a privilege quarantine"** — UNSUPPORTED — This claim of closure is directly in tension with the note's own Scope Limit section ("it does not eliminate risk from the pipeline — it relocates where the risk concentrates") and Open Question 1 ("Zero verification collapses quarantine to a relabeling of trust rather than a reduction of it"). If a manipulated reader can emit a malicious-but-well-formed extraction that the actor then acts on "exactly as designed," the attacker's content still steered a privileged action — the same failure mode the intro claims is "closed," just moved one hop downstream. The note never reconciles "closes" with "relocates, does not eliminate"; a reader has to supply the narrowing themselves.

2. **"a single call already carries enough authority to do damage once its action is steered by injected content, so scoping *which tools a call may use* cannot fix a problem located in *which content is allowed to steer a call*"** — THIN — The "so" asserts an architectural (categorical) impossibility, but the counterexample isn't ruled out: a per-call scope that denies *all* privileged tools whenever the call is touching untrusted content achieves the identical effect to quarantine, just expressed at call-granularity instead of role-granularity. The note needs to show why role-level enforcement is architecturally different from a maximally strict per-call rule (e.g., that per-call rules are harder to guarantee consistently, or easier to misconfigure) — but it asserts the categorical distinction rather than arguing for it.

3. **"Quarantine is the same gap read one level down"** (linking to the aggregate-authority note) — THIN — This treats "per-call permission checks don't bound aggregate authority across many calls" (the cited note's claim) as the same mechanism as "a per-call check doesn't ask where the instruction that shaped this call came from" (this note's confused-deputy claim). These are two distinct failure modes — authority summing across a sequence of individually-legitimate calls, versus a single call's provenance-blindness to injected content — and the note asserts their identity ("the same gap") without showing the two are actually one mechanism rather than two analogous-but-separate gaps.

4. **"Neither system cites the other. The convergence is on the role-level split itself"** — THIN — Built from exactly two examples, both flagged by the note's own Open Questions as "first-party accounts... with no adversarial red-team measurement." Two independent-but-unaudited instances of a decades-old security principle (privilege separation) being applied is weak ground for the word "convergence," which implies a meaningful, surprising pattern rather than two practitioners independently reaching for a well-known idea.

5. **"An agent can be fully within its allowed tool scope and still be a confused deputy... because nothing in the per-call check asks where the instruction that shaped this call came from."** — HOLDS — This is the strongest joint surfaced: the confused-deputy framing is a well-established security argument, and the reasoning (permission checks authorize actions, not the provenance of the input that selected the action) is internally sound and does the work it's asked to do.

## For the human
Look first at joint 1 (the closes/relocates tension) — it is the exact spot where the note's title-level claim ("quarantine is the defense that closes this") and its own Scope Limit section pull in opposite directions, and the note offers no explicit reconciliation for a reader to lean on.
