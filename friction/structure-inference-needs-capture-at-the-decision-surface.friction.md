# Friction check: Bottom-up structure inference needs capture at the decision surface, not the state

**Note:** kb/notes/structure-inference-needs-capture-at-the-decision-surface.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Bottom-up inference of entities and relations from traces is feasible only when capture happens at the decision surface (decision-shaped: inputs, constraints, exception, approval, action, outcome) rather than in the resulting state (state-shaped: the changed row, the final value), because the "why" behind a decision is cheaply available at the moment of decision and decays toward unrecoverable once only state remains.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The claim wants two things at once that look like they could conflict: (1) a *necessary precondition* strong enough to gate feasibility outright ("bottom-up structure inference is feasible only when..."), and (2) the note's own Open Questions section admitting the decision/state split may really be "a continuum along how close the capture point sits to the decision," not a binary. A hard gating condition and a continuum-valued variable can look incompatible — either capture crosses a bright line or it doesn't; how do you gate on a spectrum?

They coexist without dissolving because the note's own first open question already states the resolving move: the precondition is a *threshold on a continuum* ("the claim only requires enough of the why to be present for the target structure to be inferable"). A threshold on a continuous variable is still a necessary condition — "enough decision-shapedness" is required, and how much is enough is left open rather than answered. That is underspecification, honestly flagged, not a hidden contradiction. So the claim resolves to one thing: capture position is a gating variable on trace-derived structure inference, and the note commits to that without a fake binary/continuum split.

The nearest thing to a fracture is not in the Open Questions (which name the tension) but in the body text, which asserts the gate in fully categorical language ("not an optimization on the same design — it changes which world models are learnable at all") that reads stronger than a threshold-on-a-continuum framing licenses. That overclaim is recorded below as a Signal joint rather than treated as a Filter-level dissolve, since the note elsewhere supplies the softer framing that would fix it.

## Signal — thinnest joints

1. **"Recording those 'decision receipts' at the commit surface is nearly free — the information is already in hand."** — UNSUPPORTED — The premise ("the deciding process is holding all the material that explains it") only establishes that the information is *available* to the process at that instant. It does not establish that *capturing* it — deciding what to record, formatting it, writing it durably, doing this consistently across every decision path including exceptions — costs nothing. Availability and free-to-record are different claims; the sentence swaps one for the other without argument. This is the load-bearing "cheap" half of the note's central cost asymmetry, so the gap matters.

2. **"Choosing to instrument decision surfaces rather than data stores is not an optimization on the same design — it changes which world models are learnable at all."** — UNSUPPORTED — Asserts a categorical, all-or-nothing difference in kind. This is in tension with the note's own Open Questions, which concede the decision/state split may be "two ends of a continuum" rather than a bright line. If it's a continuum, instrumenting "more toward" the decision surface is exactly the kind of matter-of-degree change this sentence explicitly denies is happening ("not an optimization on the same design"). The sentence needs the binary reading to land as written; the note elsewhere undercuts that reading.

3. **"The reason is a locality property of intent: the 'why' behind a change is maximally available at the moment of decision and decays toward unrecoverable in the state that decision produces."** — THIN — This is the single mechanism the entire note is built on, introduced by an explicit "The reason is," and it is simply asserted — no worked example, no cited case, no account of *why* intent should decay in this particular way (as opposed to, say, being reconstructable from sufficiently rich event logs, which is literally the note's own second open question). A load-bearing premise stated once and never independently corroborated.

4. **"This is the same gap [raw accumulation does not create usable memory] names as the ingress problem: you can have state, but not the legible 'why.'"** — THIN — Claims identity ("is the same gap") between this note's decision-surface/state-shape distinction and the other note's general ingress problem. The two may be genuinely the same mechanism, or this note may be a specific instance being folded into a broader frame it only resembles. Identity is a stronger claim than analogy, and no argument is given for why it's identity rather than family resemblance.

5. **"So this note and that one name two independent preconditions on trace-derived memory... Missing either sinks the approach, and satisfying one says nothing about the other."** — THIN — The independence claim (positioning and verification vary completely independently) is asserted by architectural parallelism ("two orthogonal axes") rather than demonstrated. It's plausible, but also plausible that decision-surface capture systematically correlates with easier verification (e.g., an approval-and-signoff record is both decision-shaped *and* easier to verify than a bare state diff), which would make the two preconditions correlated rather than independent. The note doesn't rule this out.

## For the human

Start with joint 2 (the categorical "changes which world models are learnable at all" line) — it's the one place the note's confident prose outruns the softer, more defensible threshold-on-a-continuum position it states elsewhere, and reconciling the two is a small, concrete edit rather than a deep rewrite.
