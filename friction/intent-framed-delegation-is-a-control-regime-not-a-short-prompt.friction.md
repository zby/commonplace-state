# Friction check: Intent-framed delegation is a control regime; prompt length does not establish it

**Note:** kb/notes/intent-framed-delegation-is-a-control-regime-not-a-short-prompt.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** In consequential planner-executor handoffs, preserving intent while leaving means open depends on a governed allocation of information, authority, integration, feedback, and recovery across the handoff boundary, so prompt length or outcome-only wording does not by itself establish adequacy.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The note asks upstream to fix purpose and non-negotiable boundaries while leaving execution-dependent means to the downstream actor. Those properties can coexist because the claimed freedom is bounded freedom over means, not freedom to redefine purpose. The note also distinguishes "prompt length is not enough" from "short cues can work inside a governed system"; that is a coherent structural claim rather than a split thesis.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **"Their overlap shows bounded delegation."** — THIN — The two source formulations plainly overlap on purpose, delegated means choice, and supervision, but the note does not yet show that these overlapping features are the decisive common mechanism rather than a family resemblance abstracted after the fact.
2. **"The recurring functional surfaces are the worker's authority boundary; access to relevant context and resources; ownership and integration of its output; feedback or verification; and recovery or escalation."** — UNSUPPORTED — The note has shown that consequential handoffs need control, but not why this exact five-surface decomposition is necessary or complete rather than a useful local taxonomy.
3. **"Without one, a failure that can change acceptance, violate a boundary, contaminate composed work, or prevent recovery has no structural route back to the fixed intent."** — THIN — This is stronger than the note's prior argument. Missing one operative mechanism may be dangerous, but the text does not establish that it always removes every route back; some failures could still be caught by other controls or by human intervention.
4. **"Structural adequacy for preserving stated intent and boundaries therefore turns on the control relation, not on prompt length or outcome-only wording."** — THIN — The note does show that length is not sufficient. It does not fully show the stronger contrast as written, because prompt detail can itself be one carrier of the control relation and may matter as part of it.
5. **"These patterns can coexist in one workflow, but none substitutes for the defined mechanism."** — THIN — The comparison section states non-substitutability, but it does not work through a case that demonstrates why trigger-based, rolling-wave, or broader-autonomy patterns cannot preserve the same bounded judgment relation when used alone.

## For the human
Look first at joints 2 and 4: the title claim can survive, but the note most needs the author to decide whether the five control surfaces are a derived necessity, a local heuristic, or a provisional decomposition that should be stated more weakly.
