# Friction check: Superseded choices need a historical witness; refuted beliefs lose subject-matter standing

**Note:** `kb/notes/superseded-choices-are-retained-superseded-beliefs-are-not.md`
**Central claim (one sentence):** Whether displaced content must be retained depends on its remaining truth role rather than on supersession alone: a superseded choice still needs at least one reliable witness to the choice event, while a refuted belief loses subject-matter standing unless some separate historical proposition earns retention.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **"If later evidence refutes it, the current theory should be rewritten. Preserving the old wording as history is a separate retention decision."** — UNSUPPORTED — The note shows that refutation removes current subject-matter standing, but it does not yet show why that settles the storage policy against retaining the displaced wording as a named historical witness.
2. **"Where applicable requirements, constraints, and beliefs determine a selection uniquely, no residual choice was made."** — THIN — Unique determination does support the claim that no free selection needed recording, but the note does not defend the test for when apparent choice is fully reconstructable rather than merely heavily constrained.
3. **"Those artifacts may show the winning option without showing that a decision was taken, which alternatives remained live, or which forces were weighed."** — THIN — This supports the possibility that downstream artifacts underdescribe the event, but the general obligation to retain a witness needs a sharper account of which missing historical details are load-bearing often enough to ground the rule.
4. **"Retaining the artifact keeps a witness to the first without granting equal standing to every region."** — THIN — The mixed-artifact carveout is plausible, but the note does not yet explain the mechanism that lets a bounded reader preserve the event-fact while not accidentally carrying forward the expired prescription or stale rationale.
5. **"This instance establishes feasibility, not unique correctness."** — HOLDS — As a bounded existence claim, the Commonplace example does support coexistence of append-only choice history and holistic belief rewrite without overclaiming uniqueness.

## For the human
Look first at the unsupported jump from "refuted beliefs lose standing" to "their prior wording should be rewritten rather than retained as named history"; that is the joint most in need of concretization.
