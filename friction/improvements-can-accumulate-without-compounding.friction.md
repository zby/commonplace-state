# Friction check: Improvements can accumulate without compounding

**Note:** kb/notes/improvements-can-accumulate-without-compounding.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Improvements accumulate when later improvement consumes, transforms, or preserves retained results, but they compound only when an earlier result's benefit causally increases a later episode's gain or reduces its cost, directly or through reinvestment.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
Retention of a growing stock, dependence of later work on retained results, and unchanged productivity of later improvement can coexist; compounding adds a distinct counterfactual productivity effect that accumulation does not require.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“A system that retains each useful rule while every new rule remains equally difficult to discover accumulates without compounding.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Unchanged discovery difficulty excludes one productivity effect, but an earlier rule could still increase the later gain or reduce evaluation, integration, or retention cost, so the example has not excluded compounding under the note's own broader test.
2. **“The metric used to accept a change tests its immediate target. ... Evidence for compounding must therefore ... be measured on a productivity dimension other than the accepting metric”** — UNSUPPORTED — An immediate acceptance result cannot establish a cross-episode causal effect by itself, but that does not show that the same metric or dimension cannot be applied to a later episode within a counterfactual design.
3. **“A sequence supports sustained compounding only when each successive contribution carries that evidence.”** — THIN — Sustained compounding requires evidence of repeated causal contribution, but the note does not establish that every episode must carry its own local trace; a sequence-level or aggregate design could potentially identify the repeated effect.
4. **“Those benefits can reach later improvement in two ways”** — THIN — The direct/indirect split is used as exhaustive routing, but defining direct as no resource reallocation and indirect as reinvested savings does not show where mixed paths belong or that every mediated productivity effect fits one side cleanly.

## For the human
Look first at the retained-rule example: either hold every later productivity dimension fixed or narrow it so equal discovery difficulty really excludes compounding.
