# Friction check: Moving revision decisions into the automatic system

**Note:** kb/articles/moving-revision-decisions-into-the-automatic-system.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** Moving a revision decision from a maintainer into machinery without losing governance requires six represented conditions, while Commonplace currently moves work around such decisions but leaves generic admission and model-dependent decisions with maintainers because their inputs are not represented reliably.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES

The desired properties can coexist: machinery can inspect, evaluate, authorize, install, and later revisit a change while decisions that still depend on unrepresented inputs remain human. The claim describes a bounded transfer of decision work, not simultaneous full autonomy and continued reliance on tacit maintainer judgment for the same decision.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **“The semantic gate is routinely invoked, so the *application* of the revised criterion has moved.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Routine invocation establishes application of whatever the gate actually encodes, not of the revised criterion as a whole; the next sentence says the gate omits observed fit and that no retained review shows dependence on all four revised parts.
2. **“Once codified, its application needs no maintainer.”** — UNSUPPORTED — Codification makes a rule machine-executable, but it does not by itself provide inputs, invocation, admission, or an operative consumer; the article's own six requirements make those additional dependencies load-bearing.
3. **“What has moved is the work around that decision: noticing, drafting, checking and — for codified rules — application.”** — UNSUPPORTED — The listed revision cases show that changes occurred across several artifact classes, but they do not establish who or what performed each of noticing, drafting, and checking, so breadth of changed artifacts does not establish this allocation of work.
4. **“A revision decision the machinery performs as part of a governed, inspectable, repeatable path needs six things represented.”** — THIN — The table explains what the absence of each proposed requirement can leave with a maintainer, but it does not establish that the six are an exhaustive account; the later applicability gap is not explicitly placed within them.
5. **“Its incumbent formalization must prove that switching is better than continuing ... so every revision decision has moved.”** — THIN — The proof condition supports machine warrant and switching for changes expressible inside the supplied formal system, but it does not establish that choices of boundary, objective, formalization, or admissible proof system have also moved.

## For the human
Look first at the claim that application of the revised explanatory-reach criterion has moved, because the article's own next sentence says the operative gate omits one of that criterion's requirements.
