# Friction check: A better-factory claim compares operative states under an antecedent assessment relation

**Note:** `kb/notes/a-better-factory-claim-compares-operative-states.md`
**Central claim (one sentence):** A determinate better-factory claim compares a predecessor factory state with an operative successor under an antecedent, scope-indexed assessment relation, while successor route and evaluator location are declared separately from the learner boundary.

## Filter
**Verdict:** SURVIVES
The claim's required properties can coexist: predecessor and operative-successor endpoints can be fixed separately from the assessment relation; an antecedent, scope-indexed relation can make the pairwise comparison capable of failing; and evaluator location can be declared apart from whose causal contributions count as learning. The hard cases are applicability and attribution, not an internal contradiction among the commitments.

## Signal — thinnest joints
1. **"Both components are declared while only the predecessor exists. Antecedence is the pairwise form of the condition that makes an improvement objective a declared parameter rather than a reading taken off the result."** — THIN — The timing requirement blocks post-hoc dimension selection, but the reason does not fully establish that every legitimate assessment relation must be fixed before any candidate or successor-relevant evidence exists. The note needs the missing rule that separates antecedent specification from later calibration.
2. **"Without the index, the requirement is either unmeetable or vacuous, depending on how generously the reader reads 'no worse'."** — THIN — Scope indexing clearly helps, but the stated dilemma is too strong. A comparison could also make "no worse" determinate by declaring a bounded task family, operating envelope, or risk class rather than by indexing only to the predecessor's prior scope.
3. **"It is already operative when measured, so the evaluation judges a realized trajectory rather than performing adoption."** — THIN — Being operative when measured supports the measurement/admission distinction, but it does not by itself specify which continuous-process state is the successor or why that boundary is the right endpoint. The note needs more concretization for cases where installation and measurement are not cleanly separated.
4. **"Judgment that controls installation does, because an admission decision determines what the factory retains."** — THIN — The causal point supports treating admission as part of the update path, but it does not settle whether an external evaluator thereby falls inside the learner boundary. That depends on the boundary rule for external gating, which the note leaves as an open question.
5. **"Because an experiment identifies only the contrast it actually runs, that link is established by supplying versus withholding the predecessor's retained state."** — THIN — Supplying versus withholding the retained state is the right kind of attribution contrast, but the inference overstates what it establishes unless other successor-producing inputs are controlled. The intervention identifies the experience link only under a cleaner experimental design than the sentence states.

## For the human
Look first at the evaluator-location and successor-boundary joints: the note's distinctions hold, but those two places need the missing rule that makes the declarations operational rather than merely named.
