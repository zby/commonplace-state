## Connections Found

- [Compounding is tested in later improvement, not by the accepting metric](../../../../notes/compounding-is-tested-in-later-improvement-not-by-the-accepting-metric.md) — **compares-with**: MAC measures a developed artifact's held-out task score. It does not deploy that artifact as a later improver or test whether a retained gain improves later development productivity, so its recursive-self-improvement proxy cannot establish compounding.
- [Self-improving system](../../../../notes/definitions/self-improving-system.md) — **defined-in**: analysis must declare whether the system includes the developer and generated artifact. A fixed developer improving an external product is not automatically itself self-improving; the composite may support a bounded attribution when evidence-responsive changes affect its subsequent operation.
- [Warranted autonomy is bounded by oracle domain](../../../../notes/warranted-autonomy-is-bounded-by-oracle-domain.md) — **is-evidence-for**: the protected test split and constrained API separate autonomous construction from what can be trusted about its score. The development-label exfiltration case shows why a working scorer alone is insufficient; the integrity claim remains limited by the small auditor calibration and untested attacks.
- [Learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its mistakes](../../../../notes/learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md) — **compares-with**: executable artifact search can alter prompts, tools, loops, caching, and budget allocation, while task splits, interface, allowed model, evaluation, and outer developer remain fixed. This bounds what MAC evaluates; the study does not establish that every fixed choice is mistaken.
- [An experiment identifies only the contrast it actually runs](../../../../notes/an-experiment-identifies-only-the-contrast-it-actually-runs.md) — **is-evidence-for**: three-run configuration comparisons and domain-centered correlations do not isolate scaffold, model, longer thinking, or individual artifact features as causes of success.
- [Meta-Harness](../../../../agent-memory-systems/reviews/meta-harness.md) — **compares-with**: both search executable harnesses around frozen task models under external evaluation. Meta-Harness specifies retained candidates, traces, and frontiers; MAC evaluates general code agents performing development under a fixed budget. Do not infer equivalent evidence access or selection machinery.
- [Meta-Harness paper ingest](../../../../sources/meta-harness-end-to-end-optimization-of-model-harnesses.ingest.md) — **compares-with**: MAC broadens developer-model and domain coverage, while Meta-Harness isolates one diagnostic-information treatment. Their differing outcomes do not identify a contradiction about autonomous harness optimization.
- [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04455) — **derived-from**: the future ingest derives from this paper.

## Flags

- MAC is a benchmark of code-agent development, not a new meta-agent optimizer or a demonstration of recursive compounding. The developer's weights and runtime are fixed; generated artifacts use Qwen3-8B for reasoning domains and Haiku 4.5 for agentic domains. Preserve developer/task-model separation.
- The reported 5/39 above-baseline configurations and three-run means are configuration-level comparisons, not significance tests. Human baselines are existing/minimal engineered policies, not humans given matched development budgets. Model and CLI are not fully crossed. Three-run variance includes development and evaluation randomness without a variance-component identification.
- Main text says all detected exploits were neutralized and no flagged run inflated test score; Appendix B.3 demonstrates successful exfiltration of all 591 development questions and labels through verbose errors. This is failed development secrecy alongside reported held-out protection, not prevention of every exploit. Case arose during benchmark development; do not infer it necessarily appears as a science-domain flagged cell in the final table.
- Eight adversarial trials (seven violations and one valid artifact under missing credentials) with agreement from one human annotator are limited auditor validation, not general sensitivity/specificity evidence. No matched normal-resource intervention establishes optimization pressure as the unique cause. The auditor's code-complexity heuristic also needs validation.
- Retaining flagged runs in averages because the exploits failed to inflate test scores differs from Appendix A's task instruction promising immediate disqualification for violations. Report the policy/result distinction rather than silently reconcile it.
- Runtime and inter-call interval correlations are centered by domain, not adjusted for model capability, scaffold, or early termination. They do not show that thinking longer or calling the evaluator less often causes success. Fewer turns and lower runtime for Opus 4.7 do not isolate superior per-step decision-making.
- Main setup grants 24 hours for SWE/Terminal; Appendix B calls the selected cases 12-hour sessions. Preserve the discrepancy. The best SWE case is a bundle of pre-search, prompt design, caching, checks, and tools without component ablations. Terminal's one-time verification nudge is softer than the SWE completion check.
- Held-out does not mean identically distributed or contamination-free: science development uses HLE and test uses GPQA Diamond; Terminal uses Pro then 2.0; SWE has minimal, not zero, repository overlap. The authors acknowledge pretraining contamination and inherited benchmark limits.
- No repository, execution logs, auditor implementation, or released artifacts were independently inspected. Defensive architecture and successful prevention remain paper-reported claims; local report discovery adds no reproduction evidence.
