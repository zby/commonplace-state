Source claim (single-thread X post by @shivsakhuja, practitioner reflection):

1. Naive "skill graph" (skills linking to dependent skills, Obsidian-style) degrades: agents fail to reliably traverse deep chains, and dense graphs behave non-deterministically. Circular dependencies are also problematic.
2. The proposed reframe is a three-level compositional hierarchy — **atoms** (narrow deterministic primitives, don't call other skills), **molecules** (2–10 atoms chained by a structured workflow or a small-set orchestrator, agent given bounded judgement), **compounds** (higher-level orchestrators over molecules; "run outbound sales playbook"; human-driven, less deterministic).
3. Leverage argument: each level is an order of magnitude of leverage; humans are bottlenecked on "brain RAM" / context-switching across parallel agents, so driving compounds multiplies output relative to driving atoms.
4. Reliability ceiling hypothesis: compounds start breaking past ~8–10 molecules.
5. Author names their three tiers `capabilities` / `composites` / `playbooks`.
6. Open problem flagged: testing skills at every level is expensive; speculation that auto-research can help.

## Synthesis Opportunities

1. **"Composition depth degrades by the same mechanism as flat-context dynamic scope."** The tweet observes that dense skill graphs behave non-deterministically. `llm-context-is-composed-without-scoping` explains why: each skill executes in the parent's flat context, so deeper chains inherit more accumulated state, producing exactly the "spooky action at a distance" the note describes for in-skill reasoning. A synthesis note could argue that the tweet's molecule/compound discipline is *functionally equivalent* to enforcing sub-agent frames at composition boundaries — even when the practitioner frames it as "keep the graph shallow." This is a stronger mechanism than the tweet offers and would connect the tweet into the scoping cluster.
2. **"Hierarchical composition and hierarchical context scoping are the same move at different layers."** Combining the tweet (compose skills in atom/molecule/compound tiers), `topology-isolation-and-verification-form-a-causal-chain-for-reliable-agent-scaling` (topology → isolation → verification), `subtasks-that-need-different-tools-force-loop-exposure-in-agent-frameworks` (sub-agents as the clean-composition primitive), and `decomposition-heuristics-for-bounded-context-scheduling` (exploit clean frames recursively) suggests: molecules and compounds need separate execution frames, not just separate files — the tweet's reliability ceiling at ~8–10 molecules-per-compound is evidence that flat-context compounds hit the same depth-N collapse ConvexBench reports.
3. **"Skill-tier testing cost is the verification-step cost of the topology→isolation→verification chain."** The tweet's hand-waving at "autoresearch will solve testing" plus Nurijanian's concrete failure modes plus Tu's theoretical argument together suggest a note on *where* in the composition hierarchy verification cost concentrates. Likely answer: verification cost at atoms is hard-oracle (cheap automation); at molecules it is the interaction profile (harder); at compounds it is goal specification (hardest, phase-gated by comprehension per Nurijanian).

These are candidates for a future write pass, not to be written here.
