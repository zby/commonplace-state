## Flags

Four-task easy gate, fixed 50-task search evaluation, top-ten incumbent comparisons and combined judge/accuracy/visit ranks define the tested loop. Pending children inherit parent accuracy temporarily; the judge sees code, not tasks or outcomes. The full-file rendering omits identical files and truncates files at 30,000 characters, so it is not unlimited whole-program inspection. Polyglot's final 225 tasks include the 50-task search subset; distinguish full-benchmark evaluation from a disjoint held-out set. SWE-60 repeats use the same 60-task set, and its reported means include the search score plus three repeats. TerminalBench uses one search per condition; repeating selected agents is not repeating independent searches. Its best judged candidate repeats at 36.7% versus 29.2% baseline/no-judge selection, while the highest search-accuracy judged candidate repeats at 28.1%. Selection by judge and selection by accuracy must remain separate; Algorithm 1 returns highest measured accuracy.

The o3-mini narrative describes 30 expansions, 42 CPU-hours and about $150, whereas Table 3 reports a 20-expansion run, 59 CPU-hours and $86.8; do not combine them as one run. Other baseline comparisons change models and resource protocols. Figure 6 reports most speedup from disaggregation, with judging adding speculative guidance. Theoretical top-K recovery assumes non-adaptive random comparisons and latent judge utilities, unlike SIFT's adaptive frontier graph; no-starvation is for a fixed finite archive, not its growing tree. Restricted edits protect the evaluator, but inspection of source code and execution were not performed for this paper ingest. Judge conservatism misorders the two selected SWE-60 descendants, and a single scalar can hide task-specific tradeoffs. Example prompt/test-runner improvements are retained code changes; neither their presence nor a lineage's gain isolates every proposed diagnosis.
