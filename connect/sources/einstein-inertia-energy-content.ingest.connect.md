## Flags

The source is John Walker's edition of the 1923 English translation of the September 27, 1905 paper. The edition notice and its modern E = mc² reformulation are editorial context. The source uses L for emitted energy and compares before/after mass. Extracted equation layout is imperfect; no formal proof check or experiment was executed.

A body emits equal radiation energies in opposite directions so it remains at rest in one frame. The inherited light-energy transformation and conservation in each frame imply a difference between pre- and post-emission kinetic energies. Identifying frame-energy differences with kinetic energy up to an unchanged additive constant, then taking the low-velocity expansion, yields a mass loss L/c². The author generalizes from radiative loss to arbitrary change of energy content and proposes high-energy-content materials such as radium salts as a possible test. No such test is performed or reported here. A modern full relativistic mass-energy treatment or the later experimental history should not be inserted as if supplied by this brief source.
