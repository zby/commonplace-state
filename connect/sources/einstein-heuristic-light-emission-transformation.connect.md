## Flags

This is the March 1905 paper in an English translation identified as American Journal of Physics, May 1965. It is a theoretical argument using existing observations and proposing tests, not a new experimental report. Extracted notation contains damaged symbols and layout; preserve meaning without silently treating reconstructed equations as exact retained quotes.

The opening preserves wave theory's successful optical predictions while questioning continuous-energy descriptions of emission and transformation. Classical oscillator equilibrium gives a radiation density growing with squared frequency and an unbounded integrated energy. Its long-wavelength limit nevertheless agrees with Planck's formula and supports an estimate of molecular number. The subsequent entropy argument deliberately starts from Wien's empirical law, valid at large frequency-to-temperature ratio, rather than claiming a general derivation across all radiation densities.

At fixed energy and frequency in that regime, the entropy's logarithmic dependence on volume matches independent localized particles. Einstein interprets this as behavior as if radiation consists of independent quanta with energy proportional to frequency, then tests its explanatory potential in other phenomena. Boltzmann's entropy/probability relation uses independent systems and an ideal dilute-particle comparison. These named assumptions bound the argument.

Stokes's rule follows from energy conservation plus low-density independent conversion, with possible exceptions explicitly discussed. Photoemission additionally assumes that at least some single electrons receive a whole quantum and perform material-specific escape work. It predicts a linear stopping-potential/frequency relation with material-independent slope and separates electron energy from incident intensity. The paper reports order-of-magnitude agreement with Lenard, not a completed modern verification of the full relation. Dropping complete energy transfer weakens equality to inequality. Gas-ionization energy bounds are compared with existing estimates, and proportional ionization yield under specified absorption conditions is proposed for testing. Do not import later confirmation or modern universal formulations as observations in this source. No software system or source code is presented.
