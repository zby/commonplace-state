# Connection Report: Software Design Model Use in Practice

**Source:** [Software Design Model Use in Practice](../../../../sources/.snapshots/software-design-model-use-in-practice.md)

## Discovery Trace

**Description and title scans:**

- Searched notes and sources for software factories, MDE, design models, adoption, diffusion, reuse, family scope, scale, and tool support. The closest results were the versioned factory ontology, breadth-pressure and universality notes, the Greenfield program sources, the 2008 evidence review, the 2014 MDE-practitioner study, and the Santos retrospective.
- Read the full snapshot and the closest notes and ingests. The study's general-developer sampling makes it a diffusion counterweight to studies that recruited only modelers or MDE practitioners.

**Link following:**

- Followed the family-scoped ontology to the 2003 and 2007 program accounts. Their proposed machinery assumes maintained formal models and reusable assets; this survey directly measures how often the weaker prerequisite of ordinary design-model use occurred.
- Followed the broad-demand note to the universality note. The survey bears on adoption of supplied model-driven machinery, not on whether agentic factory development can solve the breadth problem.

## Connections Found

- [A software factory is family-scoped lifecycle production machinery](../../../../notes/a-software-factory-is-family-scoped-lifecycle-production-machinery.md) -- **compares-with**: the Greenfield ontology includes model-driven development as one constituent and requires maintained family machinery; the survey finds that even ordinary design models were seldom used or maintained across a broad developer sample.
- [Universal software factory needs a declared universality axis](../../../../notes/universal-software-factory-needs-a-declared-universality-axis.md) -- **is-evidence-for**: the study separates isolated companies that fully apply MDE from population-level diffusion, illustrating why success inside one scope does not establish broad production reach.
- [Mass Customizing Solutions with Software Development Factories](../../../../sources/greenfield-mass-customizing-software-factories-2007.ingest.md) -- **compares-with**: Greenfield's economic program depends partly on model-driven automation and reusable maintained assets; the survey finds low use of the more basic modeling practice and explicitly says the prerequisite for MDE diffusion was unmet.
- [Where Is the Proof? — A Review of Experiences from Applying MDE in Industry](../../../../sources/where-is-the-proof-mde-industry.ingest.md) -- **extends**: the 2008 review found too little industrial evidence to generalize; the 2014 survey adds direct, broad evidence that basic modeling had not become standard practice.
- [The State of Practice in Model-Driven Engineering](../../../../sources/state-of-practice-in-model-driven-engineering.ingest.md) -- **corrects**: that study's MDE-practitioner sample can characterize adopters but cannot estimate prevalence; this developer-population survey directly asks how often modeling is used and finds low diffusion.

## Synthesis Opportunities

- The 2008 review and this 2014 survey support a stronger, carefully scoped historical statement: reported industrial benefits did not generalize from a robust evidence base, and basic modeling did not become standard developer practice. Microsoft's retirement records can separately establish product or program retreat.

## Flags

- The survey used one modeling question in a broader voluntary online survey of object-oriented developers. Its large, non-modeler-targeted sample is strong diffusion evidence, but it does not identify why developers did not adopt MDE and does not directly measure Greenfield-style software factories.
