## Synthesis Opportunities

Three notes independently gesture at the same unwritten claim: `only-explicit-retention...` names interpretability-grade editing as its falsifier, `reflection-buys-addressability` asks in an Open Question whether interpretability tooling can make parametric retention addressable *to the improving system itself*, and `reflection-makes-retained-lessons-second-order` separates content-addressability (enables rejection and revision) from scope-addressability (enables rescoping). ROME supplies the case that resolves them jointly, and the resolution is more specific than any of the three states: **parametric editing delivers content-addressability without scope-addressability**. A ROME-edited fact can be revised or replaced, but the edit carries no applicability boundary the system could later narrow, so the second-order operation of rescoping stays unavailable even where modification succeeds. That distinction would refine, not overturn, the explicit-only retention claim — it converts a binary falsifier into a two-property test. Do not write this during connect; it needs the ingest first, and it should be checked against what ROME's specificity/generalization metrics actually measure (the snapshot's abstract-level summary is not enough to settle it).
