## Connections Found

- [Representational form](../../../../notes/definitions/representational-form.md) — **compares-with**: Smolensky distinguishes levels of cognitive description and two forms of knowledge resident in connections; Commonplace distinguishes artifact encoding and consumption. This prevents equating his symbolic/subsymbolic paradigms with the KB's natural-language/symbolic/distributed-parametric categories.
- [Code complements the weight–prompt pair with independently executed symbolic operations](../../../../notes/code-complements-weight-prompt-with-symbolic-operations.md) — **compares-with**: section 6 contrasts exact implementation of rule interpretation in a conventional computer with a proposed subsymbolic implementation, while section 9 distinguishes rule-described competence from the computation producing it. The shared axis is execution of stated rules versus behavior describable by rules; the paper does not evaluate modern runtime separation.
- [Treat continual learning as representational-form coevolution](../../../../notes/treat-continual-learning-as-representational-form-coevolution.md) — **compares-with**: section 6 proposes that task experience generated by sequential rule use can train direct parallel performance. This is a historical proposed coupling between rule-guided action and weight adaptation, not an experiment establishing today's three-form coevolution.
- [Natural-language meaning is coordination among learned approximations, not approximation of a target](../../../../notes/natural-language-meaning-is-coordination-among-learned-approximations.md) — **compares-with**: sections 2 and 6 separate publicly formulated cultural rules from individual intuitive processing; section 7 situates representation in environmental goal satisfaction. These distinctions constrain an extension from learned formal-rule competence to a population account of natural-language meaning, which Smolensky does not establish.
- [Symbols, neural networks, and mathematical intelligence](../../../../sources/symbols-neural-networks-mathematical-intelligence.ingest.md) — **compares-with**: Lampinen's account of learned, fallible symbol manipulation can be compared with the earlier explicit proposal for a subsymbolic rule interpreter and its contrast with intuitive processing. Shared lineage is not independent evidence for modern mathematical-performance claims.
