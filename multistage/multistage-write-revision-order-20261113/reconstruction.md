# Source reconstruction: revision choices before dependent polish

## Commission and evidence boundary

The commissioned contribution is a bounded argument for agents and maintainers revising KB documents: address an unresolved choice before investing in polish that its resolution could discard, subject to evidence needs and the costs of waiting. It is not a universal revision schedule or a demonstrated improvement in agent performance.

## 1. What Purdue supports

Authorized source: `kb/sources/purdue-higher-order-lower-order-concerns.ingest.md`, complete `## Quotes`; snapshot: `kb/sources/.snapshots/purdue-higher-order-lower-order-concerns.md`.

Purdue supplies institutional writing advice. It recommends unequal revision priority: address thesis or focus, audience and purpose, organization, and development before sentence structure and grammar. It calls the former higher-order concerns and the latter lower-order concerns. Both that recommendation and its exceptions are retained in the Quotes section.

The exceptions are material, not optional qualifications. Purdue permits movement between the two classes during revision. It also gives a case for addressing local errors first: punctuation and spelling errors can prevent a reader from reaching the thesis. The source therefore does not support an uninterrupted higher-order-first pass or a prohibition on early sentence-level work.

The snapshot expands the examples. Organization includes the sequence of paragraph topics, outlines, moving parts, and checking whether readers can anticipate the paper's direction. Development includes missing detail, examples, explanation, and support. These examples help identify decisions that can change what prose remains useful. They do not state the commissioned dependency/rework mechanism. Those detailed examples are snapshot evidence, not additional retained Quotes support.

Source limit: the page presents advice and revision questions, not a comparative experiment, measured cost reduction, or a finding about agents. Its classification also cannot be equated with decision dependency: some local work matters immediately, while some broad choices need evidence before commitment.

## 2. What the adjacent internal notes supply

### Option preservation is a different ordering mechanism

`kb/theory/solve-low-degree-of-freedom-subproblems-first-to-avoid-blocking.md` orders choices by the number of viable options. A flexible choice made early can consume the few usable alternatives of another choice. Committing the less flexible choice first can preserve feasibility for the remaining choices.

Its Scope section explicitly separates this from discarded revision work. A claim may admit many revisions while much prose depends on it. A metadata requirement may admit one valid value while discarding no prose. These are reasoning examples in the note, not observations of measured outcomes.

Represent this as a distinction between two questions:

- Which commitment could remove another choice's viable options?
- Which unresolved choice could make completed prose work unusable?

The first note supplies the first mechanism and explicitly declines to establish an ordering that best avoids discarded revision work. It does not warrant treating all governing prose choices as low-option choices. Nor does the new mechanism refute its option-preservation argument.

### Evidence-producing work can precede commitment

`kb/theory/productive-deferral-requires-option-evidence-and-convergence.md` requires three things for controlled postponement: an option that remains exercisable, possible evidence that can change the decision, and a rule joining a return condition to a response. Its conditions are necessary, not sufficient to show that waiting is worth its cost.

Evidence can arrive later or be produced by bounded work. Work counts as evidence-producing for a particular decision only if some possible output could change selection, timing, modification, or abandonment. Maintaining alternatives and waiting both carry costs. The note supplies no portable numerical threshold for comparing them with immediate commitment, and no test of the combined rule in LLM-agent systems.

Application to revision is commissioned inference: drafting a small example, testing a paragraph, or obtaining a reader's interpretation may help decide a claim or organization. Such activity is not ruled out merely because a governing choice remains open. Distinguish a bounded probe from polish whose usefulness presumes that choice is settled. These activities can look similar; the distinction depends on what possible result could change the decision, not on whether the activity edits a sentence.

### Timing does not allocate authority

`kb/theory/fix-what-the-executor-cant-determine-not-what-it-will.md` separates who decides, when judgment occurs, and which evidence supports it. A different actor can decide immediately; the same actor can decide later. Later timing helps only when an observation has a possible result that changes the choice. Competence and authority remain decision-specific requirements.

For the commissioned note, early identification or checking of a governing choice does not require deciding it immediately or moving its ownership upstream. If evidence needed for a safe choice will be produced during revision, the authorized chooser can retain the judgment until that evidence exists. Conversely, calling a choice urgent does not authorize a reviser to settle it. Where acceptance conditions or permissions are missing, a timing heuristic cannot repair that gap.

## 3. Commissioned dependency/rework inference

The following is the commissioned argument, not Purdue's stated explanation or an empirically tested result:

1. Some prose work is useful only if a particular claim, audience, scope, or organization survives revision.
2. An unresolved choice can require replacing or deleting that prose, or changing the relationships that made its polish useful.
3. Polishing it first adds work exposed to that change. Resolving the choice first can avoid that particular exposure when the polish would otherwise be discarded.
4. This gives a reason to address the choice before substantial dependent polish. It does not establish that immediate commitment has the lowest total cost.

The mechanism concerns the dependence of work's usefulness on a choice. It does not require that the choice have few alternatives, that every governing choice be settled, or that every later revision invalidate every sentence. If the text survives unchanged or the polish transfers, that work is not lost through this mechanism.

A hypothetical illustration, not evidence: polishing transitions between two sections is exposed to rework if their order is still under decision. A short trial transition may instead reveal that the proposed order does not support the argument. The first is dependent investment; the second may be a probe. Structural organization concerns the order and relation of sections or paragraphs; a local transition expresses a connection at a particular boundary. They can interact without being the same task.

Another hypothetical illustration: correcting an independent broken link need not wait for a claim revision that cannot affect it. Independence must be assessed against the actual contemplated changes; it is not guaranteed by the repair's small size.

## 4. Boundaries the candidate must preserve

- **Early checking versus premature commitment:** identify consequential open choices and their dependent work early. Do not manufacture a settled answer before the necessary evidence or authority exists.
- **Diagnostic work versus premature polish:** permit bounded local work that could alter the decision. Name the question it helps decide; activity alone does not establish an evidence route.
- **Independent work versus a global freeze:** work whose usefulness does not depend on the open choice need not wait. The rule concerns affected work, not every task in the document.
- **Cost comparison:** avoid treating potential rework as the only cost. Waiting, probing, carrying alternatives, and delayed delivery can outweigh avoided polish. Sometimes proceeding provisionally or committing on current evidence is warranted.
- **Purdue's exception:** local readability can be a prerequisite for obtaining useful reader feedback or reaching the central argument. The reader-access case is directly in Purdue; its use as decision evidence is an application, not a quoted source claim.
- **Authority versus timing:** the need to settle a choice before dependent investment does not determine which actor may settle it.
- **Structural versus local work:** do not translate “organization before grammar” into “all broad work before all small work.” Dependency and evidence needs cut across that division.

Purdue's higher-order-first recommendation and its local-first exception must remain visible together. There is no need to erase either to motivate the narrower inference. Similarly, the internal option-ordering rule can recommend one commitment while evidence needs recommend postponing it. The authorized evidence supplies no universal rule for resolving every such conflict.

## 5. Scope, uncertainty, and governing gaps

The warranted product is a reasoned revision heuristic for KB documents. The dependence mechanism explains a possible source of wasted polish; it does not demonstrate net savings, improved writing quality, or agent effectiveness. Broader software or design applications remain conjectural and are unnecessary to establish the commissioned KB contribution.

No source provides a measured threshold for “substantial” polish, an estimator of invalidation likelihood, a method for reliably discovering all prose dependencies, or a numerical comparison of rework and delay costs. These are genuine operational gaps. Do not hide them behind an unqualified instruction to resolve all choices first or introduce invented scores.

No governing evidence gap blocks the bounded conceptual argument. A stronger claim that this order generally improves outcomes would require new evidence and a return to reconstruction. A claim that one order is always optimal is unsupported by both the available advice and the explicit cost boundaries of the internal notes.

No new registered definition is necessary. Ordinary working meanings suffice: dependent polish is text improvement whose usefulness can be undone by the named open choice; resolving a choice is selecting an answer within the chooser's authority, not proving it permanently immune to later revision. These descriptions delimit this argument rather than propose adopted vocabulary.
