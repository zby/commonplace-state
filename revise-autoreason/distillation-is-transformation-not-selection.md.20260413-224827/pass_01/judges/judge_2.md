# Judge 2 evaluation

## Candidate 1

**Strengths.** Retains the original's "richest concrete case in hand" qualifier. The rewritten opening of "The substrate must persist" — "If distillates are transformations rather than subsets, they don't subsume their source" — makes the logical bridge from the note's central claim to the persistence argument explicit rather than implied. Merging the two "Implication" paragraphs into one creates a smoother arc from "memory primitives are different kinds of objects" to "traces → memory primitives is a family of transformations." The post-table paragraph is crisper.

**Weaknesses.** The original's post-table paragraph contained a specific dual-move observation — "A trace-to-preference distillation discards most of the trace *and* introduces structure that wasn't there" — that grounded the abstract claim in a worked example. This is lost. The phrase "different representations at different levels of abstraction" is also dropped; it added a useful categorization. "Misses the shape-change that did the work" is punchier than the original's "misses what happened" but editorializes slightly about what "did the work."

## Candidate 2

**Strengths.** Perfect semantic fidelity — no claims changed, no caveats dropped, no structure altered. The two-paragraph structure in the "Implication" section separates two distinct observations (what memory primitives are vs. the family-of-transformations reframe) which aids scanability. The post-table paragraph preserves the dual-move observation and the "different representations at different levels of abstraction" framing that candidates 1 and 3 lose.

**Weaknesses.** The "Substrate" section opens with "The inventory of shapes evolves" without connecting back to the transformation-not-selection claim — the reader must infer why trace retention follows from the note's thesis. This is the gap that candidates 1 and 3 fill. The two separate paragraphs in "Implication" slightly fragment what is really one continuous argument.

## Candidate 3

**Strengths.** Same structural improvements as candidate 1: better Substrate opening, cleaner Implication flow, crisper post-table paragraph.

**Weaknesses.** Same semantic losses as candidate 1 (dual-move observation, "different levels of abstraction" framing). Additionally drops "richest" from "the richest concrete case in hand," reducing it to "the concrete case in hand." This is a gratuitous loss — "richest" was a meaningful qualifier distinguishing memory distillation as the most fertile example domain, not merely one example among equals.

## Summary

Candidates 1 and 3 make the same set of purposeful editorial improvements over the original: a clearer logical bridge in the Substrate section and a more cohesive Implication section. These are genuine flow gains, not change for its own sake. Both pay a semantic cost — losing the dual-move observation and one useful categorization phrase. Candidate 1 retains "richest"; candidate 3 drops it without cause. Candidate 2 preserves everything but misses the editorial improvements that 1 and 3 introduce.

RANKING: 1, 2, 3
