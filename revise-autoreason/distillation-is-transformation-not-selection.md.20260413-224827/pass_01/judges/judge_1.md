# Judge 1 evaluation

## Candidate 1

**Strengths:** Body text is nearly identical to the original. The post-table paragraph ("None of these artifacts is 'part of' the trace...") is preserved verbatim, retaining its specificity — the observation that distillation "discards most of the trace *and* introduces structure" is an important dual claim. The two-paragraph structure in the memory-design section is kept, preserving the rhetorical emphasis on "single arrow is too coarse" as a standalone point. The substrate section is unchanged.

**Weaknesses:** Description expanded significantly beyond the original (added "reframing memory primitives as an inventory of shapes rather than denser traces"). Status changed from `current` to `seedling` — an unauthorized frontmatter regression.

## Candidate 2

**Strengths:** The merged memory-design paragraph flows well and the added opening sentence in the substrate section ("If distillates are transformations rather than subsets, they don't subsume their source") makes the logical bridge from the core claim more explicit.

**Weaknesses:** Drops "richest" from "the richest concrete case in hand" — a minor semantic loss (the original emphasized memory distillation's primacy as a case study). The post-table paragraph is rewritten, losing two specific formulations: "None of these artifacts is 'part of' the trace" and "different representations at different levels of abstraction, sometimes in a different medium." The replacement ("Each artifact introduces structure absent from the trace") is more generic and collapses the dual observation (discards + introduces) into one side. The substrate section's new opening sentence, while logically sound, is invented content not present in the original. Same unauthorized description expansion and status downgrade as Candidate 1.

## Candidate 3

**Strengths:** Retains "richest concrete case in hand" (unlike Candidate 2). Same cohesion improvement in the substrate section opening.

**Weaknesses:** Otherwise identical to Candidate 2 — same post-table rewrite losing specificity, same paragraph merge in the memory section, same invented opening sentence in the substrate section. Same unauthorized frontmatter changes.

## Summary

Candidate 1 makes the fewest changes to an already well-written note, preserving specific formulations that carry semantic weight. Candidates 2 and 3 are near-identical revisions that improve cohesion in the substrate section but at the cost of lost specificity in the post-table analysis and an invented framing sentence. Between 2 and 3, Candidate 3 preserves "richest" while Candidate 2 drops it.

RANKING: 1, 3, 2
