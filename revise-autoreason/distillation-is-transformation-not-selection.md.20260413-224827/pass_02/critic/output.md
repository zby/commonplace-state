# Critique — pass 02

## 1. Triple statement of the core claim before evidence arrives

The reader encounters the central claim three times before seeing any evidence: the title ("transformation, not selection"), the opening paragraph ("produce an artifact of a different kind"), and the first section heading ("Distilled artifacts are not subsets of the trace"). By the time the table arrives, the claim feels belabored rather than set up. One of these three could carry the load the other two currently duplicate.

## 2. Scope disclaimer interrupts the opening momentum

The second paragraph ("The examples below are all memory distillation — the richest concrete case in hand — but the claim applies to distillation generally") is a defensive aside wedged between the claim and its evidence. It stalls the reader just when they want to see the examples. The information it carries (scope = memory distillation as a concrete case) could be folded into the opening paragraph without breaking stride.

## 3. Table column header "What's new" is ambiguous

"What's new" could be read as "what has this revision added" or "what does the distilled artifact introduce that the trace lacked." The latter is intended but the header doesn't distinguish the two readings. A more precise header would reduce the interpretation load.

## 4. Post-table paragraph partially recapitulates the table

The paragraph beginning "None of these artifacts is 'part of' the trace" re-explains the lesson the table already demonstrated, then layers on new material (universal quantification, condition clause, rationale, codification as extreme case). The recapitulation of the table's point dilutes the new material. The paragraph could lead with the new observations — the structural additions distillation introduces — without first restating what the table showed.

## 5. Second and third paragraphs of "Implication" section overlap

The second paragraph concludes that "designing a memory system is largely designing the inventory of shapes it supports." The third paragraph then re-enters the same point by recasting "traces → memory primitives" as "a family of transformations." The progression from "different kinds of objects" to "a family of transformations" is a restatement in different vocabulary, not an advance. The reader may feel the argument is circling rather than building.

## 6. Abrupt transition into "The substrate must persist"

The "Implication" section ends on the shape-inventory idea. The next section opens with "The inventory of shapes evolves" — a new premise introduced without connection to what preceded it. Why the shape-change framing leads to caring about trace retention is left implicit. The reader has to bridge from "distillation is shape-change" to "therefore keep the raw traces" without help.

## 7. Third bullet in the final section drifts from the section's claim

The section heading promises an argument about substrate persistence. The first two bullets deliver on that (traces are irreplaceable; bias toward retention). The third bullet ("Distilled artifacts are not permanent products. They are current-best representations...") is a restatement of the note's main transformation claim rather than a consequence of trace persistence. It reads as a coda for the whole note placed inside a section about something narrower.
